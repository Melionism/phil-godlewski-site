All right, guys, here's BlackRock Aladdin explained simply, with one made-up portfolio. Think of Aladdin as a giant dashboard. It reads what a fund owns, measures what could go wrong, checks the rules and helps place trades. BlackRock's 10-K lists those jobs: "risk analytics, portfolio management, trade execution, and investment operations" [1]. What it doesn't do is decide for the manager.

This is Aladdin for beginners. Let's walk one portfolio through it.

**Quick answer**

- Aladdin simple explanation: a dashboard for risk, rules and trading [1].
- What it reads: holdings, prices, market data [2].
- What a manager sees: risk numbers, stress results, rule warnings [3].
- What the manager still decides: what to buy and sell.

## A sample portfolio

Here's our Aladdin portfolio example, a small pension fund (numbers are made up for illustration):

| Holding | Share of portfolio |
| --- | --- |
| US stocks | 40% |
| Government bonds | 35% |
| Corporate bonds | 15% |
| Interest-rate swaps | 10% |

The fund also owes pensions to retirees. That's its liability. See [the mechanics of the platform](/blog/blackrock-aladdin-work/).

## What does the system read?

Every position, its price, its terms, and market data like rates and credit spreads. It puts all of it in "a common data language," as BlackRock puts it [2]. That's the step that lets stocks, bonds and swaps sit on one screen.

## What does a manager see?

Numbers like these (the kind of metrics Aladdin offers include duration, convexity, value-at-risk and credit risk measures [3]):

1. **Value-at-risk:** how much the fund could lose on a bad day.
2. **Duration:** how much the bonds move if rates change.
3. **Stress test:** what happens in a scenario like a 2008-style crash.
4. **Rule check:** whether any limit, like "no more than 20% in one sector," is close to breaking.

The Bank of Israel used scenario tools like these to study Russia–Ukraine risk [3].

## What does the manager still decide?

Everything that matters. I need you to hear this one: Aladdin shows the risk. It doesn't pick the trade. A manager might see the duration is too high and decide to sell some long bonds, or might decide to keep them. That choice is human. For [the words behind those seven letters](/blog/aladdin-stand-blackrock/), see the name page, and for [how it all started](/blog/history-blackrock-aladdin/), the history page.
