All right, guys, here's the most serious attempt so far to answer "who wrote Q," and it didn't use rumors. It used writing style. Stylometry measures habits in how people write, like word choices and letter patterns, and compares them across texts. In February 2022 the New York Times reported on two independent teams [1]. The Swiss startup OrphAnalytics, led by Claude-Alain Roten and Lionel Pousaz, broke texts into three-character sequences and reported about 93% accuracy; it found Q's style changed over time [1]. French computational linguists Florian Cafiero and Jean-Baptiste Camps trained software that correctly identified Ron Watkins' writing in 99% of tests and Paul Furber's in 98% [1]. Both teams found the same pattern: Furber's style dominated early posts, and Watkins' signature grew as Furber's faded, around the start of 2018, when the posts moved to 8chan, owned by Jim Watkins [1]. Ron Watkins said: "I am not Q." Furber denies it too and argued Q's posts influenced his own writing [1]. Stylometry gives probabilities, not confessions, and it compares only the candidates you feed it.

Here's how stylometry works, Paul Furber, the Watkins family, the two studies, and limits.

**Quick answer**

- QAnon stylometry: two 2022 studies compared Q's writing with candidates' [1].
- OrphAnalytics: ~93% accuracy; style shifted over time [1].
- Cafiero-Camps: 99% (Watkins), 98% (Furber) identification [1].
- Result: Furber early, Watkins from 2018; both deny [1].
