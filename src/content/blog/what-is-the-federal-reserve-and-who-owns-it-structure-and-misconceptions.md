All right, guys, what is the Federal Reserve, and who owns it? The Fed is the central bank of the United States, created by Congress [1]. It has three main parts: the Board of Governors in Washington, twelve regional Reserve Banks and the Federal Open Market Committee, which sets interest rates. Who owns the Federal Reserve? The Fed's own answer: "The Federal Reserve System is not 'owned' by anyone" [2]. Its Reserve Banks are "required by law to transfer net earnings to the U.S. Treasury" [2].

Here's how the Fed makes money, rates and the economy, independence and the common misconceptions.

**Quick answer**

- Federal Reserve explained: the US central bank, created by Congress [1].
- Who owns the Federal Reserve: no one, per the Fed [2].
- Is the Federal Reserve private? The Fed calls that a misconception [2].
- Profits: sent to Treasury by law [2].

## How does the Fed make money?

The Fed earns interest on the bonds it holds and pays interest on bank reserves. Leftover earnings go to Treasury [2]. Does it "print money"? Read [whether the Fed really prints money](/blog/fed-creates-money/).

| Part | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| Board of Governors | Seven governors, set policy and supervise [1] |
| 12 Reserve Banks | Regional operations, payments, lending [1] |
| FOMC | Sets the federal funds rate [1] |

## Independence

The Fed makes rate decisions without day-to-day orders from the White House, but Congress can change its rules [1]. Read [why the Fed is kept apart from politics](/blog/federal-reserve-independence/).

## Common misconceptions

I get it, I get it. You've heard the Fed is a private bank owned by a few families. Here's what the record says:

1. **"Private owners get the profits."** Net earnings go to Treasury by law [2].
2. **"Nobody controls it."** Congress created it and can change it [1].
3. **"It's the government."** It's a public institution with independence in policy [1][2].
