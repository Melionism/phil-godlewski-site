All right, guys, let's clear up one of the biggest myths online. What does incognito mode hide? Mainly, your activity from other people who use the same device. It doesn't save your history, cookies or form entries after you close the window. It does not make you invisible to websites, your internet provider or your employer. That gap was at the heart of a big lawsuit: in 2024 Google agreed to delete billions of data records, update its disclosures and let Incognito users block third-party cookies for five years, to settle claims that it tracked people who thought they were browsing privately [1]. Google paid no damages and called the suit meritless [1].

Here's what it hides, what it doesn't, the common myths and when to use it.

**Quick answer**

- Is incognito mode private: only from other users of the same device.
- What it hides: local history, cookies and form data after you close it.
- Private browsing limits: websites, providers and employers can still see activity.
- Incognito mode myths: it doesn't hide your IP address or stop logged-in tracking.

## What does it hide?

| Hidden from | Yes or no |
| --- | --- |
| Others using your device | Yes |
| Your browser history after closing | Yes |
| Cookies after closing | Yes |
| Third-party cookies (Chrome, by default) | Blocked [2] |

It helps against some of [how trackers and cookies follow you](/blog/ad-tracking-explained/), but only within the session.

## What doesn't it hide?

| Still visible to | Why |
| --- | --- |
| Websites you visit | They see your visit and IP address |
| Your internet provider | It sees the sites you connect to |
| Your employer or school | On their network or device |
| Your account | If you log in, the site knows it's you |
| Downloads and bookmarks | They stay on your device |

## Common myths

1. **"Incognito hides me from Google."** Not if you log in, and sites still see visits [1].
2. **"It hides my location."** Your IP address still shows roughly where you are. Google announced IP Protection for Chrome Incognito in 2025 to address part of this [2].
3. **"It protects me from viruses."** No.
4. **"It's like a VPN."** No.

I get it, I get it. The dark window feels secret. It's really just a tidy-up button.

## When to use it

This isn't a "trust me" thing. This is "verify me."

1. **On a shared or borrowed computer.**
2. **For a gift search** you don't want family to see.
3. **To log into a second account** temporarily.
4. **Not** for hiding from your employer, provider or the sites themselves.
