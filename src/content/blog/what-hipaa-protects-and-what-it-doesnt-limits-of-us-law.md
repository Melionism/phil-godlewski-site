All right, guys, a lot of people think HIPAA protects all health information. It doesn't. What does HIPAA protect? Health information held by "covered entities," meaning health care providers, health plans and clearinghouses, plus their contractors. It gives you rights to your records and limits how those organizations share them [1]. What HIPAA doesn't cover is just as important: most health apps, fitness trackers, DNA companies, and what you post yourself. HHS says that once your data goes to an app that isn't a covered entity or business associate, it "is no longer subject to the protections of the HIPAA Rules" [2].

Here's what's covered, clinic data breaches, protecting your data and what's not covered.

**Quick answer**

- HIPAA explained: a federal law limiting how providers, insurers and their contractors use your health data.
- What HIPAA covers: medical records, billing, insurance claims at covered organizations [1].
- What HIPAA doesn't cover: most apps, wearables, DNA tests, employers' general records [2].
- HIPAA limits: it doesn't stop breaches; it requires notice and safeguards.

## What's covered?

| Covered entity | Example |
| --- | --- |
| Health care providers | Doctors, hospitals, clinics, pharmacies |
| Health plans | Insurers, Medicare, employer health plans |
| Clearinghouses | Billing processors |
| Business associates | Contractors that handle data for them |

Your rights under HIPAA include getting copies of your records and asking for corrections [1]. Start with [who can see your medical records](/blog/who-can-see-your-medical-records/).

## Clinic data breaches

HIPAA requires safeguards and breach notifications, but breaches still happen, and some are huge: the Change Healthcare attack affected about 192.7 million people [3]. Read [what happens after a hospital is breached](/blog/healthcare-data-breaches/).

## What's not covered?

This isn't a "trust me" thing. This is "verify me."

| Not covered by HIPAA | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| Fitness and period apps | Can share data under their own policies |
| Health data you send to an app | Loses HIPAA protection [2] |
| DNA testing companies | Separate policies and state laws |
| Your employer (outside the health plan) | Different rules |
| Social media posts | Public |
| Life insurance and some others | Different laws |

I get it, I get it. It feels like the law should cover all health data. In the US, it doesn't yet. That's why the app you choose matters: see [what the tracker knows](/blog/health-app-privacy/).

## A closer look: the "I agreed" trap

Many apps ask you to "authorize" them to pull your records from your doctor. That's legal and sometimes useful. But once the data reaches the app, HIPAA no longer limits what the app does with it [2]. Read the app's policy before you tap "Connect."

## FAQ

**What does HIPAA protect?** Health data held by providers, insurers and their contractors [1].

**What is HIPAA, explained simply?** A federal law limiting sharing of your medical records by covered organizations.

**What are HIPAA's limits?** It doesn't cover most apps or prevent all breaches [2][3].

**What doesn't HIPAA cover?** Apps, wearables, DNA tests, many employers.

**Can I get my records under HIPAA?** Yes, you have a right of access [1].
