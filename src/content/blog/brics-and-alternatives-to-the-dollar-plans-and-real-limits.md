All right, guys, "BRICS is launching a gold-backed currency to replace the dollar." You've seen that one. Here's what BRICS alternatives to the dollar actually look like on paper. At their October 2024 summit in Kazan, BRICS leaders called for more trade and financial settlement in local currencies and for linking payment systems, including a cross-border payments initiative [1]. They did not launch a common BRICS currency. Meanwhile the dollar is still about 57% of world reserves; the renminbi is 2.11% [2].

Here's what BRICS has announced, the payment projects, the practical limits and what it means.

**Quick answer**

- BRICS currency: not launched; no common currency was announced [1].
- BRICS dollar replacement: the stated goal is more local-currency trade [1].
- BRICS payment system: proposals to link national systems [1].
- Reality: the dollar's share is drifting, not collapsing [2].

## Payment projects

The plan is to connect national payment systems so members can pay each other without going through dollars. That's real work, but slow.

## Practical limits

I need you to see three:

1. **Different economies.** India, China, Brazil and others don't share one central bank.
2. **Trust between members.** Not every member wants to hold the others' currency.
3. **Market depth.** Few markets match the US Treasury market. The renminbi is just 2.11% of reserves [2].

## What does it mean?

This isn't a "trust me" thing. This is "verify me." BRICS can shave off some dollar use over time. A sudden replacement isn't on any official document.
