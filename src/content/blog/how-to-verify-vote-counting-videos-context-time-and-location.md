All right, guys, every election, a video goes viral: someone pulling ballots from under a table, a worker shredding papers, a van unloading boxes at 3 a.m. In 2020, the most famous was security footage from State Farm Arena in Atlanta that some said showed "suitcases" of hidden ballots. Georgia investigators reviewed the full footage and found it showed normal ballot processing in standard containers [1]. That doesn't mean every video is innocent. It means a clip without context can make normal work look like a crime, and a real problem can get lost in the noise. Here's how to check.

Here's finding the original, time and place, context, and asking officials.

**Quick answer**

- How to verify election videos: find the original upload, confirm date and place, get the full footage.
- Vote counting video fact check: compare with official explanations and records.
- Election video misinformation: old clips, other countries, cropped footage.
- Verify viral election video: reverse image search key frames and contact the county.

## Find the original

1. **Who posted first?** Search the earliest upload.
2. **Reverse image search** a few frames.
3. **Look for the full version**, not a 20-second cut.
4. **Check if it's old**: many "new" clips are years old or from other countries.

Then go to the records: [go to the originals](/blog/2020-election-primary-sources/).

## Context

1. **Normal procedures look strange**: ballots are stored in bins, moved in batches, scanned repeatedly when machines jam.
2. **Observers' rules**: workers may continue when observers step away.
3. **Damaged ballots** are duplicated by bipartisan teams, which can look like "filling out" ballots.
4. **Shredding** may be envelopes or misprinted test materials, not ballots.

## Ask officials

This isn't a "trust me" thing. This is "verify me."

1. **Call or email the county election office.**
2. **Look for an official statement** or investigation.
3. **Check if police or prosecutors reviewed it.**
4. **Watch for follow-up**: real crimes lead to charges.

In the State Farm Arena case, Georgia investigators reviewed the full footage [1], and two workers shown in the clip later won a defamation case against people who accused them.

## FAQ

**How do I verify an election video?** Find the original, confirm date and place, get context.

**Can real fraud appear on video?** Yes, which is why videos deserve investigation, not instant verdicts.

**What's the most common trick?** Old or out-of-context clips.

**Who can explain a clip?** The county election office.
