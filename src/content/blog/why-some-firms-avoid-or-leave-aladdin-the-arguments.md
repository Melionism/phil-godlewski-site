All right, guys, not everyone signs up. Why do firms leave Aladdin, or never join? BlackRock's own 10-K lists the reasons it worries about: clients may move to "standalone or internally developed solutions" because of "price, perceived market share, platform offerings, or flexibility" [1]. It also says Aladdin faces competitors, including some "with more sophisticated offerings" [1].

Here are the arguments: cost and dependence, competing with the vendor, what's documented, and how firms decide.

**Quick answer**

- Cost and dependence: one big vendor for core systems [1].
- Competing with the vendor: BlackRock also manages money against its clients.
- Aladdin alternatives: MSCI, SimCorp, Charles River and in-house builds [1][2][3].
- Firms rejecting Aladdin: BlackRock's 10-K names the risk; firm-by-firm details are rarely public [1].

## What about competing with the vendor?

I need you to see this one clearly. BlackRock managed $15.5 trillion at September 30, 2026 [5]. Some Aladdin clients are asset managers competing for the same investors. Some firms don't like running their business on a rival's software.

## What's documented?

Public, named examples of firms leaving are rare; contracts are private. What is documented: the alternatives exist and changed hands. State Street bought Charles River on October 1, 2018 [2]. Deutsche Börse closed its SimCorp takeover in 2023 [3]. Those deals built big rivals to Aladdin.

## How do firms decide?

This isn't a "trust me" thing. This is "verify me." The usual checklist:

1. **Fit** with their asset classes.
2. **Total cost** over several years, beyond year one.
3. **Exit cost** if they want to leave later.
4. **Vendor conflicts.**
