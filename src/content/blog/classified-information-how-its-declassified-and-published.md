All right, guys, if more election documents are coming, it helps to know how classified information actually gets released. The system runs on Executive Order 13526. It sets three classification levels: Top Secret, where disclosure could cause "exceptionally grave damage" to national security; Secret, for "serious damage"; and Confidential, for "damage" [1]. Information is classified by officials with original classification authority, and the order provides for declassification, including automatic review of older records and mandatory declassification review on request [1]. The president, as head of the executive branch, has broad authority to declassify, and in 2026 the DNI and CIA released election-related intelligence at the president's direction [2][3].

Here are the levels, who decides, the process and the limits.

**Quick answer**

- How classified information is declassified: by officials with authority, through review or presidential direction [1].
- Declassification process: review, redaction of what must stay secret, release.
- Who can declassify documents: the president, agency heads and authorized officials [1].
- Classified information explained: three levels based on potential damage [1].

| --- | --- |

## Who decides

1. **The president** directs or approves declassification.
2. **Agency heads**: DNI, CIA, FBI, DHS.
3. **Original classification authorities** within agencies [1].
4. **Courts and Congress** can request or press for release, but don't usually declassify themselves.

## The process

1. **Request or decision** to release.
2. **Review** by the owning agency.
3. **Redaction** of sources, methods and private details.
4. **Coordination** with other agencies whose information appears.
5. **Publication**, as on the White House transparency page in July 2026 [3].

## Limits

This isn't a "trust me" thing. This is "verify me."

1. **Sources and methods** usually stay redacted.
2. **Foreign partners' information** needs their consent.
3. **Raw intelligence** may be unverified, even when released.
4. **Declassified isn't the same as proven.**

## FAQ

**Who can declassify documents?** The president and authorized officials [1].

**What are the classification levels?** Top Secret, Secret, Confidential [1].

**Can anyone request declassification?** Yes, through mandatory declassification review [1].

**Is declassified intelligence always true?** No; it can be raw or disputed.
