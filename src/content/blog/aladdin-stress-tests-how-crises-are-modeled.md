All right, guys, here's the "what if" machine. Aladdin stress testing means running a portfolio through a shock, real or made up, and seeing what it would lose. Central Banking's 2022 review says Aladdin runs stress tests and scenario analysis, such as shocking covariance matrices or volatility [1]. The Bank of Israel used it to study Russia–Ukraine risk, with both long-term and 2014-based short-term correlations [1].

Aladdin Climate added another kind: it lets clients "stress test investments to estimate how they might perform in different climate scenarios" [2]. Here's what a stress test is, the two kinds of scenarios, how to read the results and what tests can miss.

**Quick answer**

- What it is: a "what if" run on a portfolio.
- Aladdin scenario analysis: historical and hypothetical shocks [1].
- Aladdin crisis simulation: e.g., a repeat of 2008 or a war shock [1].
- Limits: a test only covers the scenarios someone thought to run.

## What is a stress test?

A question you ask your portfolio: what if rates jumped 2%? What if stocks fell 30%? The system reprices every holding under that shock. See [how Aladdin measures portfolio risk](/blog/aladdin-risk-management/).

## Historical and hypothetical scenarios

| Type | Example | What it shows |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Historical | A 2008-style credit crash | How the portfolio would do if history repeated |
| Hypothetical | Rates up 3% in a month | A shock that hasn't happened yet |
| Correlation shock | Assets that usually differ start moving together | Hidden concentration [1] |
| Climate | A carbon price or extreme weather path | Climate exposure [2] |

## How do you read the results?

Look at three things: the size of the loss, which holdings drive it, and whether any rule breaks. A good test points at the weak spot. I need you to remember that a number like "down 18% in scenario X" isn't a forecast. It's an answer to one question.

## What can tests miss?

This isn't a "trust me" thing. This is "verify me." Stress tests miss:

1. **Scenarios nobody ran.** The test only knows what it's asked.
2. **New kinds of shocks.** History doesn't include everything.
3. **Liquidity.** Selling in a panic can cost more than the model says.
4. **People.** Decisions in a crisis are human.

## FAQ

**What is Aladdin stress testing?** Running a portfolio through shocks to estimate losses [1].

**What is Aladdin scenario analysis?** Testing historical and hypothetical scenarios [1].

**Does Aladdin do crisis simulation?** Yes, scenarios like a crash or a war shock [1].

**How does Aladdin stress test portfolios?** It reprices each holding under the shock and adds up the result [1].

**Can a stress test predict a crisis?** No; it answers "what if," not "when."
