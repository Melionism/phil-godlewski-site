All right, guys, here's why I don't buy overnight resets: why financial reforms take years is written all over history. Bretton Woods was agreed in July 1944 and didn't work fully until 1958 [1]. The Glass-Steagall Act was signed June 16, 1933, and parts of it lasted until 1999 [2]. Dodd-Frank was signed July 21, 2010, and agencies spent years turning it into rules [3].

Here's Bretton Woods, the Glass-Steagall era, Dodd-Frank and what the timelines show.

**Quick answer**

- Dodd-Frank timeline: signed 2010, rules written over the following years [3].
- How long financial reform takes: usually years to decades.
- History of financial reform: crisis first, then laws, then slow rulemaking.
- Lesson: big change leaves long public records.

## Bretton Woods

Forty-four nations met in July 1944. They fixed the dollar to gold at $35 an ounce and created the IMF [1]. Full convertibility of currencies came in 1958 [1]. Compare with [what is being promised next year](/blog/2027-financial-reset/).

## The Glass-Steagall era

The Banking Act of 1933 separated commercial and investment banking and created the FDIC, which started insuring deposits in January 1934 [2]. Its separation rules were repealed in 1999 by the Gramm-Leach-Bliley Act [2]. That's 66 years from one law to its repeal.
