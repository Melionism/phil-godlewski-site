All right, guys, you'll hear "21 million Americans can't prove citizenship" on one side and "everybody has a birth certificate" on the other. Both miss detail. The most cited number comes from a 2023 national survey of 2,386 adult citizens by the University of Maryland's Center for Democracy and Civic Engagement. It estimated more than 21.3 million citizens, just over 9%, lack easy access to a passport, birth certificate, naturalization certificate or certificate of citizenship, meaning they don't have one or couldn't get it by the next day [1]. PolitiFact rated a senator's use of it "Mostly True," but noted the survey didn't ask why people couldn't access documents; some, like college students, keep them at their parents' homes [2]. A different survey, the 2024 MIT Survey of the Performance of American Elections, looked at actual voters and found 97% had a driver's license and 85% said it was unexpired and matched their registration [2]. Different questions, different groups, different numbers.

Here are the surveys, how they differ, who's affected, and how to read them.

**Quick answer**

- Americans without citizenship documents: about 21.3 million adult citizens lack easy access, per a 2023 survey [1].
- Caveat: "easy access" means next-day access; the survey didn't ask why [1][2].
- Voters vs adults: a 2024 MIT survey of voters found 97% had a license [2].
- Undercount: name mismatches weren't measured [1].

## The surveys

| Survey | Group | Finding |
| --- | --- | --- |
| UMD CDCE, 2023 | Adult citizens | 21.3M (~9%) lack easy access to proof [1] |
| UMD CDCE, 2023 | Adult citizens | ~9% lack an unexpired driver's license [2] |
| MIT SPAE, 2024 | Voters | 97% have a license; 85% current and matching [2] |

Which papers count as proof: [which papers count as proof](/blog/documents-prove-citizenship-vote/).

## How they differ

1. **Who was asked**: all adult citizens vs. people who voted.
2. **What was asked**: citizenship documents vs. a driver's license.
3. **Timing**: next-day access vs. ownership.
4. **Sponsors**: the UMD survey partnered with groups that oppose proof-of-citizenship laws [1]; read it with that in mind.

## Who's affected

- Young adults 18–24, whose documents are often with parents [1].
- Men slightly more than women in the raw count (11% vs 8%), though women are undercounted on name changes [1].
- In Texas, Republicans were about twice as likely as Democrats to lack access [1].

## How to read them

This isn't a "trust me" thing. This is "verify me."

- "Lack easy access" isn't the same as "can't ever get."
- A voter survey misses people who aren't voting yet.
- The name-mismatch issue is a problem for many voters: [a problem for many voters](/blog/name-change-proof-citizenship-voter/).

## FAQ

**How many Americans lack citizenship documents?** About 21.3 million adult citizens lack easy access, per one survey [1].

**Is that number reliable?** It's a survey estimate with limits [2].

**Do most voters have ID?** Yes, about 97% have a license [2].
