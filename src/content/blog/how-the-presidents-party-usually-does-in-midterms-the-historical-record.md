All right, guys, before anyone tells you 2026 proves something shocking, look at the record. Midterms almost always punish the party in the White House. From 1946 to 2022, the president's party lost House seats in 18 of 20 midterms, with an average loss of 26 seats [1]. Democratic presidents lost about 29 seats on average and Republican presidents about 23 [1]. In the Senate, the president's party lost seats in 13 of those 20 midterms [1]. The only two times the president's party gained House seats were 1998 under Clinton and 2002 under Bush, right after 9/11 [1]. The biggest losses: 64 seats for Obama's Democrats in 2010, 54 for Clinton's in 1994, and 42 for Trump's Republicans in 2018 [1]. In 2022, Biden's Democrats lost the House but picked up one Senate seat [1]. In 2026, Republicans lost control of both chambers [2].

Here's the record, why it happens, the exceptions, and how 2026 compares.

## Why it happens

1. **Turnout gap**: the out-party's voters are more motivated.
2. **Referendum effect**: voters judge the President even when he isn't on the ballot.
3. **Balance**: some voters want a check on whoever is in power.
4. **Economy**: prices and jobs weigh on the party in charge.

## Exceptions

- **1998**: Clinton's party gained amid backlash to impeachment efforts [1].
- **2002**: Bush's party gained after 9/11 [1].

Both came with unusual national moods.
