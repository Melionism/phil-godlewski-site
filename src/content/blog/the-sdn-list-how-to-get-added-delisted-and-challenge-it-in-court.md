All right, guys, people imagine the sanctions list as a one-way door. It isn't. OFAC has a formal process to get off it. A listed person, or their authorized representative, can file a petition through OFAC's Reconsideration Portal, and OFAC reviews it under 31 C.F.R. § 501.807 [1]. There are two main grounds: a change in circumstances, meaning the basis for the listing no longer applies, or an insufficient basis from the start, such as mistaken identity [1]. OFAC aims to finish an initial completeness check within 7 to 10 business days and typically sends a first questionnaire within 90 days, but there's no fixed deadline for a decision [1]. If OFAC grants the petition, the removal is published in its Recent Actions and in the Federal Register [1]. Listed persons can also ask for the unclassified basis for their designation and can challenge it in federal court.

Here's how listing works, searching OFAC, press releases, and due process, appeals and court.

**Quick answer**

- SDN list removal: petition OFAC under 31 C.F.R. § 501.807 via the Reconsideration Portal [1].
- Grounds: changed circumstances or insufficient basis [1].
- Timeline: completeness check in 7–10 business days; first questionnaire usually within 90 days; no fixed end date [1].
- If denied: refile with new evidence, or go to court [1].

## Due process, appeals and court

This isn't a "trust me" thing. This is "verify me."

1. **Petition**: through the portal, with ID and facts [1].
2. **Questionnaires**: answer by the deadline or risk denial [1].
3. **Courtesy documents**: request the unclassified basis [1].
4. **Court**: designations can be challenged in federal court, typically under the Administrative Procedure Act; courts usually defer on national-security judgments.
5. **Honesty**: false statements can be a crime under 18 U.S.C. § 1001 [1].

How Treasury's sanctions office works: [how Treasury's sanctions office works](/blog/ofac/).

## FAQ

**Can you get off the SDN list?** Yes, by petition [1].

**What are the grounds?** Changed circumstances or insufficient basis [1].

**How long does it take?** No fixed timeline [1].

**Do you get notice before listing?** No, under Section 9 [2].

**Can you sue?** Yes, in federal court.
