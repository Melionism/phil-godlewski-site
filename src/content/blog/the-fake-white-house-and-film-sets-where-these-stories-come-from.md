All right, guys, you've seen the photos: Biden at a podium, and someone says "that's not the real White House." The fake White House set story says Biden worked from a replica, a studio or a green screen. Patel Patriot put it in one line: Biden is "on a set replica of the WH almost always" [1].

Some of the confusion has a simple source. Many White House events happen in the South Court Auditorium, on the White House campus in the Eisenhower Executive Office Building, not in the West Wing [2]. Here's where the stories come from and how to check a location yourself.

**Quick answer**

- Claim: a Biden fake Oval Office or a movie-set White House [1].
- Real venue often mistaken: the South Court Auditorium on the White House campus [2].
- Biden green screen White House claim: backgrounds said to be digital.
- How to check: compare the room with official photos and the event listing.

## What are the studio and set claims?

| Claim | What it says | What's often being shown |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fake Oval Office | A replica room used for speeches | A different room or a stage backdrop |
| White House movie set | Hollywood-style sets stand in for the real thing | Event stages with flags and drapes |
| Green screen | Backgrounds added digitally | Compression and lighting effects |
| Fake windows | Views outside don't match | Curtains, screens, or a different building |

I need you to know one thing: White House stages do get built. A podium, flags and blue drapes in an auditorium can look like a TV set because they are a set, in the stage sense [2].

## How do real sets get misread?

Here's how a real room becomes a "fake":

1. **Different room, same look.** The South Court Auditorium hosted Biden events such as the April 7, 2021 American Jobs Plan speech [2].
2. **Stage dressing.** Drapes and flags hide walls and windows.
3. **Lens and lighting.** TV lighting flattens depth and makes backdrops look painted.
4. **Repost quality.** Compressed screenshots blur edges like a green screen would.

## How do you check a location?

This isn't a "trust me" thing. This is "verify me."

1. **Find the official event listing.** It names the room.
2. **Compare with known photos of that room.** Ceiling, doors, carpet.
3. **Check other cameras.** Press pool shots from other angles show the walls.
4. **Look at the date.** Room setups change from event to event.
