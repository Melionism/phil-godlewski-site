All right, guys, one of the biggest changes of 2025 and 2026 wasn't a law at all. It was a database. SAVE, a federal system about 40 years old that was built to check noncitizens' immigration status for benefits, was overhauled by DHS in 2025. For the first time it was linked to Social Security records, which cover most Americans, and it began accepting bulk uploads of entire voter rolls [1]. The administration says the updated SAVE checked more than 65 million voters in 26 states since May 2025 and flagged 28,635 as potential noncitizens [1]. In Texas, 2,724 of about 18 million voters were flagged, and the state later reinstated about 20% of them as citizens [1]. DHS's own privacy assessment says SAVE "may produce inaccurate results" because of misspellings or incomplete data [2]. A federal judge blocked the overhauled system in June 2026 over privacy law, but on September 25, 2026, the Supreme Court let the administration use it while the case continues [1]. The SAVE America Act would make every state run its rolls through it within 30 days [3].

Here's what's required, the SAVE link, privacy, and state responses.

**Quick answer**

- Voter rolls federal government sharing: states uploaded rolls to DHS's SAVE system; the SAVE America Act would require it [1][3].
- Scale: 65M+ voters checked, 28,635 flagged as potential noncitizens, per the administration [1].
- Errors: some flagged voters proved citizenship [1][2].
- Courts: Supreme Court allowed use in September 2026 while litigation continues [1].

## The SAVE link

1. **Before 2025**: case-by-case checks using immigration numbers.
2. **2025 overhaul**: Social Security data, bulk uploads [1].
3. **Results**: Denton County, Texas, sent notices to 84 flagged voters; 14 proved citizenship, 14 had marked "not a citizen" on forms and none had voted, and 55 were canceled for not responding [2].

## Privacy

This isn't a "trust me" thing. This is "verify me."

- The district judge found the overhaul violated the Privacy Act and the Social Security Act; that ruling is on hold [1].
- DHS says SAVE is "a secure, and reliable way" to verify citizenship [2].
- SAVE may not confirm some citizens who acquired citizenship through parents [2].

## State responses

- **26 states** used the updated SAVE, per the administration [1].
- **Texas, Louisiana, Tennessee, Indiana** announced findings [2].
- **Other states** refused to share full rolls with DOJ, leading to lawsuits.

## FAQ

**Are voter rolls shared with the federal government?** Many states uploaded them to SAVE [1].

**What is SAVE?** A DHS citizenship and immigration verification system [1].

**How accurate is it?** DHS says it may produce inaccurate results [2].

**What did the Supreme Court do?** Allowed its use during litigation [1].

**Would the SAVE America Act require it?** Yes, within 30 days [3].
