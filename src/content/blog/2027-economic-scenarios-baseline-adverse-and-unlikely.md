All right, guys, nobody knows exactly what 2027 brings, me included. So instead of a prediction, here are three 2027 economic scenarios: a baseline, an adverse case and an unlikely one. This is how professionals plan. The Fed itself tests big banks every year against a "severely adverse" scenario, not because it expects it, but to be ready [1]. You can use the same idea for your own money.

Here's the baseline case, the adverse case, the unlikely case and how to prepare for all three.

- Recession scenarios 2027: possible; recessions are normal parts of the cycle [2].

## Baseline case

Slow growth, inflation drifting toward target, scheduled rule changes like the GENIUS Act taking effect [3]. Life mostly goes on. Compare with [what supporters promise for 2027](/blog/2027-financial-reset/).

## Adverse case

A recession. Unemployment rises, stocks fall, credit gets tight. It's happened before: from December 2007 to June 2009, unemployment went from 5% to 9.5% and peaked at 10% in October 2009 [2].

## Unlikely case

A total system breakdown: banks stop, money stops working. It hasn't happened in modern US history. Even 2008 ended in rescue and reform [2].

| Scenario | What it looks like | How likely | What to watch |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Baseline | Slow growth | Most likely | Inflation, jobs |
| Adverse | Recession | Possible | Credit, jobs, yield curve |
| Unlikely | System breakdown | Very rare | Bank failures, payments |

## How to prepare for all three

This isn't a "trust me" thing. This is "verify me." I need you to build one plan that survives all three:

1. **Emergency fund** in an insured account [4].
2. **Spread investments** instead of betting on one scenario.
3. **Keep records** of all accounts.
4. **Watch the data:** [inflation, credit and jobs data to follow](/blog/economic-indicators-watch/).
