All right, guys, does the Fed print money? Not the way people think. Paper bills are printed by Treasury's Bureau of Engraving and Printing. How the Fed creates money is mostly digital: when it buys bonds, it pays by crediting banks' reserve accounts. That's new money, created with a keystroke. In the 2008-09 crisis the Fed bought about $1.75 trillion of longer-term assets this way [1]. Then banks create most of the money you use: when a bank makes a loan, it creates a new deposit.

Here's the basic mechanics, QE and QT, reserves and bank lending, and who else creates money.

**Quick answer**

- Does the Fed print money? It creates digital reserves; Treasury prints bills.
- How money is created: the Fed creates reserves; banks create deposits by lending.
- Money creation explained: buying bonds adds money; letting them roll off removes it.
- QE: about $1.75 trillion in the first round [1].

## The basic mechanics

| Step | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| The Fed buys a bond | It credits the seller's bank with reserves |
| The bank's reserves rise | New money exists |
| The bank lends | A new deposit appears in a borrower's account |
| The loan is repaid | That deposit disappears |

## QE and QT

Quantitative easing (QE) means buying bonds in large amounts. Quantitative tightening (QT) means letting them mature without replacing them. The Fed decided in October 2025 to end its latest QT on December 1, 2025 [2]. Read [how QE and QT work](/blog/quantitative-easing-vs-tightening/).

## Reserves and bank lending

Reserves are the money banks hold at the Fed. Banks use them to settle payments with each other. More reserves can make lending easier, but banks still decide whether to lend.

## Who else creates money?

I need you to see this. Commercial banks create most of the money you use when they make loans. That's part of [the basic structure of modern finance](/blog/financial-system-works/).
