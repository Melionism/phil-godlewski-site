All right, guys, let's go back to the first week of November 2017. Drop 34 had promised an "arrest announcement of Mr. Podesta (actionable 11.4)," riots and temporary military control [1]. November 4 passed with small, peaceful rallies and no arrest [2]. So how did the young movement react? Three explanations showed up fast. First, the wording: "announcement" could mean the arrest had happened privately. Second, Q's own framing: on November 9, 2017, a drop said "Disinformation is real. DISINFORMATION IS NECESSARY," and on November 20 another said "Operations underway. Operators active" before repeating the line [3]. If some posts were meant to mislead enemies, a miss could be part of the plan. Third, security: within weeks Q said the 4chan board had been "infiltrated," without naming by whom, and moved to 8chan [4]. Many readers stayed. Researcher Marc-André Argentino points to the 1956 study "When Prophecy Fails," where a group expecting a flood on December 21, 1955, decided their faith had "spared the Earth from destruction" and then recruited harder [5]. Reinterpretation, not abandonment, is the common response.

Here are the first reactions, explanations, who stayed, and lessons.

**Quick answer**

- QAnon reaction to drop 34 failing: reinterpretation, not abandonment [5].
- Key line: "DISINFORMATION IS NECESSARY," November 9, 2017 [3].
- Other reasons given: the arrest was private; the board was "infiltrated" [4].
- Research: failed prophecy often strengthens commitment [5].
