All right, guys, this was the central fight of 2025 and 2026, so let's get the rulebook straight. The Constitution's Elections Clause says the "Times, Places and Manner of holding Elections for Senators and Representatives, shall be prescribed in each State by the Legislature thereof; but the Congress may at any time by Law make or alter such Regulations" [1]. So states write the rules first, and Congress can override them by passing a law. The President isn't named. That's why Trump pushed the SAVE America Act in Congress, which passed the House 218-213 in February 2026 and stalled in the Senate [2][3]. It's also why courts blocked key parts of his March 2025 election order. On June 13, 2025, Judge Denise Casper in Massachusetts wrote that "there is no dispute (nor could there be) that U.S. citizenship is required to vote in federal elections," but found the order's requirements could leave citizens disenfranchised [4]. Courts are the referees when the players disagree.

Here's the division of power, the fight since 2025, the courts' role, and what the President can't do.

**Quick answer**

- Who sets election rules: state legislatures first; Congress can change them by law [1].
- The President: can direct federal agencies, not state election offices.
- Courts: decide disputes; blocked parts of the 2025 and 2026 orders [4][5].
- Bills: the SAVE America Act passed the House, stalled in the Senate [2][3].

## The division of power

| Who | Power |
| --- | --- |
| State legislatures | Write election rules [1] |
| Congress | Can change rules for federal races by law [1] |
| President | Signs or vetoes laws; directs agencies |
| Courts | Decide whether rules are lawful |
| Local officials | Run the elections |

## Courts' role

1. **District courts**: first rulings, like Judge Casper's in 2025 and Judge Talwani's in 2026 [4][5].
2. **Appeals courts**: the First Circuit refused to pause Talwani's ruling in July 2026 [5].
3. **Supreme Court**: in August 2026 it said the states' challenge to the 2026 order was premature [6].

## What the president can't do

This isn't a "trust me" thing. This is "verify me."

- Set state voter ID rules by order.
- Change the federal registration form; the Election Assistance Commission controls it under federal law [4].
- Cancel or move Election Day; Congress sets it.
