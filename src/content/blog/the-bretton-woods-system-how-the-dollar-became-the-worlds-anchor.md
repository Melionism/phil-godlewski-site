All right, guys, what was Bretton Woods? It's the deal that made the dollar the center of the world's money. In July 1944, delegates from 44 nations met at the Mount Washington Hotel in Bretton Woods, New Hampshire [1]. They fixed other currencies to the dollar within a 1% band, and the dollar to gold at $35 an ounce [1]. They also created the IMF and what's now the World Bank [1]. The system ended in 1971 [1][2].

Here's the 1944 conference, how the system worked, the dollar's new role and why it ended.

**Quick answer**

- Bretton Woods agreement: July 1944, 44 nations [1].
- How it worked: currencies pegged to the dollar, dollar pegged to gold at $35 [1].
- Institutions: the IMF and the World Bank [1].
- End: Nixon closed the gold window on August 15, 1971 [2].

## The 1944 conference

World War II was still on. The goal: stable exchange rates, no competitive devaluations, and money for rebuilding [1]. It's a key chapter in [how we got here](/blog/history-money/).

## The dollar's new role

Countries settled their balances in dollars, and the US promised to keep gold at $35 [1]. That made the dollar the world's reserve currency. Its role is still huge: about 58% of disclosed reserves in 2024 [3].

## Why did it end?

I need you to see the pressure. More dollars circulated abroad than the US had gold to back. In 1971 Nixon ended convertibility [2]. Read [what the Nixon shock changed](/blog/end-gold-standard-1971/).
