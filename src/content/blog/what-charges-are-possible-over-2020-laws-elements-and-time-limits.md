All right, guys, if anyone is ever charged over 2020, it'll be under specific laws, each with specific elements a prosecutor has to prove. Let's lay them out. The FBI says it investigates election crimes such as ballot fraud, voter registration fraud, voting by ineligible people, and campaign finance crimes [1]. The Justice Department's November 2026 voter fraud announcement shows the kinds of statutes in use today: voting by noncitizens in federal elections, false claims of citizenship to register or vote, fraudulent registration, and false statements [2]. For conduct by officials, prosecutors have used conspiracy and civil rights laws. And every one of these has a time limit.

Here are the federal laws, their elements, state laws and the time limits.

**Quick answer**

- Federal election crimes: ballot fraud, registration fraud, ineligible voting, voter intimidation, conspiracy [1][2].
- Possible charges 2020 election: depend on who did what, and whether time limits have run.
- 2020 election conspiracy charges: conspiracy laws require an agreement plus an overt act.
- Election crime laws: federal and state; most federal crimes have a five-year limit.

## Elements

1. **The act**: what was done.
2. **Intent**: knowingly or willfully.
3. **Jurisdiction**: a federal election or federal interest.
4. **Proof** beyond a reasonable doubt.

## State laws

States prosecute most election crimes in local races, and many in federal ones. Georgia, Arizona, Michigan, Wisconsin and Nevada brought state cases over the 2020 fake electors effort; outcomes have varied, including dismissals.

## Time limits

This isn't a "trust me" thing. This is "verify me." The general federal limit is five years. For November 2020 conduct, that window closed in late 2025 for most crimes. A federal judge in Georgia said in July 2026 that the limit for any possible 2020 crime "has long expired" [3]. Read [the time limits on federal election crimes](/blog/statute-limitations-2020-election-crimes/) and [what Trump v. United States held](/blog/presidential-immunity-supreme-court-2024/).

## FAQ

**What are federal election crimes?** Fraud, ineligible voting, intimidation, conspiracy [1].

**What charges are possible for 2020?** Few, given time limits [3].

**What must prosecutors prove?** The act, intent and jurisdiction.

**Do states prosecute election crimes?** Yes, often.

**What's the time limit?** Usually five years.
