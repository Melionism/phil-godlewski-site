All right, guys, you don't need a law degree to read an executive order. You need four questions. Who acts? What must they do? When? And on what legal basis? Ask those of any order and most of the myths fall away. Take EO 13848 as practice. Who acts: the DNI, the Attorney General, the Secretary of Homeland Security, the Secretaries of State and the Treasury [1]. What: assess foreign interference, report on infrastructure, and block property of foreign persons who interfered [1]. When: within 45 days after an election, then another 45 days [1]. On what basis: IEEPA, the National Emergencies Act, immigration law section 212(f) and 3 U.S.C. 301 [1]. Notice what isn't there: no military, no arrests, no ballot seizures. If it's not in the text, it's not in the order.

Here's who acts, what is required, when, and on what basis.

**Quick answer**

- How to read an executive order: ask who acts, what's required, when, and under what authority.
- Who: named officials, e.g., DNI, AG, DHS, Treasury in EO 13848 [1].
- What: verbs like "shall," "may," "block" [1].
- Basis: the laws cited in the opening line [1].

## Who acts

Circle every official named. In EO 13848:

| Official | Role |
| --- | --- |
| DNI | Assessment [1] |
| AG and DHS | Infrastructure report [1] |
| Treasury | Blocking, rules [1] |
| State | Recommendations, entry [1] |

## What is required

- **"Shall"** means must.
- **"May"** means may.
- **"Determined by"** names who decides.
- **Exceptions** like "except to the extent provided by statutes" limit everything [1].

## When

Look for deadlines and dates: "within 45 days," "effective," and whether it lasts as long as an emergency [1].

## On what basis

This isn't a "trust me" thing. This is "verify me."

The first line lists the authority. An order can only do what those laws allow [2]. Then read the preamble for the reason given. In EO 13848, it includes a sentence worth reading twice: [a sentence worth reading twice](/blog/no-evidence-altered-results-eo-13848/). And why orders work this way: [a basic tool of the office](/blog/executive-order/).

**Checklist**

1. Number, date, Federal Register citation.
2. Authorities cited.
3. Officials named.
4. "Shall" vs "may."
5. Deadlines.
6. Definitions.
7. Exceptions and limits.
8. What's missing that people claim is there.

## FAQ

**What do I read first?** The authority line.

**What does "shall" mean?** A requirement.

**Where are definitions?** Usually near the end; Section 8 in EO 13848 [1].

**How do I know if it's still valid?** Check revocations and court rulings.

**Where do I find orders?** The Federal Register.
