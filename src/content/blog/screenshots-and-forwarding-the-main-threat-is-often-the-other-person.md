All right, guys, here's a truth that no encryption can fix. Can someone screenshot your messages? Yes. Always. End-to-end encryption protects a message on its way; once it's on the other person's screen, it's theirs. They can screenshot it, forward it, photograph it with another phone, or read it aloud to someone. That's why screenshots and message privacy come down to trust as much as technology. Even the best-protected apps, like the end-to-end encrypted ones CISA recommends [1], can't stop the person on the other end.

Here's why encryption ends at the screen, real cases, reducing risk and what to expect.

**Quick answer**

- Can someone screenshot my messages: yes, in any app.
- Forwarded messages privacy: forwarding moves your words to people you didn't choose.
- Screenshot notification: some apps alert you, many don't, and a second camera bypasses it.
- Rule: send only what you'd be OK with the other person keeping.

## Encryption ends at the screen

| Protection | Stops | Doesn't stop |
| --- | --- | --- |
| End-to-end encryption | Carriers, hackers in the middle, the app company [1] | The recipient |
| Disappearing messages | Long-term storage | Screenshots, a second camera |
| Screenshot alerts | Casual screenshots in some apps | Photos of the screen |
| View-once photos | Saving in the app | Another phone's camera |

## Real cases

You don't need headlines. Think about the everyday ones:

1. **A group chat** screenshot that ends up at work.
2. **A breakup**, where old messages get shared to embarrass someone.
3. **Intimate photos** shared without consent.
4. **Extortion**, where someone threatens to share private messages unless you pay. The FBI's IC3 logged 89,129 extortion complaints in 2025 [2].

## Reducing risk

This isn't a "trust me" thing. This is "verify me."

1. **Think before you send.** Would you be OK with this being shown to someone else?
2. **Use disappearing messages** for everyday chats.
3. **Be careful with large groups;** the more people, the more phones.
4. **Avoid sending** intimate images, ID documents or passwords.
5. **Turn off message previews** on your lock screen.

## What to expect

I get it, I get it. It feels unfair that the weak link is someone you trusted. If someone threatens to share your messages, don't pay, save the evidence and report it to the FBI's IC3 [2].
