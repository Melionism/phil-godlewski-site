All right, guys, your browser is the window everyone peeks through. Ten minutes in the settings can close a lot of curtains. Here are the browser privacy settings that matter most, plus search engines and extensions. One thing to know first: in April 2025 Google said Chrome would keep third-party cookies under its current user-choice settings instead of phasing them out, though Incognito blocks them by default [1]. So in Chrome, blocking them is up to you. The Electronic Frontier Foundation's Cover Your Tracks tool lets you test how trackable your browser is [2].

Here are the browser settings, search engines, extensions and what not to expect.

**Quick answer**

- Most private browser settings: block third-party cookies, send fewer site permissions, clear data on exit.
- Private search engine: one that doesn't build a profile from your searches.
- How to stop browser tracking: settings plus a reputable tracker blocker.
- Test it: EFF's Cover Your Tracks [2].

## Browser settings

| Setting | Where (most browsers) | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Block third-party cookies | Privacy and security | Stops cross-site ad tracking [1] |
| Site permissions | Location, camera, microphone, notifications | Stops sites from asking or accessing |
| Clear cookies on exit | Cookies and site data | Wipes trackers after each session |
| Safe browsing / HTTPS-only | Security | Protects against bad sites |
| Turn off "Allow sites to see if you have payment methods" | Privacy | Less fingerprinting |

These settings fight [how those ads follow you](/blog/ad-tracking-explained/).

## Search engines

Big search engines tie searches to your account. If you stay with one, turn off web and app activity in your account settings and delete history regularly. Or use a search engine that says it doesn't build search profiles, and read its privacy policy.

## Extensions

1. **Use one reputable tracker blocker.** More isn't better.
2. **Check permissions:** an extension that can "read all data on all sites" sees everything.
3. **Remove extensions you don't use.**
4. **Install only from official stores**, and check the developer.

## What not to expect

This isn't a "trust me" thing. This is "verify me."

1. **Logged-in sites still know you.** Settings don't hide you from your own accounts.
2. **Your internet provider still sees** which sites you connect to: see [whether your ISP can see what you browse](/blog/who-can-see-your-browsing-history/).
3. **Fingerprinting** can still identify some browsers; test with Cover Your Tracks [2].
4. **A VPN changes some of this**, not all: see [what a VPN does and doesn't protect](/blog/you-need-vpn/).
