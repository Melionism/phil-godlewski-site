All right, guys, forget the doom videos for a second. Here are the economic indicators to watch, the actual numbers that tell you where things are heading: inflation, interest rates, credit, debt and jobs. Every one of them is free and public. The Bureau of Labor Statistics publishes inflation and jobs data [1]. The St. Louis Fed's FRED database has thousands of series in one place [2]. The New York Fed uses the yield curve to estimate the chance of a recession twelve months ahead [3].

Here's inflation, rates and credit, debt and jobs.

**Quick answer**

- Key economic indicators: inflation, rates, credit spreads, debt, jobs.
- Recession indicators to follow: yield curve, unemployment, credit [3].
- Leading economic indicators: signals that tend to move before the economy.
- Where: BLS, FRED, Treasury [1][2][4].

## Inflation

The Consumer Price Index (CPI) from BLS shows how fast prices rise [1]. Watch the yearly change, not one month. This is the number that matters more than [the claims about a 2027 restructuring](/blog/2027-financial-reset/).

## Rates and credit

| Indicator | What it tells you | Where |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fed funds rate | Cost of short-term money | Fed, FRED [2] |
| 10-year Treasury yield | Long-term borrowing costs | FRED [2] |
| Yield curve | Recession odds | NY Fed [3] |
| Credit spreads | Stress in corporate borrowing | FRED [2] |

## Debt

Treasury publishes total public debt daily in "Debt to the Penny" [4]. In late 2026 it passed $40 trillion. The size matters, but so do interest costs and who holds the debt.

## Jobs

This isn't a "trust me" thing. This is "verify me." The monthly jobs report from BLS gives payrolls and the unemployment rate [1]. Rising unemployment is one of the clearest recession signals.

I need you to pick a few and track them monthly:

1. **CPI** (inflation) [1].
2. **Unemployment rate** [1].
3. **Yield curve** [3].
4. **Debt to the Penny** [4].
