All right, guys, Q didn't invent the anonymous insider. Before October 2017, 4chan had a run of posters claiming secret government knowledge, and knowing them helps you read Q. FBI Anon claimed "intimate knowledge of the inner workings of the Clinton case," posted all of it in one 4chan session on July 2, 2016, and was never heard from again [1]. High Level Insider Anon, or HLI Anon, posted from June 2016 to March 2017 in long Q&A sessions, with lines like "Waking up creates a lot of uncertainty for people" [1]. Researcher Mike Rothschild described HLI Anon as the closest in style to Q, though it gave straight answers rather than riddles [1]. After the 2016 election came CIA Anon and CIA Intern in early 2017; both vanished after a few weeks. A White House Insider Anon posted on and off in summer 2017 [1]. The shared pattern: claims of secrets about intelligence agencies, the Obama administration, the DNC or the Clinton campaign, delivered with riddles, rhetorical questions and prompts to "do their own research" [1]. Go back further and there's John Titor, who claimed to be a time traveler online from 1998 to 2001 [2].

Here's FBIAnon, HLIAnon, other cases, and the shared format.

**Quick answer**

- FBIAnon HLIAnon: anonymous 4chan "insiders" in 2016 and 2017 [1].
- FBI Anon: one session, July 2, 2016 [1].
- HLI Anon: June 2016 to March 2017 [1].
- Pattern: secrets, riddles, "do your own research" [1].

## The shared format

This isn't a "trust me" thing. This is "verify me."

- Claimed access nobody can check.
- Questions instead of statements.
- "Do your own research" [1].
- Identity shown by a password, if at all: [a password in disguise](/blog/tripcode/).
