All right, guys, drop 34 said "a state of temporary military control will be actioned" [1]. That line still gets quoted, so let's look at what U.S. law actually allows. There's no "martial law" statute that lets a president put the country under military control. The main legal path for using troops at home is the Insurrection Act. Under 10 U.S.C. 252, the president may use the militia and armed forces when "unlawful obstructions, combinations, or assemblages, or rebellion against the authority of the United States" make it "impracticable to enforce the laws" through the courts [2]. Before using that power, the president must issue a proclamation ordering "the insurgents to disperse and retire peaceably" [3]. It's been used before, for example in Arkansas in 1957 and Alabama in 1963 to enforce federal court orders [2][3]. Otherwise, the Posse Comitatus Act makes it a crime to use the armed forces to enforce the law unless the Constitution or Congress expressly allows it, with up to two years in prison [4]. In November 2017, no proclamation was issued and no troops were deployed.

**Quick answer**

- Temporary military control in Q: drop 34 said it "will be actioned" [1].
- Law: the Insurrection Act allows limited use of troops to enforce law [2].
- Required first: a public proclamation to disperse [3].
- Default rule: Posse Comitatus bars military law enforcement [4].
- 2017: no proclamation, no deployment.
