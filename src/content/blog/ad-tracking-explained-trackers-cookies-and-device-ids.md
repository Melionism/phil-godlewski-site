All right, guys, ever looked at shoes once and then seen them on every website for a week? That's ad tracking. Here's ad tracking explained in plain words: small pieces of code on websites and apps record what you do and link it to an identifier, like a cookie in your browser or an advertising ID on your phone. Advertisers use those IDs to recognize you across sites. And some of that data ends up with brokers: the FTC said X-Mode sold precise location data tied to mobile advertising IDs, which could be matched to real people [1]. In April 2025 Google said Chrome would keep third-party cookies under its current settings rather than add a new prompt to turn them off [2].

Here's how tracking works, browser settings, search history, incognito mode, VPNs and what's next for cookies.

**Quick answer**

- How ad tracking works: code on sites and apps ties your actions to an ID.
- Tracking cookies: small files that let ad companies recognize your browser across sites.
- Advertising ID: a phone-level ID apps use for ads; you can reset or limit it.
- What's next: cookies stay in Chrome for now [2]; other browsers block more by default.

## How does tracking work?

| Tracker | Where | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| First-party cookie | The site you visit | Remembers login, cart |
| Third-party cookie | An ad company on many sites | Follows you across sites |
| Tracking pixel | Emails, pages | Reports that you opened or visited |
| Advertising ID | Your phone | Links app activity to one device [1] |
| Fingerprinting | Browser settings, fonts, screen | Identifies you without cookies |

## What's next for cookies?

Google had planned for years to phase out third-party cookies in Chrome. In April 2025 it said it would keep its current approach, letting people choose in settings, and would add IP Protection to Incognito [2]. Safari and Firefox already block many third-party cookies by default. On phones, ad IDs matter more than cookies.

This isn't a "trust me" thing. This is "verify me." Check your phone's ad settings: read [android app permissions](/blog/dangerous-app-permissions/).

## FAQ

**How does ad tracking work?** Code links your actions to a cookie or device ID.

**What are tracking cookies?** Files that let ad companies recognize you across sites.

**What is an advertising ID?** A phone ID used for ads; you can reset or limit it.

**Is Chrome removing third-party cookies?** No; Google kept its current approach in 2025 [2].

**Can ad data identify me?** Yes, when tied to location and IDs [1].

## What to do next

1. Reset or limit your phone's advertising ID.
2. Block third-party cookies in your browser.
3. Review which apps can track you.
