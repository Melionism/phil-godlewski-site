All right, guys, here's an industry most people have never heard of, and it knows a lot about you. What are data brokers? Companies that collect information about people from many sources, combine it into profiles, and sell or share those profiles. The FTC studied nine of them and found that one had 3,000 data segments for nearly every US consumer, another added three billion new records every month, and the collection happens "largely without consumers' knowledge" [1]. Some brokers have even sold sensitive location data: in 2024 the FTC barred one, X-Mode Social, from selling data showing visits to places like medical clinics, places of worship and domestic abuse shelters [2].

Here's how the market works, removing your data, requesting and deleting, your legal rights and the ad tracking links.

**Quick answer**

- What are data brokers: firms that compile and sell personal profiles [1].
- How data brokers work: they buy data from apps, stores, public records and each other.
- Who buys your personal data: marketers, insurers, landlords, employers, other brokers, sometimes government contractors [2].
- What you can do: opt out, file deletion requests, use state tools like California's DROP [3].

## How does the market work?

| Step | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| Collect | Public records, purchases, apps, websites, other brokers [1] |
| Combine | Match data to one person using names, emails, device IDs |
| Infer | Guess traits like income, health interests, politics [1] |
| Sell | To marketers, people-search sites, risk-scoring firms |

X-Mode, for example, gathered location data through code inside other companies' apps and sold it tied to mobile advertising IDs, which made it possible to identify individuals [2]. It's part of [how tech companies use your data](/blog/data-tech-companies-collect/).

## Requesting and deleting

In many states you can ask a company what it holds and tell it to delete it. Read [how to request deletion of personal data](/blog/request-deletion-personal-data/).

## Your legal rights

There's no single federal privacy law covering brokers, but states are filling the gap. California's Delete Request and Opt-out Platform (DROP), run by the California Privacy Protection Agency, went live January 1, 2026; one request reaches more than 500 registered brokers, who must start processing by August 1, 2026 [3]. Read [gdpr and us state privacy laws](/blog/gdpr-us-state-privacy-laws/).

## Ad tracking links

Much broker data comes from ad tracking: device IDs and cookies that follow you across apps and sites [2]. Read [how ad tracking works](/blog/ad-tracking-explained/).

This isn't a "trust me" thing. This is "verify me." Search your own name on a few people-search sites and see what's listed.
