All right, guys, a decode can be smart, fun and still wrong. Here's a checklist I want all of us to run before we share one. Step one is facts: the exact drop text, its number and its timestamp, from an archive. For example, drop #34 was posted on November 1, 2017, at 10:56 PM Eastern and said events were "slated to occur 11.3 upon the arrest announcement of Mr. Podesta (actionable 11.4)" [1]. That's the fact. Step two is the hypothesis: what you think it means, written in one sentence. Step three is the test: what would have to happen, by when, for the hypothesis to be right, and what would show it's wrong. For drop #34, the test was simple: was there an arrest announcement on November 3 or 4, 2017? There wasn't. His brother Tony Podesta stepped down from his lobbying firm on October 30 amid the Mueller probe, but no John Podesta arrest was announced [2]. Step four is the conclusion: right, wrong, or can't tell yet. Researchers found Q archives disagree, so step one matters more than it looks [3].

Here are facts, hypothesis, test, and conclusion.

**Quick answer**

- Q decode checklist: facts, hypothesis, test, conclusion.
- Facts: exact text, number, timestamp [1].
- Test: what would prove it wrong, and by when.
- Example: drop #34 named 11.3; no arrest came [1][2].

## Test

1. **What must happen?**
2. **By what date?**
3. **What would prove it wrong?**

## Conclusion

This isn't a "trust me" thing. This is "verify me."

- **Confirmed**: event happened as predicted.
- **Failed**: deadline passed [2].
- **Open**: no deadline yet.
