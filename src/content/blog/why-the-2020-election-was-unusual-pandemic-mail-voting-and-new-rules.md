All right, guys, the 2020 election didn't look like any election before it, and that's a big part of why so many people still don't trust it. COVID-19 hit in the spring of 2020, and states scrambled to let people vote without standing in crowded lines. The result: according to the Census Bureau, 43% of voters cast ballots by mail in 2020, double the 21% of 2016, and another 26% voted early in person [1]. Only about three in ten voted on Election Day itself [1]. Many of those changes came through emergency orders, last-minute legislation and court rulings, and people who opposed them argued they bent the rules in ways that helped one side.

Here's the pandemic, mail voting, the new rules and their effects.

**Quick answer**

- Why was the 2020 election unusual: a pandemic, record mail voting and rapid rule changes [1].
- Mail voting 2020 changes: more states mailed applications or ballots, added drop boxes and extended deadlines.
- COVID election rules: many came through emergency orders or court decisions, not normal legislation.
- Effect: counts took longer, and early leads shifted as mail ballots came in.

## Pandemic

Spring 2020 primaries were postponed in several states. Poll workers, often older Americans, dropped out over health fears. Election offices had to plan a general election with masks, distancing and far more mail. For the order of events, see [how events unfolded](/blog/2020-election-timeline/).

## Mail voting

| Method | 2016 | 2020 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| By mail | 21% [1] | 43% [1] |
| Early in person | 19% [1] | 26% [1] |
| Total nontraditional | ~40% [1] | 69% [1] |

Mail ballots were checked by signature or ID depending on the state, tracked by barcode, and counted after verification. Democrats used mail voting more than Republicans in 2020, which mattered for how counts looked on election night.

## New rules

1. **Drop boxes** expanded in many states.
2. **Deadlines** extended in some places, like Pennsylvania's three-day window, ordered by the state Supreme Court and challenged all the way to the U.S. Supreme Court.
3. **Mailed applications** sent to all voters in some states.
4. **Witness and signature rules** loosened or tightened by courts.

Critics argued that only legislatures, not courts or officials, can set election rules. That argument shaped several 2020 lawsuits and later reforms.

## Effects

This isn't a "trust me" thing. This is "verify me."

1. **Turnout rose**: states with the most nontraditional voting saw turnout grow fastest [1].
2. **Counts slowed**, especially where mail processing couldn't start early.
3. **Early leads moved**, the "red mirage."
4. **Trust split by party**, as polls showed afterward. See [what the surveys found](/blog/polls-2020-election-doubts/).

## FAQ

**Why was the 2020 election unusual?** A pandemic pushed record mail and early voting [1].

**How many people voted by mail in 2020?** About 43% [1].

**Did states change their rules?** Yes, many did, some by court order.

**Were the rule changes legal?** Most were upheld; some were litigated for years.

**Did mail voting change the count?** It slowed counting and shifted early leads.
