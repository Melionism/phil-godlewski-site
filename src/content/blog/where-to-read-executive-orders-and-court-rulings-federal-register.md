All right, guys, you don't need anyone's summary, including mine, to know what an order or a ruling says. The primary documents are free. Executive orders are published in the Federal Register at federalregister.gov, which you can search by order number, like 14248 or 14399, or by date [1]. Federal court filings live in PACER, the courts' official system, which charges 10 cents a page with a cap of $3 per document, waives fees if you spend $30 or less in a quarter, and makes court opinions free [2]. CourtListener, run by the nonprofit Free Law Project, mirrors many PACER documents for free and lets you set alerts on a case [3]. The Supreme Court posts its opinions and orders on supremecourt.gov [4]. Agencies post their own: the Justice Department's press releases are at justice.gov. With those five sites, you can check almost any claim about an election order or case yourself.

Here's the Federal Register, CourtListener, tips, and what to avoid.

**Quick answer**

- Where to read court rulings: PACER (official), CourtListener (free mirror), supremecourt.gov.
- Executive orders: federalregister.gov.
- Cost: PACER fees waived up to $30 per quarter; opinions free; CourtListener free [2][3].
- Tip: search by case number or order number.

2. Open the docket.

## Tips

This isn't a "trust me" thing. This is "verify me."

- **Read the first and last pages** of a ruling: the holding and the order.
- **Check the date** and whether a newer ruling replaced it.
- **Note the court**: district, appeals or Supreme Court.
- **Look for "stay"** or "injunction" to see what's in effect.

## What to avoid

- Screenshots without a case number.
- Summaries that don't link the document.
- Old rulings shared as new.
- "Sealed" claims that nobody can check.
