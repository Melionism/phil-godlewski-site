All right, guys, here's a matchup for the wealth side. Aladdin vs Addepar comes down to who they're built for. Aladdin Wealth gives wealth firms "portfolio construction, modeling, and risk analytics tools" as part of BlackRock's bigger platform [1]. Addepar is an independent platform known for pulling a wealthy family's holdings, public and private, into one report.

One comes from the world's biggest asset manager. The other doesn't manage money. Here's what each targets, the key differences, typical users and how to compare them.

**Quick answer**

- Aladdin Wealth: BlackRock's tools for wealth firms and advisors [1].
- Addepar wealth management: data aggregation and reporting for advisors and family offices.
- Key difference: Addepar doesn't sell funds; BlackRock does [2].
- Addepar vs Aladdin Wealth: reporting focus vs risk-and-models focus.

## What are the key differences?

1. **Who owns it.** BlackRock also manages $15.5 trillion [2]. Addepar is a software company.
2. **Focus.** Aladdin Wealth leans on risk and model portfolios [1]. Addepar leans on seeing everything in one place.
3. **Size of firm.** Aladdin Wealth sells to large wealth firms; Addepar is popular with independents.

## Who are the typical users?

Big wealth platforms lean toward Aladdin Wealth; independent advisors and family offices often use Addepar. I need you to remember: the tool your advisor uses shapes what they see about your money.

## How do you compare them?

This isn't a "trust me" thing. This is "verify me." Ask:

1. Does the software cover all my holdings, including private ones?
2. What risk numbers does it show?
3. Is the software maker also selling me funds?
4. Can I get the reports myself?
