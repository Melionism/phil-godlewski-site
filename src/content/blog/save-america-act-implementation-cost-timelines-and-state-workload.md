All right, guys, a law is only as good as its rollout. So what would the SAVE America Act actually cost and demand from the people who run elections? First, timing: as passed by the House, its provisions take effect immediately on enactment [1]. Election officials say the bill includes no new money for the staff who'd review documents [1]. Orange County, California, estimated it would need 59 more staffers and about $6 million just to verify the citizenship of people who registered in 2024 [1]. Within 30 days of enactment, states would send their voter lists to DHS for checks against the SAVE database, and anyone flagged would have to prove citizenship or be removed [2]. Kansas's experience is the cautionary example: lawmakers estimated $12,500 in extra costs, but the Secretary of State's office spent more than $192,000 in the first full year, and the state later paid $1.9 million in attorneys' fees after losing in court [3].

Here are the costs, voter lists, penalties and the timeline.

**Quick answer**

- SAVE America Act implementation cost: no new federal funding; local estimates in the millions [1].
- Voter lists: sent to DHS within 30 days [2].
- Timeline: immediate effect upon enactment [1].
- Lesson: Kansas's costs far exceeded estimates [3].

## Penalties

Officials who register applicants without required proof could face criminal charges, and individuals could sue them [2]. That raises the cost of mistakes for offices.

## Timeline

This isn't a "trust me" thing. This is "verify me."

1. **Day 1**: proof of citizenship and photo ID requirements apply [1].
2. **Day 30**: voter lists to DHS [2].
3. **Ongoing**: flagged voters must prove citizenship [2].

Election experts recommend at least a year's lead time for changes like this [4]. The executive order route had its own problems: [march 2026 executive order mail ballots](/blog/march-2026-executive-order-mail-ballots/).
