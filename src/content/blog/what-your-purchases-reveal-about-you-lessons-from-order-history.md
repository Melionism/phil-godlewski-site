All right, guys, look at your last 50 purchases. Vitamins, a pregnancy test, a gun safe, a Bible, a dating app subscription, a debt counselor. What does that list say about you? A lot. That's what purchase history reveals, and it's why stores, card networks, apps and data brokers want it. The FTC found brokers create marketing categories from purchases and browsing, like "Diabetes Interest," and that one broker's database covered one trillion dollars in consumer transactions [1]. Loyalty programs and online accounts feed that machine.

Here's what purchases reveal, loyalty cards, crypto and how to limit the trail.

**Quick answer**

- What purchase history reveals: health, family plans, religion, money trouble, habits.
- Who sees it: the store, your bank or card, delivery apps, and often data brokers [1].
- Loyalty cards: discounts in exchange for a detailed buying record.
- Limit it: pay attention to accounts, loyalty sign-ups and sharing settings.

## What do purchases reveal?

| Purchase pattern | What it can suggest |
| --- | --- |
| Prenatal vitamins, unscented lotion | Pregnancy |
| Glucose strips | Diabetes [1] |
| Payday loan or debt service | Money trouble |
| Religious books, holiday items | Religion |
| Alcohol, tobacco, pharmacy items | Health risks |
| Kids' sizes changing | Children's ages |

The FTC warned that categories like these could affect how consumers are treated [1]. It's a big part of [how big tech uses your information](/blog/data-tech-companies-collect/).

## Who sees it?

1. **The store**, especially when you're logged in or using a loyalty card.
2. **Your bank and card network.**
3. **Delivery and payment apps.**
4. **Data brokers**, who buy from retailers and others [1].

## How to limit the trail

This isn't a "trust me" thing. This is "verify me."

1. **Check out as a guest** when you don't need an account.
2. **Skip loyalty programs** for sensitive purchases.
3. **Use cash** for purchases you'd rather keep private.
4. **Review sharing settings** in your bank and payment apps.
5. **Request your data** from big retailers in states that allow it.
6. **Delete old order histories** where possible.
7. **Be careful linking bank accounts to apps:** read [fintech app privacy](/blog/fintech-app-privacy/).

## A closer look: why "harmless" items matter

One purchase means little. A year of purchases is a profile. Brokers combine your buying with your browsing, location and public records [1]. That profile can shape what ads you see, what offers you get, and sometimes what price you're shown.
