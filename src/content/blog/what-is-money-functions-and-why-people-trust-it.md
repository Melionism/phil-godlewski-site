All right, guys, let's start at the very bottom. What is money? Money is anything people accept to pay for things, measure prices and save value. That's the textbook version: a medium of exchange, a unit of account and a store of value. Today the US dollar does all three at home and plays a huge role abroad. It's on one side of about 88% of all currency trades worldwide [1] and makes up about 57% of the world's official reserves [2]. Why does money have value? Because people trust it will be accepted tomorrow.

Here's how money evolved, the dollar's global role, why trust matters and related topics.

**Quick answer**

- Functions of money: exchange, unit of account, store of value.
- Definition of money: what people widely accept as payment.
- Why does money have value: trust that others will accept it.
- The dollar: still the world's main currency [1][2].

## How money evolved

Shells, metal, coins, paper, then numbers in bank accounts. Until 1971, the dollar was tied to gold for foreign governments [3]. Read [history of money](/blog/history-money/).

| Function | What it means | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Medium of exchange | Used to pay | Buying groceries |
| Unit of account | Used to price | "$4 a gallon" |
| Store of value | Used to save | A savings account |

## The dollar's global role

I need you to see how big it is:

- About 88% of foreign exchange trades involve the dollar [1].
- About 57% of world reserves are in dollars [2].
- About 60% of foreign currency debt is in dollars [1].

## Why does trust matter?

Money works only if people believe others will take it. That belief rests on laws, a central bank and an economy that keeps producing. When trust breaks, as in hyperinflations abroad, people switch to other money fast.
