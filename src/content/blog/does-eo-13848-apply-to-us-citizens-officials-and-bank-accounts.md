All right, guys, this is the question I get the most: can EO 13848 be used against Americans? Short version: the sanctions target foreign persons, and Americans are affected through the rules about dealing with them. Section 2 blocks the property of "any foreign person" Treasury determines engaged in, supported, or is owned by those engaged in election interference [1]. The order's definition of "foreign interference" also centers on foreign governments and those acting for them [1]. What reaches Americans is the prohibition side: U.S. persons, defined as citizens, permanent residents, U.S. entities and anyone in the U.S., can't deal in a blocked person's property, give them donations, or provide or receive funds, goods or services to or from them [1]. Breaking those rules can bring civil penalties of up to $250,000 or twice the transaction, adjusted for inflation, and willful violations can be prosecuted [2].

Here's foreign person vs US person, foreign vs domestic violations, dealing with blocked persons, and media, election workers, accounts and donations.

**Quick answer**

- Does EO 13848 apply to US citizens: not as sanctions targets under Section 2, which covers foreign persons [1].
- How Americans are affected: they can't deal with blocked persons [1].
- Penalties: civil and, if willful, criminal under IEEPA [2].
- Domestic election fraud: handled by other laws, not this order [1].

## Foreign vs domestic violations

The order is about foreign governments and their agents [1]. A purely domestic violation, like a local official breaking state election law, falls under state and federal criminal law. More: [foreign interference vs domestic election fraud](/blog/foreign-interference-vs-domestic-election-fraud/).

## Dealing with blocked persons

An American who knowingly sends money to, takes money from, or does business with a designated person can face liability [1][2]. Details: [americans dealing with blocked persons](/blog/americans-dealing-with-blocked-persons/).

## Media, election workers, accounts and donations

This isn't a "trust me" thing. This is "verify me."

- **Media**: IEEPA doesn't allow regulating informational materials or personal communications that don't transfer anything of value [3]. Reporting on or quoting foreign propaganda isn't a sanctions violation.
- **Election workers and officials**: the order doesn't make domestic officials targets [1].
- **Bank accounts**: a U.S. bank must freeze a blocked person's property it holds and report it to OFAC [4]. Your own account isn't affected unless you're dealing with a blocked person.
- **Donations**: you can't donate to a blocked person [1]. Foreign nationals already can't give to U.S. campaigns under federal election law [5].

## FAQ

**Does EO 13848 apply to US citizens?** Indirectly: through rules on dealing with blocked persons [1].

**Can Treasury sanction an American under Section 2?** Section 2 covers foreign persons [1].

**What are the penalties?** Up to $250,000 or twice the transaction, civil; willful violations can be criminal [2].

**Does it cover media?** IEEPA excludes informational materials [3].

**Does it cover domestic fraud?** No [1].
