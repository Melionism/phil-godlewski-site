All right, guys, whenever someone talks about charging a former president, the 2024 immunity ruling comes up. Here's what it actually said. In Trump v. United States, decided July 1, 2024, the Supreme Court ruled 6–3 that a former president has absolute immunity from criminal prosecution for actions within his "conclusive and preclusive constitutional authority," at least presumptive immunity for other official acts, and no immunity for unofficial acts [1]. The case came from the federal prosecution over Trump's efforts after the 2020 election. The ruling sent the case back to lower courts to sort official from unofficial conduct. After Trump won the 2024 election, the special counsel dropped that case.

Here's the ruling, official acts, applying it and the open questions.

**Quick answer**

- Presidential immunity Supreme Court 2024: absolute immunity for core powers, presumptive for official acts, none for unofficial acts [1].
- Trump v. United States immunity: decided July 1, 2024, by a 6–3 vote [1].
- Former president immunity: applies to any former president, of either party.
- Can a former president be charged: yes, for unofficial acts, and for official ones only if prosecutors overcome the presumption [1].

## The ruling

| Type of act | Immunity [1] |
| --- | --- |
| Core constitutional powers (e.g., pardons, commanding the military) | Absolute |
| Other official acts | Presumptive; government must show prosecution poses no danger to the executive branch |
| Unofficial acts | None |

## Official acts

The Court said discussions between the president and the Justice Department are core official conduct, and that official acts can't be used as evidence in a prosecution over unofficial acts [1]. That makes prosecuting a former president far harder.

## Applying it

1. **To Trump's 2020 case**: sent back, then dropped after the 2024 election.
2. **To claims about Obama**: any case over his acts as president would face the same test.
3. **To officials below the president**: the ruling doesn't give them presidential immunity, though other defenses may apply.
