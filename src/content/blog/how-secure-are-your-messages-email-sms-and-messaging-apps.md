All right, guys, are your messages secure? Here's a fact that surprised a lot of people: in December 2024, after Chinese-linked hackers known as Salt Typhoon broke into major US phone carriers, including AT&T, Verizon, T-Mobile and Lumen, the government's own cybersecurity agency, CISA, told highly targeted people to use end-to-end encrypted apps like Signal and to stop relying on SMS for login codes [1]. Regular text messages aren't end-to-end encrypted. Most email isn't either. Some messaging apps are, by default; others only if you turn it on.

Here's end-to-end encryption, the app comparison, the weak spots and what to change.

**Quick answer**

- Are text messages private: regular SMS isn't end-to-end encrypted; carriers can access it.
- Is email secure: usually encrypted in transit, not end-to-end; providers can read it.
- Can text messages be intercepted: yes, as the carrier breaches showed [1].
- Better: end-to-end encrypted apps, as CISA recommends [1].

## End-to-end encryption

End-to-end encryption means only you and the person you're talking to can read the message. Not the app company, not the carrier, not a hacker in the middle. Read [end-to-end encryption explained](/blog/end-end-encryption-explained/).

| Channel | End-to-end encrypted? | Who could read it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| SMS / regular texts | No | Carrier, attackers in the network [1] |
| Standard email | Usually no | Email providers |
| Signal | Yes, by default | Only the people in the chat |
| WhatsApp | Yes, by default | Only the people in the chat |
| iMessage | Yes, between Apple devices | Only the people in the chat |
| Telegram (regular chats) | No; only "secret chats" | Telegram servers |

## Weak spots

Even encrypted chats can leak through:

1. **Cloud backups** that aren't end-to-end encrypted.
2. **The other person:** screenshots and forwarding.
3. **A hacked or unlocked phone.**
4. **Metadata:** who you talk to and when.
5. **SMS login codes**, which CISA says to avoid [1].

## What to change

This isn't a "trust me" thing. This is "verify me." CISA's December 2024 advice [1]:

1. **Use an end-to-end encrypted app** for sensitive conversations.
2. **Switch from SMS codes** to an authenticator app, passkeys or a security key.
3. **Set a carrier PIN** to block SIM swapping.
4. **Use a password manager.**
5. **Update your phone** promptly.
