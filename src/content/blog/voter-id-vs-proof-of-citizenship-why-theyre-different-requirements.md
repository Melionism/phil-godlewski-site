All right, guys, these two get mixed up all the time, including by politicians. Voter ID means showing identification when you vote, to prove you're the person on the rolls. Proof of citizenship means showing documents when you register, to prove you're allowed to be on the rolls at all. A driver's license works for the first in most states, but a standard license usually can't do the second, because noncitizens with legal status can get one [1]. Thirty-six states have some form of voter ID law [2]. Far fewer require documentary proof of citizenship to register; Kansas tried it from 2013 until courts struck it down in 2018, Arizona has a two-tier system, and Indiana, New Hampshire, Ohio and Wyoming passed laws in 2025 [3]. The House-passed SAVE America Act would have required both nationwide [4]. Polls show strong support for both ideas: in March 2026, CBS found 80% back photo ID and 66% back proof of citizenship to register [5].

Here's how often noncitizens vote, state laws and cases, what each requires, and why it matters.

**Quick answer**

- Voter ID vs proof of citizenship: ID at the polls proves who you are; citizenship documents at registration prove eligibility.
- Voter ID: 36 states have laws [2].
- Proof of citizenship: a handful of states, several new since 2025 [3].
- Support: 80% for photo ID, 66% for proof of citizenship, per CBS [5].

## How often noncitizens vote

Audits find it rare. Georgia's 2024 audit found 20 noncitizens among about 8.2 million registered voters [6]. More: [how often do noncitizens vote](/blog/often-noncitizens-vote/).

## Why it matters

This isn't a "trust me" thing. This is "verify me."

- A strict voter ID law doesn't stop a noncitizen who registered with a false attestation.
- A proof-of-citizenship law doesn't confirm identity at the polls.
- That's why the SAVE America Act tried to do both. What the bill would actually require: [what the bill would actually require](/blog/save-america-act-requirements/).

## FAQ

**What's the difference between voter ID and proof of citizenship?** ID proves identity at voting; citizenship documents prove eligibility at registration.

**Is a driver's license proof of citizenship?** Usually not [1].

**How many states have voter ID?** 36 [2].

**Which states require proof of citizenship?** A few, including Arizona and several with new 2025 laws [3][7].

**Do Americans support both?** Yes, by large margins [5].
