All right, guys, the midterms are over, and a lot of us didn't get the result we wanted. So let's look at what happened and what it means for power in Washington. On November 3, 2026, Democrats won control of both the House and the Senate, according to the Associated Press [1]. In the Senate, Democrats picked up seats in Kansas, Iowa, Texas, Ohio, Maine, North Carolina and South Carolina and held Georgia and Michigan, including Republican Sen. Susan Collins losing in Maine [1][2]. In the House, they flipped more than two dozen districts, some of which Trump won by double digits in 2024; 59 House races were still uncalled on Wednesday [2]. Trump stays President until January 20, 2029. What changes is Congress. The new Congress takes office on January 3, 2027, and with it control of committees, investigations, the budget and which bills reach the floor [1]. Trump said he would accept the results and veto legislation from a Democratic Congress [1].

Here's what midterms are, who writes election rules, how the predictions held up, and common questions and terms.

**Quick answer**

- Power after the 2026 midterms: Democrats control both chambers of Congress from January 3, 2027, per AP [1].
- President: Trump remains in office until January 20, 2029.
- What shifts: committees, subpoenas, the budget, Senate confirmations and the legislative agenda.
- What doesn't: Trump's veto and his executive orders, within the law.
- Uncounted races: some House seats were still uncalled two days after the vote [2].

## What midterms are

Midterms happen halfway through a presidential term. All 435 House seats and about a third of the Senate are on the ballot, along with many governors; in 2026, 36 states elected governors [1]. ## Who writes election rules

Elections are run by the states under rules set by state legislatures, and Congress can change those rules by law [3]. Presidents can direct federal agencies but can't set state election rules by executive order, as the courts ruled in 2025 and 2026 [4]. ## Common questions and terms

This isn't a "trust me" thing. This is "verify me."

**Terms**

- **Majority**: 218 seats in the House; 51 in the Senate, or 50 plus the Vice President's tie-breaking vote.
- **Call**: a media projection, like AP's. It isn't the official result.
- **Certification**: the official result, done by states on their own schedules.
- **Divided government**: one party holds the White House, the other Congress.
- **Lame duck**: the period between the election and January 3, when the old Congress still sits.
