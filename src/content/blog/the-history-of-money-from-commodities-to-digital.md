All right, guys, the history of money is one long story of people trusting something new. Metal coins, then paper backed by gold, then paper backed by trust, then numbers on a screen. Two dates matter most for the dollar. In July 1944, 44 nations at Bretton Woods tied their currencies to the dollar and the dollar to gold at $35 an ounce [1]. On August 15, 1971, President Nixon closed the gold window, and the world's money became fiat [2]. Today the dollar is still the anchor, on one side of about 88% of currency trades [3].

Here's from barter stories to coins, Bretton Woods, the end of gold convertibility, toward digital money and the dollar's role today.

**Quick answer**

- Evolution of money: commodities, coins, paper, digital.
- How money evolved: each step needed a new kind of trust.
- History of currency: gold-linked until 1971, fiat since [2].
- Today: mostly digital balances in banks.

## From barter stories to coins

The classic story says barter came first, then money. Historians debate how much barter really existed. Coins made of metal came later, valued for the metal and the stamp on them. Start with [the basic question first](/blog/money/).

| Era | Money | What backed it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ancient | Metal, coins | The metal itself |
| 1800s-1930s | Paper and gold | Convertibility to gold |
| 1944-1971 | Bretton Woods dollar | Gold for foreign governments [1] |
| 1971-now | Fiat dollar | Law and trust [2] |
| Now | Digital balances | Bank records, the Fed |

## Toward digital money

Most dollars today are already digital: numbers in bank accounts. New steps are instant payments like FedNow [4] and regulated stablecoins under the 2025 GENIUS Act.

## The dollar's role today

This isn't a "trust me" thing. This is "verify me." The Fed's 2025 review says the dollar "will likely remain the world's dominant international currency for the foreseeable future" [3]. Some people want to go back to gold: see [return to gold standard](/blog/return-gold-standard/).
