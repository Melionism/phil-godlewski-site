All right, guys, every September someone posts "Biden renewed 13848!" or "Trump renewed 13848!" like it's breaking news. It's paperwork the law requires every year. Under the National Emergencies Act, a national emergency ends on its anniversary unless the President publishes a continuation notice in the Federal Register within the 90 days before that date [1]. EO 13848's anniversary is September 12. So there's been a notice every year since 2019: Trump in 2019 and 2020, Biden from 2021 through 2024, and Trump again in 2025 and 2026 [2][3][4][5][6][7]. The most recent was signed August 31, 2026, and continues the emergency beyond September 12, 2026 for one more year [7]. One change: unlike the notices from 2019 through 2025, the 2026 notice doesn't include the sentence about "no evidence of a foreign power altering the outcomes or vote tabulation" [6][7].

Here's every notice by year, whether Biden was required to renew, other orders Biden extended, and what expiry would mean, legally and politically.

**Quick answer**

- EO 13848 renewals by year: one continuation notice every year since 2019 [2][7].
- Why: the NEA ends an emergency on its anniversary without a notice [1].
- Latest: signed August 31, 2026 [7].
- Change in 2026: the "no evidence" sentence was dropped [7].

## Was Biden required to renew

No. The law lets a president let an emergency lapse or end it by proclamation [1]. Renewal was a choice: [why renewal was a choice, not a duty](/blog/biden-required-renew-eo-13848/).

## What expiry would mean, legally and politically

This isn't a "trust me" thing. This is "verify me."

**Legally** [1]:

- Powers under the emergency stop.
- Actions already taken, pending cases and penalties already incurred stay valid.
- Targets also listed under other orders, like the cyber order or the Russia order, stay sanctioned under those.

**Politically:**

- Letting it lapse in an election year would be read as a signal.
- Supporters of every side would argue about why.
