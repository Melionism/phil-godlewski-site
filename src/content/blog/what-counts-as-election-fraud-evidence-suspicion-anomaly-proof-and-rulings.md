All right, guys, here's the most important article in this whole series. Before we argue about any 2020 claim, we need to agree on what counts as election fraud evidence. A gut feeling isn't proof. A weird chart isn't proof. Even a sworn affidavit isn't proof until it's tested. Election fraud is a real crime, and the FBI investigates it: ballot fraud, voter registration fraud, voting by ineligible people and similar offenses [1]. People do get caught. On November 2, 2026, the Justice Department announced charges against 63 people for election-related crimes [2]. Those cases are built on documents, records and testimony that can survive a courtroom. That's the bar for any claim about 2020.

Here are the levels of evidence, error vs fraud, the scale question, common myths, and what real proof would require.

**Quick answer**

- What counts as election fraud evidence: records, physical ballots, witnesses who can be cross-examined, and rulings.
- Anomaly vs evidence: an anomaly is a question; evidence answers it.
- How election fraud is proven: investigation, charges, trial, conviction, or an election contest decided by a court.
- What would prove 2020 was stolen: fraud documented on a scale larger than the margins in several states.

## Levels of evidence

| Level | Example | What to do with it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Suspicion | "It didn't feel right" | Ask questions |
| Anomaly | A vote spike, a glitch | Look for an explanation |
| Allegation | An affidavit or video | Test it |
| Corroboration | Matching records, recount | Weigh it |
| Proof | Conviction or court finding | Accept it |

## Error vs fraud

A machine set up wrong, a clerk's typo, a delayed upload: those are errors. Fraud needs intent. The difference matters. Read [election error vs fraud](/blog/election-error-vs-fraud/).

## The scale question

Fraud has to be big enough to matter. In 2020, Biden's smallest margins in the deciding states were about 10,000 to 20,000 votes. Read [how many votes to change 2020 election](/blog/many-votes-change-2020-election/).

## What real proof would require: documents, witnesses and court

This isn't a "trust me" thing. This is "verify me."

1. **Documents**: ballots, poll books, logs, chain-of-custody records.
2. **Witnesses** who testify under oath and face cross-examination.
3. **Independent checks**: hand counts matching or not matching machine counts.
4. **A forum**: a court or official body that weighs it all.
5. **Scale**: enough to change the result; showing that one crime happened is a smaller claim.

Courts in 2020 dismissed many cases on procedure, but some did weigh the evidence. The difference matters: [dismissed vs decided on the merits](/blog/dismissed-vs-decided-merits/).
