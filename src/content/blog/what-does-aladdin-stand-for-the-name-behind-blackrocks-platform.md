All right, guys, let's start with the name, because the name already tells you a lot. What does Aladdin stand for at BlackRock? It's an acronym: Asset, Liability, Debt and Derivative Investment Network [1]. BlackRock first launched it in 1988 [1], the same year the firm itself got started. Today the company's annual report calls it "an end-to-end, SaaS solution for investment and risk management" [2].

Here's something I want you to notice. BlackRock's own Aladdin page never spells the acronym out. It just calls Aladdin "a tech platform that unifies the investment management process through a common data language" [3]. So let's spell it out ourselves, word by word, and see what the name tells you and what it doesn't.

**Quick answer**

- Aladdin full form: Asset, Liability, Debt and Derivative Investment Network [1].
- First launched: 1988 [1].
- What it is today: BlackRock's software platform for investment and risk management [2].
- What it earns: BlackRock's technology services brought in $1.6 billion in 2024, and Aladdin was "the majority" of it [2].

## What is the full name?

The Aladdin acronym stands for **A**sset, **L**iability, **D**ebt and **D**erivative **I**nvestment **N**etwork [1]. You'll see small differences in wording. A 2017 trade report wrote it as "Asset Liability and Debt and Derivatives Investment Network" [4]. Same letters, same idea.

| Letter | Word | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A | Asset | [1] |
| L | Liability | [1] |
| D | Debt | [1] |
| D | Derivative | [1] |
| IN | Investment Network | [1] |

That's the name. For how the system actually runs day to day, see [how aladdin works](/blog/blackrock-aladdin-work/).

## What does each word mean?

Each word names a piece of what a big investor has to keep track of. Here they are in plain language:

- **Asset.** What an investor owns: stocks, bonds, real estate, loans.
- **Liability.** What an investor owes. For a pension fund, that's the pensions it has promised. For an insurer, it's future claims.
- **Debt.** Bonds, mortgages and other loans. BlackRock's early business was built around this kind of fixed income.
- **Derivative.** A contract whose value depends on something else, like a swap or an option. These can move fast and need close watching.
- **Investment Network.** One shared system that ties all of the above together.

I need you to see why those four words sit side by side. A pension fund doesn't just want to know what its assets are worth. It wants to know if they can cover what it owes. That's the balance the name describes.

## Why does the name matter?

Because it tells you what the system was built for: risk. Not picking hot stocks. Not running the world. Measuring what you own against what you owe, across debt and derivatives, in one place.

The numbers show how far that idea went. In 2017, BlackRock said more than 85 asset-manager clients ran about $18 trillion on the platform [4]. By 2020, Aladdin "was said to have been used to manage some $21.6 trillion in assets" [1]. Central banks use it too: the Bank of Israel has used Aladdin since 2019 for reserves of $208 billion [5].

| Year | Figure | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1988 | Aladdin first launched | [1] |
| 2017 | 85+ asset-manager clients, about $18 trillion on the platform | [4] |
| 2020 | About $21.6 trillion said to be managed with it | [1] |
| 2024 | Technology services revenue $1.6 billion, up 8% | [2] |
| Dec 31, 2024 | BlackRock's own assets under management: $11.6 trillion | [2] |

Here's why that last row is important. BlackRock managed $11.6 trillion at the end of 2024 [2]. The trillions "on Aladdin" are a different number: assets that other firms run through the software. Those two get mixed up all the time.

## What doesn't the name tell you?

A lot. This isn't a "trust me" thing. This is "verify me." Here's what the seven letters leave out:

1. **Who makes the decisions.** The name says "network," not "decision maker." The 10-K describes software for investment and risk management, used by BlackRock and by "a growing number of institutional and retail investors around the world" [2].
2. **Who owns the assets.** Assets on the platform belong to the clients who use it.
3. **How it grew.** It started at one firm in 1988 and became a product sold to others [1][2]. See [how Aladdin grew from a 1988 workstation](/blog/history-blackrock-aladdin/).
4. **How it works on a real portfolio.** For that, take [a beginner's walk through Aladdin](/blog/blackrock-aladdin-explained-simply/).
