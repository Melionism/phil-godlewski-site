All right, guys, is the national debt a problem? Yes, it's a real long-term problem. Is it a bomb that goes off next month? No. The US Treasury's "Debt to the Penny" shows total public debt above $40 trillion in late 2026 [1]. That debt is mostly Treasury bonds held by Americans, US institutions, the Fed and foreign investors. And the bonds are priced in the world's main reserve currency, still about 57% of global reserves [2].

Here's how big it is, who holds it, the real risks and the exaggerated claims.

**Quick answer**

- National debt explained: what the federal government owes on its bonds.
- How big: over $40 trillion in late 2026 [1].
- Can the US default? It borrows in its own currency; the risk is political (debt ceiling), not running out of dollars.
- Real risk: rising interest costs over years.

## How big is it?

Over $40 trillion, updated daily by Treasury [1]. The dollar's reserve role helps the US borrow; see [why the dollar remains the world's reserve](/blog/dollar-reserve-currency-status/).

## Who holds it?

| Holder | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| Federal trust funds | Money the government owes itself, like Social Security |
| US investors | Pensions, banks, funds, individuals |
| The Federal Reserve | Bonds bought through its balance sheet |
| Foreign investors | Governments and private buyers abroad |

Treasury's data splits debt into debt held by the public and intragovernmental holdings [1].

## Real risks

I need you to see three:

1. **Interest costs** take a bigger share of the budget as rates rise.
2. **Debt-ceiling fights** create default scares, as in 2011 and 2023.
3. **Less room** to respond to the next crisis.

## Exaggerated claims

This isn't a "trust me" thing. This is "verify me."

| Claim | Reality |
| --- | --- |
| "China owns America" | A share of debt is foreign-held; most is domestic |
| "The dollar collapses tomorrow" | Still ~57% of world reserves [2] |
| "Debt will be wiped in a reset" | No law or plan does that |
