All right, guys, Q started as an American story, but it didn't stay here. By 2020 it had local versions around the world, each adapted to its own politics. Germany became the biggest. Voice of America reported in October 2020 that "Germany now has the second largest following outside the United States" [1]. A March 2022 study by the Berlin research group CeMAS found 12.4% of respondents in Germany agreed at least partly with central QAnon narratives, 16.2% in Austria, and 46% among unvaccinated respondents [2]. It counted 115 Telegram channels and 84 groups tied to QAnon [2]. In Japan, a group calling itself JAnon, "a self-proclaimed Japanese arm of QAnon," brought hundreds into the streets of Tokyo in November 2020 to back Trump's election claims [3]. In April 2022, five members of a QAnon-inspired anti-vaccine group, Yamato Q, were arrested over alleged sabotage at a Tokyo vaccination clinic [3]. On Twitter in 2019 and 2020, after the U.S., the most QAnon activity came from the U.K., Canada, Australia and Germany [4].

Here's Germany, Japan, others, and how it adapts.
