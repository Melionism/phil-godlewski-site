All right, guys, if you want to see what proof-of-citizenship rules look like in practice, look at Arizona. A 2004 state law requires documentary proof of citizenship to vote in state and local elections [1]. But federal law requires only a sworn attestation for federal elections, and in 2013 the U.S. Supreme Court ruled that Arizona must accept the federal form; so people who register without proof can vote only in federal races [1]. That created a separate "federal-only" list. Then in 2024 came the error: Arizona's Motor Vehicle Division had coded anyone with a driver's license issued before 1996 as a citizen, even without proof, and duplicate or renewed licenses made the dates unreliable [1]. The number of affected voters grew from about 98,000 to at least 218,000: 79,000 Republicans, 61,000 Democrats and 76,000 unaffiliated [1]. Only two had been identified as noncitizens when Votebeat reported it [1]. In September 2024, the Arizona Supreme Court let them vote full ballots that November because the mistake wasn't theirs [1].

Here's the system, federal-only voters, the database error, and lessons.

**Quick answer**

- Arizona two tier voter registration: proof of citizenship for state races; federal-only ballots without it [1].
- Federal-only voters: can vote for federal offices only.
- 2024 error: 218,000 voters wrongly coded as having proof [1].
- Ruling: they voted full ballots in 2024 [1].

## Federal-only voters

They're citizens who swore they were but didn't provide documents, or whose documents weren't on file. They still vote for President and Congress.

## The database error

1. **Pre-1996 licenses** were coded as proof of citizenship [1].
2. **Duplicates and renewals** showed misleading dates [1].
3. **218,000 voters** affected [1].
4. **Party split**: 79,000 R, 61,000 D, 76,000 unaffiliated [1].
5. **Two** confirmed noncitizens at the time [1].
