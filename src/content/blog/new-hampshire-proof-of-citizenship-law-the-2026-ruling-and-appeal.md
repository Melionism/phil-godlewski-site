All right, guys, New Hampshire is the newest test case. In 2024, then-Gov. Chris Sununu signed HB 1569, which required documentary proof of citizenship to register and removed the option of proving citizenship with a sworn affidavit [1][2]. On May 28, 2026, U.S. District Judge Samantha Elliott ruled that removing the affidavit was unconstitutional [2]. She wrote that "the evidence shows that this is the only method of proof available to a significant number of New Hampshire voters," and that "if wrongful voting is rare in New Hampshire, wrongful voting by noncitizens is essentially non-existent" [1]. The record showed 47 wrongful-voting cases among about 8.3 million votes from 1998 to 2024, with about eight possible noncitizen voters [1]. She didn't rule that proof-of-citizenship requirements are unconstitutional in general [1]. The state, which calls the law a "common-sense approach to voter registration," appealed to the First Circuit [1][2]. Secretary of State David Scanlan said affidavits would be restored, so for the fall 2026 elections, first-time registrants didn't need hard proof of citizenship [1][2].

Here's the law, the ruling, the appeal, and what's next.

**Quick answer**

- New Hampshire proof of citizenship law: HB 1569 (2024) removed the affidavit option [1][2].
- Ruling: May 28, 2026, Judge Samantha Elliott struck that part down [2].
- Appeal: to the First Circuit; final decision at least a year away [2].
- Now: affidavits restored for 2026 elections [1][2].

## The ruling

1. **The affidavit** was the only route for many voters [1].
2. **The burden** was significant and did little to serve the state's interest [1].
3. **Not decided**: whether proof-of-citizenship laws are unconstitutional in general [1].

## The appeal

The state argues [2]:

- Burdens are ordinary, citing the Supreme Court's 2008 Crawford v. Marion County voter ID case.
- Individual plaintiffs now have documents, so they lack standing.
- The court overstepped by restoring a repealed affidavit.
- A law shouldn't fall entirely over its effect on some voters.

## FAQ

**What does New Hampshire's law require?** Proof of citizenship to register, without an affidavit option [1].

**What did the court decide?** Removing the affidavit was unconstitutional [2].

**Is the law gone?** Only the affidavit removal was struck; other rules stand [1].

**Is there an appeal?** Yes, to the First Circuit [2].

**What do new voters need now?** Proof of identity, age and domicile; an affidavit for citizenship [2].
