All right, guys, "13848 means treason arrests are coming" is one of the most repeated lines I've seen. I get the anger behind it. But treason and sanctions are two completely different things. Treason is the only crime defined in the Constitution. Article III, Section 3 says it "shall consist only in levying War against them, or in adhering to their Enemies, giving them Aid and Comfort," and that no one can be convicted "unless on the Testimony of two Witnesses to the same overt Act, or on Confession in open Court" [1]. That requires a criminal trial with a jury. EO 13848 is a sanctions order: it lets Treasury freeze property of designated foreign persons and lets the government bar their entry [2]. There's no arrest power in it. Arrests come from criminal law, prosecutors and judges.

Here are the claims, why sanctions aren't arrests, the constitutional text on treason, and why it doesn't fit.

**Quick answer**

- EO 13848 treason: the order doesn't create or mention treason charges [2].
- Treason: defined in Article III; requires two witnesses to the same overt act or a confession in open court [1].
- Sanctions: civil and administrative, against foreign persons [2].
- Arrests: only through criminal law and courts.

## The constitutional text on treason

Article III, Section 3 [1]:

1. **Levying war** against the U.S., **or**
2. **Adhering to enemies**, giving them aid and comfort.
3. **Two witnesses** to the same overt act, or a confession in open court.

"Enemies" has traditionally meant nations in a declared or open war with the U.S. Disputed elections don't fit that.

## Why it doesn't fit

This isn't a "trust me" thing. This is "verify me."

- The order names no crimes and no arrest powers [2].
- Treason needs a criminal trial [1].
- Military tribunals for civilians in the U.S. are a separate legal question; the order doesn't address them [2].

Allegations against specific people are not established facts unless proven in court. And the claim about homes: [the claim about homes](/blog/can-eo-13848-take-your-house/).

## FAQ

**Does EO 13848 mention treason?** No [2].

**Can sanctions lead to arrests?** Violating sanctions can be a crime, prosecuted in court.
