All right, guys, you hear this name on every show. What is BlackRock? It's the world's biggest asset manager: a public company that invests other people's money and sells technology. At September 30, 2026, it managed $15.5 trillion [1]. In 2025, its revenue was $24.2 billion, with about 24,900 employees in more than 30 countries [2]. It's "independent, publicly traded company, with no single majority shareholder," its 10-K says [2].

That's BlackRock explained in one paragraph. Here's the company overview: its assets, founding, business lines, owners and technology.

**Quick answer**

- What does BlackRock do? Manages money for clients and sells software like Aladdin [2].
- Assets under management: $15.5 trillion at September 30, 2026 [1].
- Revenue: $24.2 billion in 2025 [2].
- Founded: 1988, by eight people including Larry Fink and Rob Kapito [3].

## How much does it manage?

$15.5 trillion at September 30, 2026 [1], up from $14.0 trillion at the end of 2025 [2]. That's client money, not BlackRock's own. Read [blackrock assets under management](/blog/blackrock-assets-under-management/).

## Who founded and leads it?

Eight founders in 1988, including Larry Fink and Rob Kapito [3]. Fink is Chairman and CEO. Read [larry fink blackrock founding](/blog/larry-fink-blackrock-founding/).

## Who owns it, and what influence do shareholders have?

It's public. There were 155,541,536 shares outstanding on January 31, 2026, and no single majority shareholder [2]. I need you to see the difference: BlackRock manages trillions, but it's owned by its shareholders like any public company. Read [who owns blackrock](/blog/who-owns-blackrock/).

## How does technology fit in?

Aladdin is the core: "the Company's proprietary technology platform" [2]. Read [an overview of the BlackRock Aladdin system](/blog/blackrock-aladdin/).
