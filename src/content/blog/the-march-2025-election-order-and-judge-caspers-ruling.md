All right, guys, the first big court test of Trump's election orders came in Boston. On March 25, 2025, Trump signed Executive Order 14248, "Preserving and Protecting the Integrity of American Elections." It directed the Election Assistance Commission to require documentary proof of citizenship on the federal voter registration form and sought to bar states from counting mail or absentee ballots that arrive after Election Day [1]. Nineteen states with Democratic attorneys general sued [1]. On June 13, 2025, U.S. District Judge Denise Casper granted a preliminary injunction [1]. She started from common ground: "There is no dispute (nor could there be) that U.S. citizenship is required to vote in federal elections" [1]. But she found that without a pause, "citizens will be disenfranchised," and that the requirements "could create chaos and confusion that could result in voters losing trust in the election process" [1]. A permanent injunction on key provisions followed on June 24, 2026 [2].

Here's what it ordered, the ruling, where to read rulings, and appeals.

**Quick answer**

- Judge Casper election order ruling: preliminary injunction against EO 14248, June 13, 2025 [1].
- Court: U.S. District Court for the District of Massachusetts [1].
- Plaintiffs: 19 states [1].
- Later: permanent injunction on key provisions, June 24, 2026 [2].

## The ruling

1. **Citizenship**: required to vote, no dispute [1].
2. **Harm**: citizens would be disenfranchised without a pause [1].
3. **Confusion**: risk of "chaos" and lost trust [1].
