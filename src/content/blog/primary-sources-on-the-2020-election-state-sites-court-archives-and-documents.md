All right, guys, you've heard me say it a thousand times: don't trust me, verify me. This article gives you the tools to do exactly that. Primary sources are the original records: certified results from the states, court opinions, official audit reports and government documents. Not a screenshot, not a clip, not my summary. The Federal Election Commission publishes a full report of 2020 results by state [1]. The National Archives keeps the certified electoral votes [2]. State election offices post their audits: Georgia's hand count report [3] and Michigan's Antrim County hand count [4], for example. And the White House posted the documents it released in July 2026 online [5].

Here are state sites, court archives, federal documents and how to verify video.

**Quick answer**

- 2020 election primary sources: state certified results, court opinions, audit reports, federal records [1][2].
- 2020 election documents: the FEC results report, National Archives certificates, intelligence assessments.
- State election results archive: each secretary of state's website.
- 2020 election court records: PACER for federal cases; state court sites for state cases.

## Court archives

Federal cases are on PACER; many opinions are also free on court websites. State courts have their own search tools. Read the opinion, not the press release about it. Viral clips of counting rooms deserve the same care: [how to check viral clips of counting rooms](/blog/verify-election-videos/).

## How to verify video

This isn't a "trust me" thing. This is "verify me."

1. **Find who first posted it.**
2. **Confirm the date and place.**
3. **Get the official explanation** from the county.
4. **Watch the full footage**, not a cut.
