All right, guys, you've heard it: "BlackRock got rich off the 2008 crash." Here's what the record says. In 2008 the Federal Reserve Bank of New York set up three vehicles, Maiden Lane, Maiden Lane II and Maiden Lane III, to hold assets from Bear Stearns and AIG [1]. The New York Fed retained BlackRock to manage the assets in Maiden Lane and Maiden Lane II [1]. All three loans were repaid in full, with net gains for the public of about $2.5 billion, $2.8 billion and $6.6 billion [1].

Here's what happened, the contracts with authorities, the firm's real role, and the criticism and context.

**Quick answer**

- BlackRock financial crisis role: hired manager of Fed-held portfolios [1].
- BlackRock Federal Reserve 2008: Maiden Lane and Maiden Lane II [1].
- Outcome: loans repaid, with net gains for the Fed [1].
- After the crisis: BlackRock bought Barclays Global Investors in 2009 [2].

## What happened?

Bear Stearns collapsed in March 2008, and AIG needed rescue in the fall. The Fed took on pools of mortgage assets and needed someone to value and manage them [1]. BlackRock was a bond firm built around risk models, which goes back to [the founder's story](/blog/larry-fink-blackrock-founding/).

## What was the firm's real role?

A manager under contract. BlackRock didn't lend the money. The Fed did [1]. BlackRock's job was to value, manage and later sell the assets. That's the same model as its business: manage assets for a client, for a fee.

## Criticism and context

I get it, I get it. Critics ask fair questions. Should one private firm get that close to the government? Did the work help it win more business later? Those questions also came up in 2020, when the Fed hired BlackRock again during COVID.

This isn't a "trust me" thing. This is "verify me." The Fed's own page lists the vehicles, loans and results [1]. What you won't find there is proof that BlackRock "caused" or "ran" the crash.
