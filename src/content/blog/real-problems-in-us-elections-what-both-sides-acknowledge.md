All right, guys, saying 2020 wasn't stolen is not the same as saying American elections have no problems. They do, and people on both sides admit it. Voter rolls carry people who've moved or died: North Carolina, for example, identified about 34,000 deceased people on its rolls, though officials stressed that doesn't mean illegal votes were cast [1]. Noncitizens do occasionally register and vote: in Iowa, an initial list of 2,186 suspected noncitizen registrants shrank to 277 confirmed after an audit, and 35 of them voted in 2024 [1]. Long lines, outdated equipment, underfunded offices and slow counts are real. So is foreign interference in the information space. The fight is usually about how big these problems are and how to fix them.

Here are shared concerns, voter rolls, long waits and funding, security, and what reforms could help.

**Quick answer**

- US election problems: messy voter rolls, rare illegal voting, long lines, slow counts, outdated equipment.
- Election administration problems: underfunded offices and staff shortages.
- Bipartisan election concerns: accurate rolls, secure systems and fast, transparent counts.
- Where the sides split: how common fraud is versus how common barriers to voting are.

## Voter rolls

Rolls lag behind real life: people move, die, or register twice in different states. Federal law limits how fast names can be removed. Mismatches are usually paperwork, not votes. Ideas to fix it are in [reform ideas from both parties](/blog/bipartisan-election-reform-proposals/).

## Long waits and funding

Some precincts have hours-long lines; others have none. Local offices often run on thin budgets and part-time staff.

## Security

This isn't a "trust me" thing. This is "verify me." Systems have vulnerabilities. Paper ballots, audits and testing are the defense. Mail voting fraud is rare: a Brookings analysis cited by CBS found about four cases per 10 million mail votes from 2016 to 2022 [1].

## What reforms could help

1. **Better roll maintenance** with interstate data sharing.
2. **Paper ballots everywhere** with routine audits.
3. **Early processing of mail ballots** for faster counts.
4. **Stable funding** for local offices.
5. **Clear, consistent rules** set well before an election.
