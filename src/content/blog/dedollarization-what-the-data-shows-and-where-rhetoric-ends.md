All right, guys, is the world moving away from the dollar? A little, slowly, and mostly toward gold and smaller currencies. That's dedollarization in the data. The dollar's share of world reserves was 72% at its 2001 peak [1], about 58% in 2024 [1] and 56.70% in Q2 2026 [2]. Meanwhile central banks kept buying gold in 2025 at levels well above history, according to the World Gold Council [3]. But the dollar is still on one side of about 88% of all currency trades [1].

Here are reserve shares, trade invoicing, central bank gold, and rhetoric vs data.

**Quick answer**

- Dedollarization explained: countries using fewer dollars in reserves and trade.
- Is the world moving away from the dollar? Slowly, over decades [1][2].
- Dedollarization data: reserves down from 72% to about 57% [1][2].
- Gold: central banks are buying heavily [3].

## Trade invoicing

Outside Europe, most trade is still priced in dollars. From 1999 to 2019 the dollar's share averaged 96% in the Americas, 74% in Asia-Pacific and 79% elsewhere; in Europe, the euro leads at 66% [1].

## Central bank gold

This is the real trend. Central banks have been buying gold at a strong pace, and the World Gold Council reported buying stayed well above historical levels in 2025 [3]. Some of that is a hedge against sanctions and the dollar.

## Rhetoric vs data

I get it, I get it. Speeches say "the dollar is over." The data says "slow drift." Here's how to keep them apart:

1. **Check the share,** not the headline [2].
2. **Check the years:** trends take decades [1].
3. **Check what replaced it:** mostly the euro, gold and smaller currencies [2].
