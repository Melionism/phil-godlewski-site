All right, guys, in early 2026 Sen. Mike Lee of Utah, the SAVE America Act's lead sponsor in the Senate, pushed an idea a lot of you liked: make Democrats actually stand up and talk if they want to block it. That's the "talking filibuster." Under existing Senate rules, each senator gets two speeches on a bill, plus up to two more on each motion or amendment [1]. Lee's argument was that enforcing those rules isn't eliminating the filibuster; it just forces opponents to hold the floor until they run out of speeches [1]. Sens. Ted Cruz, Ron Johnson and Rick Scott backed him [1]. Majority Leader John Thune refused to go all the way. He said he "couldn't find a single example in modern Senate history" of a talking filibuster leading to a bill's passage and warned critics were "over-promising and creating false expectations" [2]. The catch: amendments are unlimited, so each one resets the speech count, and Republicans lacked the votes to limit them [1].

Here's how it would work, who benefits, why it failed, and its limits.

**Quick answer**

- Talking filibuster: forcing opponents to keep speaking to block a vote [1].
- Mike Lee's plan: enforce the two-speech rule on the SAVE America Act [1].
- Why it failed: unlimited amendments, floor time, and not enough GOP votes [1][2].
- Result: the bill never got a final vote [3].

## How it would work

1. **Majority keeps the bill on the floor** around the clock.
2. **Each opponent** uses two speeches [1].
3. **Every new amendment** gives two more speeches each [1].
4. **When speeches run out**, the presiding officer calls the vote.

## Who benefits

Whoever is in the minority likes the filibuster; whoever is in the majority wants it weaker. That flips with power. More: [how each party's view shifts with power](/blog/who-benefits-from-filibuster/).

## Why it failed

| Problem | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| Amendments | Unlimited; each resets speeches [1] |
| Floor time | Housing, permitting, farm bill waiting [1] |
| Votes | Lee needed nearly all 53 Republicans [1] |
| Precedent | Thune: no modern example of success [2] |

## Limits

This isn't a "trust me" thing. This is "verify me."

- Democrats could have offered amendments that reshaped the bill; a former McConnell aide warned it "could end up as the SAVE Ukraine Act after amendment" [1].
- Senators must be present for quorum calls, which wears on the majority too.
- It doesn't lower the 60-vote threshold for cloture [2].
