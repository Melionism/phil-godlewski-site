All right, guys, here's the question I get after every creepy ad: "Is my phone listening to me?" Are voice assistants recording? They're always listening for their wake word, like "Hey Siri" or "Alexa," and once they hear it, they record and send your request to the company. The problem comes when they wake up by mistake. In a US class action, Lopez v. Apple, plaintiffs alleged Siri recorded conversations after accidental activations; Apple denied wrongdoing and agreed to a $95 million settlement [1]. And in 2023 the FTC alleged Amazon kept children's Alexa recordings indefinitely and didn't fully delete them on request; Amazon agreed to a $25 million penalty under a proposed order [2].

Here's what assistants do, what happens with calls, the "phone is listening" theory and how to limit recordings.

**Quick answer**

- Are voice assistants recording: after the wake word, yes; sometimes by accident.
- Is my phone listening for ads: no solid evidence of constant secret recording for ads; other data explains most targeting.
- Are phone calls recorded: not by default by your carrier; apps or the other person may record.
- Limit it: review and delete recordings, turn on auto-delete, turn off "Hey" activation.

## What do assistants actually do?

| Step | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| Waiting | Device listens locally for the wake word |
| Wake word heard | Recording starts |
| Request sent | Audio goes to company servers |
| Stored | Recording may be kept, depending on settings [2] |
| Accidental wake | Unintended audio may be recorded [1] |

Microphone permission is one of the big ones; see [a quick look at your phone](/blog/dangerous-app-permissions/).

## What about calls?

Regular phone calls aren't recorded by your carrier by default. But the person you call might record, a business might ("this call may be recorded"), and some apps record calls. US recording laws vary by state: some require only one party's consent, others require everyone's.

## Is the phone listening for ads?

I get it, I get it. You talked about a product and then saw an ad. That's spooky. In the Siri case, plaintiffs described exactly that kind of experience [1]. But a lot of "listening" ads are explained by other things: your location, searches by people on the same Wi-Fi, purchase data and data brokers. Constant secret recording would drain batteries and show up in network traffic. The more realistic risk is accidental activations and stored recordings [1][2].

## How to limit recordings

This isn't a "trust me" thing. This is "verify me."

1. **Review your history:** Amazon, Google and Apple let you see and delete voice recordings.
2. **Turn on auto-delete** where available.
3. **Turn off "Hey" activation** and use a button press instead.
4. **Use the mute button** on smart speakers for private conversations.
5. **Check microphone permissions** for apps.
6. **Opt out of human review** of recordings where settings allow.
