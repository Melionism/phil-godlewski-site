All right, guys, is the dollar finished? Let's look at the dollar reserve currency status with real numbers. In the second quarter of 2026, the dollar made up 56.70% of the world's allocated official reserves, down from 57.18% the quarter before [1]. The euro was 20.60% and China's renminbi 2.11% [1]. The dollar is also on one side of about 88% of global currency trades [2]. That's slow erosion from a 72% peak in 2001 [2], not a collapse.

Here's what reserve status means, dedollarization data, BRICS alternatives, the national debt and links to gold and the Fed.

**Quick answer**

- US dollar reserve currency: 56.70% of reserves in Q2 2026 [1].
- Why is the dollar the reserve currency: deep markets, trade, trust in US law [2].
- World reserve currency trend: slow decline over 25 years [2].
- Weak spots: debt, politics, sanctions, rival payment systems.

## What does reserve status mean?

Central banks hold savings in a currency they trust and can use. For most, that's the dollar. It ties to [the core functions of money](/blog/money/).
