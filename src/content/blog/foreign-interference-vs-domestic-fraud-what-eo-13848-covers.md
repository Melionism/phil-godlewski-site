All right, guys, a lot of people expected EO 13848 to deal with everything they believed went wrong in 2020, including things done by Americans inside the U.S. But the order has a foreign element built into every key definition. "Foreign interference" means "covert, fraudulent, deceptive, or unlawful actions or attempted actions of a foreign government, or of any person acting as an agent of or on behalf of a foreign government" aimed at influencing or altering an election or undermining public confidence in it [1]. And Section 2 sanctions "foreign persons" [1]. So a purely domestic act, such as an American illegally filling out someone else's ballot with no foreign link, is outside the order. Those cases go to other laws: state election codes, federal voting crimes, and campaign finance law, which already bans foreign nationals from contributing to U.S. elections [2].

Here's the foreign element, purely domestic cases, other laws and why it matters.

**Quick answer**

- Foreign interference vs domestic election fraud: EO 13848 covers only interference by foreign governments or their agents [1].
- Domestic fraud: prosecuted under state and federal criminal law, in court.
- Overlap: Americans acting for a foreign government, or dealing with blocked persons [1].
- Foreign money: already banned by 52 U.S.C. 30121 [2].

## The foreign element

| Question | Needed for EO 13848? |
| --- | --- |
| Foreign government involved? | Yes [1] |
| Or an agent acting for one? | Yes [1] |
| Covert, fraudulent, deceptive or unlawful act? | Yes [1] |
| Target is a foreign person? | Yes, for Sec. 2 [1] |

## Purely domestic cases

- Double voting.
- Ballot tampering by a local official.
- Registration fraud by a canvasser.

None of these involves a foreign government. They're handled by prosecutors, election boards and courts.

## Other laws

1. **State election codes**: most election crimes.
2. **Federal voting crimes**: e.g., in the National Voter Registration Act and Title 52.
3. **Campaign finance**: foreign nationals can't make contributions or expenditures in U.S. elections [2].
4. **Foreign agents**: the Foreign Agents Registration Act.

## Why it matters

This isn't a "trust me" thing. This is "verify me."

If a claim is about domestic fraud, EO 13848 isn't the tool, and waiting for it won't produce results. If a foreign link exists, the order can reach the foreign side, and Americans who dealt with blocked persons can face penalties. ## FAQ

**Does EO 13848 cover domestic election fraud?** No; it requires a foreign government or its agent [1].

**Who handles domestic fraud?** State and federal prosecutors.

**What if an American acted for a foreign government?** That can fall within the definition [1].

**Can foreigners donate to campaigns?** No [2].

**Why does the distinction matter?** It tells you which law and process to look at.
