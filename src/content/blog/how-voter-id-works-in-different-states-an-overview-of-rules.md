All right, guys, "voter ID" doesn't mean the same thing in every state. According to the National Conference of State Legislatures, 36 states have laws requesting or requiring identification at the polls; the other 14, including California, New York and Pennsylvania, generally verify identity in other ways, like signatures [1]. Within those 36, rules vary. In some states, a voter without ID can still cast a regular ballot after signing an affidavit or having officials confirm identity; NCSL's state entries show that in places like Michigan, Idaho, South Dakota and Virginia [1]. In most of the rest, a voter without ID casts a provisional ballot and must come back with ID by a deadline, which ranges from the same day in Missouri to the Friday after the election in Nebraska and Wisconsin [1]. Lists of accepted IDs change too; Florida's list changes on January 1, 2027 [1]. Before you vote, check your own state's rules.

Here's strict vs non-strict, accepted IDs, what if you lack one, and changes.

**Quick answer**

- Voter ID laws by state: 36 states have them [1].
- Non-strict: you can vote a regular ballot without ID after an affidavit or other step [1].
- Strict: provisional ballot, then show ID by a deadline [1].
- Changes: lists of accepted IDs keep changing; Florida's changes in 2027 [1].

## Accepted IDs

- Driver's license or state ID.
- U.S. passport.
- Military ID.
- Tribal ID.
- In some states: student IDs, public assistance cards, Medicare cards, on set dates [1].

## What if you lack one

This isn't a "trust me" thing. This is "verify me."

1. **Ask for a provisional ballot.**
2. **Bring ID by the deadline**, often a few days after the election [1].
3. **Use exemptions** where they exist, like religious objections or indigency in some states [1].

## FAQ

**How many states have voter ID laws?** 36 [1].

**What's a strict voter ID law?** Without ID, your ballot counts only if you show ID later [1].

**What IDs are accepted?** Usually license, passport, military or tribal ID [1].

**What if I don't have ID?** Ask for a provisional ballot [1].

**Do rules change?** Yes; check before each election [1].
