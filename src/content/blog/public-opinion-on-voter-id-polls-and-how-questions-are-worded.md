All right, guys, voter ID is one of the few things most Americans agree on. In a March 2026 CBS News poll, 80% supported requiring a valid photo ID to vote, including 95% of Republicans, 79% of independents and 65% of Democrats [1]. Support for proof of citizenship to register was lower but still a majority, at 66% [1]. A Gallup survey in October 2024 found similar numbers: 84% backed photo ID and 83% backed proof of citizenship for first-time registrants, and 76% also backed early voting [2]. So why does the fight continue? Partly because polls ask about the principle, while bills come with details: which documents count, what happens to people without them, how much it costs. A question like "Should voters show ID?" gets a different answer from "Should a citizen who can't find a birth certificate be turned away?" Both questions are fair. They measure different things.

Here's what polls show, wording, partisan splits, and how to read them.

**Quick answer**

- Voter ID polls: about 80% support photo ID to vote [1][2].
- Proof of citizenship: 66% in CBS, 83% in Gallup [1][2].
- Partisan split: 95% of Republicans, 65% of Democrats in CBS [1].
- Wording: principle questions get higher support than detailed trade-offs.

## Wording

1. **"Photo ID to vote"** vs **"documents to register"**: different steps.
2. **Mentioning costs or exceptions** lowers support.
3. **"Only citizens should vote"** gets near-universal agreement.
4. **Order of questions** can shift answers.

## How to read them

This isn't a "trust me" thing. This is "verify me."

- Find the exact question.
- Check who was asked: adults, registered voters, likely voters.
- Look at the date.
- Don't read a principle poll as support for a specific bill.
