All right, guys, half the arguments about 2020 come down to three words people use as if they mean the same thing: error, irregularity and fraud. They don't. An error is a mistake, like a clerk entering the wrong number. An irregularity is something that doesn't follow procedure, like a missing signature on a log. Fraud is a crime: someone intentionally casting, changing or counting votes illegally. The FBI lists election crimes such as ballot fraud, voter registration fraud and voting by ineligible people [1], and the Justice Department charges real cases every year [2]. But calling every error "fraud" makes the word useless, and it hides the real crimes.

Here are errors, irregularities, fraud and why the labels matter.

**Quick answer**

- Difference between election error and fraud: intent. Errors are mistakes; fraud is deliberate.
- Election irregularities meaning: departures from procedure that may or may not affect votes.
- What is election fraud: illegal voting, ballot tampering or registration fraud [1].
- Why it matters: errors get corrected; fraud gets prosecuted.

## Errors

Examples: a county uploads unofficial results with a mistake, a scanner jams, a poll worker gives someone the wrong ballot style. Errors are usually caught by canvass, reconciliation or audit. The ladder from suspicion to proof is in [election fraud evidence standards](/blog/counts-as-election-fraud-evidence/).

## Irregularities

| Irregularity | Possible cause | Possible effect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Poll book doesn't match ballot count | Voter left without voting, data entry | Small, explainable |
| Late-arriving mail ballots | Postal delays | Set aside by rules |
| Observer blocked | Space, rules, conflict | Trust issue |
| Missing signature | Voter error | Ballot cured or rejected |

## Fraud

Examples of real fraud: a person voting twice, a noncitizen voting, someone filling in other people's ballots, a campaign operative tampering with absentee ballots. The Justice Department's November 2026 announcement charged 63 people, mostly for noncitizen voting and false citizenship claims [2].

## Why the labels matter

This isn't a "trust me" thing. This is "verify me."

1. **Errors aren't crimes**, but they deserve fixes.
2. **Irregularities** need explanations, not assumptions.
3. **Fraud** needs proof and prosecution.
4. **Scale** decides whether any of it changed a result. See [the state margins that decided 2020](/blog/many-votes-change-2020-election/).
5. **Popular claims** often mix all three. See [ten popular claims checked against the record](/blog/2020-election-myths/).

## FAQ

**What's the difference between election error and fraud?** Intent.

**What are election irregularities?** Departures from procedure.

**Do irregularities mean fraud?** Not by themselves.

**Is election fraud prosecuted?** Yes [2].

**Can an error change a result?** Rarely; audits usually catch it.

## What to do next

1. Label each claim: error, irregularity or fraud.
2. Ask for the explanation officials gave.
3. Check whether anyone was charged.
