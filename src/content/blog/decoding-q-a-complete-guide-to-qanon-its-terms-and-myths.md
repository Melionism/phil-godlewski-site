All right, guys, a lot of people found their way into this community through Q, and a lot of people who hear "QAnon" today have no idea what it actually was. So let's lay it out plainly, separating what's documented from what's interpretation. QAnon began on October 28, 2017, on 4chan's /pol/ board, where an anonymous poster claiming inside government knowledge started a thread called "The Calm Before the Storm" [1][2]. The poster, "Q," said he held "Q clearance," a real Department of Energy level that's the equivalent of Top Secret [2][3]. The posts, called "drops" or "breadcrumbs," moved to 8chan, then to 8kun after 8chan went offline in 2019 [1]. Q went quiet on December 8, 2020, and reappeared on 8kun on June 24, 2022, with a post about playing "the game" again [1]. Who wrote the drops is disputed. In 2022 two separate linguistic teams pointed to South African developer Paul Furber for the early posts and to Ron Watkins from 2018 on; both men deny being Q [4]. On reach: a 2021 PRRI survey classified 14% of Americans as QAnon believers [5].

Here's how it evolved, how big it is, where to check, and terms and ten myths.

**Quick answer**

- QAnon explained: an anonymous poster, "Q," began posting on 4chan on October 28, 2017 [1][2].

- Silence: December 8, 2020, until June 24, 2022 [1].
- Authorship: disputed; linguistic studies point to Furber and Watkins, who deny it [4].

## How big it is

PRRI's March 2021 survey of 5,625 adults asked about three statements [5]:

1. A ring of "Satan-worshipping pedophiles" runs things: 15% agreed [5].
2. "There is a storm coming soon that will sweep away the elites in power and restore the rightful leaders": 20% agreed [5].
3. Patriots "may have to resort to violence": 15% agreed [5].

Combined, PRRI counted 14% as believers, 46% as doubters and 40% as rejecters. Among Republicans, 23% were believers; among Democrats, 7% [5]. What polls measure and where they differ: [how many qanon believers](/blog/many-qanon-believers/).

Now the myths, with what the record shows:

1. **"Q's identity is confirmed."** It isn't. Linguistic studies point to Furber and Watkins; both deny it [4]. Who wrote the q drops: [who wrote the q drops](/blog/who-wrote-q-drops/).
2. **"Q clearance is a military rank."** It's a Department of Energy clearance, the equivalent of Top Secret [3].
3. **"Q started on 8kun."** It started on 4chan in 2017 [1].
4. **"Q never went silent."** There was an 18-month gap from December 2020 [1].
5. **"Most Americans believe it."** PRRI counted 14% believers in 2021 [5].
6. **"Only one party believes it."** Believers existed in both parties, at different rates [5].
7. **"The Storm already happened."** No mass arrests came on January 20, 2021 [6].
8. **"A tripcode proves who's posting."** It proves the same password was used, not who used it.
9. **"It never touched real-world harm."** In 2019 the FBI assessed that such theories likely motivate some domestic extremists [2].
10. **"A decode is the same as the text."** A decode is an interpretation; the drop is the source. The most famous example of a dated drop: [q drop 34](/blog/q-drop-34/).

Allegations against specific people are not established facts unless proven in court.
