All right, guys, if you want to understand the 2020 fight, you have to start with the calendar. A lot of claims fall apart, or make more sense, once you see what happened when. Election Day was November 3, 2020, but because of the pandemic, most Americans didn't vote that day: the Census Bureau found 69% of voters cast ballots by mail or early in person, up from about 40% in 2016 [1]. Counting took days. The Associated Press called the race for Joe Biden on November 7 [2]. Then came recounts, more than 60 lawsuits, the Electoral College vote on December 14, and Congress's count on January 6, 2021 [3][4].

Here are the key dates, why 2020 was unusual, why so many people doubt the result, what came after and the rules that shaped the count.

**Quick answer**

- 2020 election timeline: Nov 3 vote, Nov 7 AP call, Dec 14 electors, Jan 6 count [2][4].
- 2020 election events: recounts in Georgia and Wisconsin, lawsuits in six states, statements from election officials and the attorney general.
- November 2020 to January 6 timeline: about nine weeks from Election Day to the congressional count.
- Final result: Biden 306 electoral votes, Trump 232 [4].

## Why 2020 was unusual

COVID-19 changed how people voted. States expanded mail voting, extended deadlines and added drop boxes, often through emergency orders or court rulings. Read about [2020 election pandemic voting](/blog/2020-election-pandemic-voting/).

## Why many people doubt the result

I get it, I get it. Election night showed Trump ahead in several states, and the lead shrank as mail ballots were counted. Rules changed fast. And the people we trusted kept saying it was stolen. That's why, in December 2020, only about a quarter of Republicans told pollsters they accepted the outcome [7]. Here's [what polling shows about belief in the result](/blog/polls-2020-election-doubts/).

## What came after

1. **Audits and recounts**: Georgia hand-counted every presidential ballot; Arizona's Senate hired Cyber Ninjas.
2. **Lawsuits**: over 60, nearly all lost or dismissed [3].
3. **Defamation suits**: Dominion v. Fox settled for $787.5 million in 2023.
4. **2026**: Trump's July 16 primetime address and document release.

## Rules that shaped the count

This isn't a "trust me" thing. This is "verify me." Many states, including Pennsylvania, Michigan and Wisconsin, didn't let officials start processing mail ballots before Election Day. That's why counts there moved for days. Barr's own words on what his department found are covered in [william barr 2020 election statement](/blog/william-barr-2020-election-statement/).
