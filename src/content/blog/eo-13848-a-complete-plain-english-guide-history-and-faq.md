All right, guys, EO 13848 is one of the most talked-about documents in our community, and most people talking about it have never read it. So let's read it together. Executive Order 13848, "Imposing Certain Sanctions in the Event of Foreign Interference in a United States Election," was signed by President Trump on September 12, 2018, and published in the Federal Register on September 14, 2018 (83 FR 46843) [1]. In plain English, it does three things. It orders the intelligence community to assess foreign interference within 45 days after every federal election. It orders the Attorney General and DHS to report within another 45 days on whether that interference hit election or campaign infrastructure. And it lets Treasury freeze the assets of foreign persons found to have interfered and bar them from entering the U.S. [1]. It's a sanctions tool aimed at foreign actors. It has been renewed every year since 2019, most recently on August 31, 2026 [2].

Here's the full text, the trigger theory and myths, whether it applies to Americans, renewals since 2019, and history, glossary and FAQ.

**Quick answer**

- EO 13848: a 2018 Trump order that sets up assessments of foreign election interference and sanctions on foreign interferers [1].
- Legal basis: IEEPA, the National Emergencies Act, and immigration law section 212(f) [1].
- Who it targets: foreign persons, including people and entities [1].
- Status: national emergency continued through September 2027 by a notice signed August 31, 2026 [2].
- First used: September 30, 2019, against assets tied to Russian financier Yevgeniy Prigozhin [3].

## The full text

The order has 14 sections. These are the ones that matter most.

| Section | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| Sec. 1(a) | DNI assessment within 45 days after an election |
| Sec. 1(b) | AG and DHS report within 45 days of that |
| Sec. 2 | Blocks property of foreign persons who interfered |
| Sec. 3 | State and Treasury may recommend wider sanctions, including on a country's largest companies |
| Sec. 6 | Suspends entry into the U.S. for blocked persons |
| Sec. 8 | Definitions, including "foreign interference" |
| Sec. 9 | No prior notice of a listing |

## The trigger theory and myths

You've heard it: EO 13848 is the "trigger" for arrests, seized ballots or overturned elections. Read the text. The order's tools are assessments, reports, asset blocking and entry bans against foreign persons [1]. It doesn't mention arrests, military tribunals, ballot seizures or re-running an election. ## Does it apply to Americans

Sanctions under Sections 2 and 3 target foreign persons. Americans come in through the prohibitions: a U.S. person can't do business with, donate to, or receive funds from a blocked person [1]. The full breakdown on media, officials, bank accounts and donations: [does eo 13848 apply to us citizens](/blog/eo-13848-apply-us-citizens/).

## Renewals since 2019

A national emergency expires after a year unless the president continues it. Trump renewed it in 2019 and 2020, Biden every year from 2021 to 2024, and Trump again in 2025 and 2026 [2][4]. One detail worth seeing: the 2025 notice repeated the line "there has been no evidence of a foreign power altering the outcomes or vote tabulation in any United States election" [4]. The August 2026 notice doesn't include that sentence [2]. Every notice, year by year: [eo 13848 renewals by year](/blog/eo-13848-renewals-by-year/).

**Glossary**

- **IEEPA**: the 1977 law that lets the president regulate economic transactions in a declared emergency. More: [what is ieepa](/blog/ieepa/).
- **NEA**: the National Emergencies Act, which sets how emergencies are declared and renewed.
- **OFAC**: Treasury's Office of Foreign Assets Control, which runs sanctions.
- **SDN**: the Specially Designated Nationals list, where blocked persons are published.
- **Blocking**: a freeze. The property stays the owner's, but it can't be used or moved.

## FAQ

**What is EO 13848?** A 2018 order on assessing and sanctioning foreign election interference [1].

**Who signed it?** President Trump, on September 12, 2018 [1].

**Is it still in effect?** Yes; it was continued on August 31, 2026 [2].

**Did the 2020 reports find changed votes?** No. DOJ and DHS found no evidence any foreign government-affiliated actor manipulated results [9].

**Can it be used against Americans?** Its sanctions target foreign persons; Americans are barred from dealing with them [1].

**Has it been used?** Yes, from 2019 through 2024 [3][10].
