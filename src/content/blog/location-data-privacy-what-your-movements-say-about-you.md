All right, guys, if someone knew every place you went for a month, what would they know about you? Your home, your job, your church, your doctor, who you visit late at night. That's why location data privacy matters more than almost anything else on your phone. And this isn't theory. In 2024 the FTC barred the data broker X-Mode from selling sensitive location data after alleging it sold precise location tied to advertising IDs, including visits to medical clinics, places of worship and domestic abuse shelters [1]. In 2026 the FTC finalized an order against GM after alleging it sold drivers' location and driving data without consent [2].

Here's what location reveals, who has it, how it's sold and how to limit it.

**Quick answer**

- Who can see my location: your phone maker, apps you allowed, your carrier, and sometimes data brokers [1].
- Location tracking phone: GPS, Wi-Fi, cell towers and Bluetooth.
- How to turn off location tracking: per-app settings, precise location off, location history off.
- Why it matters: location reveals health, religion, relationships [1].

The FTC said X-Mode gave a clinical research company marketing data on people who visited certain medical facilities and then pharmacies or infusion centers in Columbus, Ohio [1]. Start with [a quick look at your phone](/blog/dangerous-app-permissions/).

## Who has it?

1. **Your phone's operating system** (Apple, Google).
2. **Apps** you allowed, and the code inside them [1].
3. **Your carrier**, from cell towers.
4. **Your car**, if it's connected [2].
5. **Data brokers**, who buy it from apps [1].

## How is it sold?

An app includes a software kit from a data company. The kit collects location in the background. The company combines it with your advertising ID and sells it. The FTC said X-Mode didn't have policies to remove sensitive locations from its raw data until May 2023 [1].

## How to limit it

This isn't a "trust me" thing. This is "verify me."

1. **Per app:** set location to "While using" or "Never."
2. **Precise location:** turn it off for weather, shopping and news apps.
3. **Location history:** turn off Google Timeline or review Apple Significant Locations.
4. **Advertising ID:** delete or reset it (Android) or turn off tracking requests (iPhone).
5. **Photos:** stop location tagging in the camera.
6. **Car:** check connected services and opt out of sharing [2].

Other devices share the same problem: see [the listening question](/blog/are-voice-assistants-recording/) and [what smart TVs and speakers collect](/blog/smart-device-privacy/).

## A closer look: "anonymous" location isn't

Companies often say location data is "anonymized." But a phone that sleeps at one address and goes to one office every day is easy to match to a person. That's why the FTC stressed that X-Mode's data was not anonymized and could be tied to individuals [1].

## FAQ

**Who can see my location?** Your phone maker, apps, carrier, and possibly brokers [1].

**How does location tracking work on a phone?** GPS, Wi-Fi, cell towers and Bluetooth.

**How do I turn off location tracking?** Per-app settings, precise location off, history off.

**Is location data really sold?** Yes; the FTC has acted against brokers [1].

**Is "anonymized" location safe?** Often it can be re-identified [1].

## What to do next

1. Review location settings on every app.
2. Turn off precise location where possible.
3. Delete or reset your ad ID.
