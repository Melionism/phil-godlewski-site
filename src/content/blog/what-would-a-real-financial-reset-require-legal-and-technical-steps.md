All right, guys, let's take the reset claim seriously and ask: what would a financial reset require? Four things: legislation, central bank action, new technical systems and international coordination. Each leaves a paper trail. The last time the world reset its money system, 44 nations met at Bretton Woods in July 1944 [1]. In the US today, even a digital dollar would need Congress: the House-passed anti-CBDC bill would require Congress to authorize any government digital dollar [2].

Here's legislation, central bank action, technical systems and international coordination.

**Quick answer**

- How could the financial system be changed: by law, then rules, then systems.
- Steps for a monetary reset: Congress, the Fed, payment rails, other countries.
- Monetary system change requirements: years of public work [1].
- What you'd see: bills, votes, rule proposals, test phases.

## Legislation

Congress would have to pass a law. Example: the GENIUS Act went from bill to Public Law 119-27, signed July 18, 2025 [3]. Compare that with [the date being promoted](/blog/2027-financial-reset/).

| Step | What you'd see |
| --- | --- |
| Bill introduced | A number like H.R. or S. |
| Committee | Hearings, markups |
| Votes | Recorded in both chambers [2] |
| Signature | Public law number [3] |

## Central bank action

The Fed would have to change how it issues money or sets policy. Under the anti-CBDC bill, it couldn't issue a CBDC or use one for monetary policy [2]. Executive order 14178 already bars agencies from promoting one [4].

## Technical systems

Every bank, card network and payment system would need new software. Upgrades like this take years. FedNow alone launched in July 2023 [5], after years of work.

## International coordination

This isn't a "trust me" thing. This is "verify me." The dollar sits at the center of world reserves [6]. Changing that would take other countries, the IMF and treaties. Bretton Woods needed 44 nations and still took until 1958 to work fully [1]. See [the slow pace of reform from Bretton Woods to Dodd-Frank](/blog/why-financial-reforms-take-years/) and [new rules with confirmed dates](/blog/financial-regulation-changes-2027/).
