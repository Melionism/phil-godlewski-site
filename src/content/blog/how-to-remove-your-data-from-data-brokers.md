All right, guys, here's the practical part. How to remove yourself from data brokers: find where you're listed, opt out site by site or through a state tool, decide whether a paid service is worth it, and check back, because listings come back. It's tedious, but it works. And if you're a California resident, it just got much easier: the state's Delete Request and Opt-out Platform (DROP) went live January 1, 2026, and one request reaches more than 500 registered brokers [1]. Why bother? The FTC found brokers build detailed profiles "largely without consumers' knowledge" [2].

Here's how to find the listings, the opt-out steps, paid services and keeping it removed.

**Quick answer**

- Remove personal information from internet: start with people-search sites and data brokers.
- Opt out data brokers: each broker has its own form; some states have one central tool [1].
- Data broker removal services: save time, but check what they cover.
- Keep it removed: recheck every few months.

## Find the listings

1. **Search your full name plus your city** in a private browser window.
2. **Search your phone number and email.**
3. **Note every people-search site** that shows your address, relatives or age.
4. **Check state broker registries** (California, Vermont, Texas and Oregon keep lists).

Background first: [how your profile is bought and sold](/blog/data-brokers/).

## Opt-out steps

| Step | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | Find the site's "opt out" or "do not sell" page |
| 2 | Search for your listing and copy its URL |
| 3 | Submit the form; use a separate email if you can |
| 4 | Confirm through the email link |
| 5 | Screenshot the confirmation |
| 6 | Recheck in 30-60 days |

Tip: give brokers only the information they already have. Don't hand over extra details.

## California's DROP

Run by the California Privacy Protection Agency [1]:

1. **Verify you're a California resident** (through the state's identity gateway or Login.gov) [1].
2. **Submit one deletion request** [1].
3. **Brokers must start processing by August 1, 2026**, then delete within 90 days and keep checking on a 45-day cycle [1].

## Paid services

They send opt-outs for you and recheck. Before paying, ask:

1. Which brokers do they cover?
2. How often do they recheck?
3. What do they do with your data?
4. Can you cancel easily?

I get it, I get it. Paying feels easier. Sometimes it is. But nobody can remove everything, and anyone promising "total erasure" is overselling.

## Keeping it removed

This isn't a "trust me" thing. This is "verify me."

1. **Recheck** every three to six months.
2. **Cut new data at the source:** limit app tracking and loyalty sign-ups.
3. **Send formal deletion requests** where the law allows: [templates for asking companies to erase your data](/blog/request-deletion-personal-data/).
4. **Know your rights:** [consumer rights under the CCPA and GDPR](/blog/gdpr-us-state-privacy-laws/).

## FAQ

**How do I remove my personal information from the internet?** Opt out of people-search sites and brokers one by one, or through a state tool [1].

**How do I opt out of data brokers?** Use each broker's form, or California's DROP if eligible [1].

**Are data broker removal services worth it?** They save time; check coverage and price.

**Will my data come back?** Often; recheck regularly.

**Why do brokers have my data?** They collect from many sources without asking [2].
