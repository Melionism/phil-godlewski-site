All right, guys, this is the question that went from the fringe to Congress. Who was running the Biden administration? On June 4, 2025, a presidential memorandum said "former President Biden's aides abused the power of Presidential signatures through the use of an autopen" [1]. In October 2025, the House Oversight Committee released "The Biden Autopen Presidency: Decline, Delusion, and Deception in the White House" [2].

That's the fair-question side. The body-double side says someone else was playing Biden [3]. Here's how presidents delegate, what was debated and where the two stories split.

**Quick answer**

- How presidents delegate: through staff and, for disability, the 25th Amendment [4].
- Who ran the White House under Biden: the question in the 2025 memo and the House report [1][2].
- Autopen: the memo says most of Biden's actions were signed with one [1].
- Body double: a separate claim with no document behind it [3].

## How do presidents delegate authority?

Every president relies on staff for most daily work. For the big step, there's the 25th Amendment. Under Section 3, a president can send Congress a written statement that he can't do the job, and the Vice President becomes Acting President until he says otherwise [4].

Biden used it once: on November 19, 2021, he transferred power to Kamala Harris for a routine colonoscopy, for about 85 minutes [5]. For where the replacement rumor began, see [where the fake Biden rumor started](/blog/fake-biden-conspiracy-theory/).

## What about the debates over Biden's age and health?

On February 28, 2024, his physician Dr. Kevin O'Connor wrote that Biden, then 81, "remains fit to successfully execute the duties of the presidency" [7]. The physical "did not test his mental faculties" [7]. The June 2025 memo later said "For years, President Biden suffered from serious cognitive decline" [1]. ## What is the autopen dispute?

The core question: did Biden personally approve what was signed in his name? Justice Department lawyers concluded in 2005 that a president "may sign a bill within the meaning of Article I, Section 7 by directing a subordinate to affix" his signature [6]. The 2025 memo and the House report ask whether that direction actually came from Biden [1][2].

Chairman James Comer said "every pardon and executive order should be null and void" and called it "a massive cover-up" [2]. Read the full [biden autopen controversy](/blog/biden-autopen-controversy/).
