All right, guys, here's the part people forget. Aladdin trading and compliance tools sit right next to the risk numbers. BlackRock's 10-K says Aladdin provides "risk analytics, portfolio management, trade execution, and investment operations" [1]. Central Banking's review lists compliance alongside positions, exposures and risk [2].

So Aladdin handles a big slice of the daily work: placing orders, checking limits before and after a trade, and keeping records. People still make the calls. Here's how each step works.

**Quick answer**

- Aladdin order management: build and send orders [1].
- Pre-trade limits: rule checks before an order goes out [2].
- Post-trade checks: making sure the portfolio still fits the rules [2].
- People: managers decide, traders execute, compliance officers review.

## How does order management work?

A portfolio manager decides to buy or sell. The order goes into the system, which tracks it from idea to execution. That's the Aladdin trade workflow in short. See [the metrics and factors Aladdin uses](/blog/aladdin-risk-management/).

| Step | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| Idea | Manager decides to change a position |
| Order | Order is created in the system |
| Pre-trade check | Rules are tested before sending |
| Execution | Trader works the order with brokers |
| Post-trade check | Portfolio is checked again |

## What are pre-trade limits?

Rules a fund must follow, built into the system: maximum per company, per sector, minimum credit rating, banned names. Aladdin compliance checks flag an order that would break one before it goes out [2]. I need you to see why that matters: it stops mistakes before they cost money.

## What are post-trade checks?

After the trade, the system checks the whole portfolio again. Prices move, so a fund can drift past a limit without buying anything. Daily compliance views catch that [2].

## Where do people stay involved?

This isn't a "trust me" thing. This is "verify me." People:

1. **Decide** what to buy and sell.
2. **Set the rules** the system checks.
3. **Override or escalate** when a check fires.
4. **Answer** to clients and regulators.
