All right, guys, this was the fight inside the Republican Party in 2026, and it decided the fate of the SAVE America Act. Trump wanted the Senate's 60-vote rule gone so the bill could pass with a simple majority. On May 5, he said he was "disappointed" in Majority Leader John Thune, whom he said he likes "a lot," and blamed "a couple of Republicans that are foolish people" [1]. Thune told him, privately and publicly, that Republicans didn't have the 51 votes needed to change the rule [1]. Earlier, in March, Thune also turned down Sen. Mike Lee's full talking filibuster, saying critics were "over-promising and creating false expectations" [2]. In June, Trump told Thune to "immediately fire the parliamentarian"; Thune replied that firing her wouldn't help because Republicans "don't have the votes" [3]. An attempt to attach the bill to a reconciliation package failed 48-50 on April 23, and a standalone photo-ID bill failed a procedural vote 52-46 on August 8 [4][5].

Here are Trump's arguments, whether reconciliation could work, Thune's position and the spring debate, and what came next.

**Quick answer**

- Trump Thune filibuster: Trump pushed to end the 60-vote rule; Thune said he lacked 51 votes [1].
- Talking filibuster: Thune rejected the full version [2].
- Parliamentarian: Trump wanted her fired; Thune declined [3].
- Votes: reconciliation amendment 48-50; photo-ID bill 52-46 [4][5].

## Could reconciliation work

Reconciliation avoids the filibuster but only for budget provisions. Under reconciliation, provisions must be principally about the budget, not policy [3]. On April 23, an amendment to add it to the party-line immigration enforcement funding bill failed 48-50; Collins, Murkowski, McConnell and Tillis voted no [4].
