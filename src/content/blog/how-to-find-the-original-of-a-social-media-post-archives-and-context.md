All right, guys, most of what we argue about online is a screenshot of a screenshot. Before you decode anything, find the original. Here's a simple process. Start with search: copy a distinctive phrase in quotation marks and search the platform and the web. Then go to the archives. The Internet Archive's Wayback Machine stores snapshots of web pages over time, and you can paste a link to see whether and when it was captured [1]. For Q drops specifically, use the aggregator sites, and remember a research team found they disagree and that 299 tripcode posts were never archived by any of them [2]. Then context: who posted it, on what account, in reply to what. A clip can be real and still be misleading if it's cut from something longer. Finally dates. Platforms show times in your local time zone, so the same post can look like it went up at different hours to different people. A screenshot shows only what the person chose to capture, and it can be edited. If you can't find the original, treat it as unconfirmed, no matter how many people share it.

Here's search, archives, context, and dates.

**Quick answer**

- Archives: Wayback Machine snapshots; Q aggregators for drops [1][2].
