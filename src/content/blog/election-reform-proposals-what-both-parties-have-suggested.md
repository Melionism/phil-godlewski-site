All right, guys, here's something that might surprise you: Democrats and Republicans have actually passed an election reform together since 2020. In December 2022, Congress passed the Electoral Count Reform Act with bipartisan support. It clarified that the vice president's role in counting electoral votes is only ministerial, raised the threshold for members of Congress to object to a state's electors to one-fifth of each chamber, and set rules so each state's governor certifies a single slate of electors [1]. Beyond that, the parties split. Republicans push for voter ID and proof of citizenship, as in the SAVE America Act, which needs 60 Senate votes and has stalled [2]. Democrats push for expanded access and federal standards. Both sides talk about security funding.

Here's the Electoral Count Reform Act, security funding, access and ID debates, and what's stalled.

**Quick answer**

- Bipartisan election reform proposals: the Electoral Count Reform Act passed in 2022 [1].
- Electoral Count Reform Act: VP role is ministerial; objections need one-fifth of each chamber [1].
- Election security reform: grants for equipment and paper ballots have had support on both sides.
- Stalled: SAVE America Act and Democratic voting-rights bills [2].

## The Electoral Count Reform Act

| Change | Before | After [1] |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Vice president's role | Unclear in old 1887 law | Ministerial only |
| Objection threshold | One member per chamber | One-fifth of each chamber |
| State certification | Room for competing slates | Governor certifies one slate |
| Court review | Limited | Expedited review of certification disputes |

These changes answer problems both sides saw on January 6, 2021. For more, see [what both parties agree needs fixing](/blog/real-problems-us-elections/).

## Security funding

Congress has sent grants to states through the Election Assistance Commission for equipment, cybersecurity and audits. Support has been broad, though the amounts are debated every budget cycle.

## Access and ID debates

| Proposal | Supporters say | Critics say |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Photo ID to vote | Confirms identity | Some lack ID |
| Proof of citizenship to register | Keeps noncitizens off rolls | Millions lack ready documents |
| Mail voting limits | Reduces risk | Hurts access |
| Same-day registration | Expands access | Harder to verify |

## What's stalled

This isn't a "trust me" thing. This is "verify me." The SAVE America Act needs 60 Senate votes; Republicans hold 53 seats, and Sen. Thom Tillis called the bills "fundamentally flawed and impossible to implement by this election" [2]. Democratic bills on voting access have also stalled. After the November 2026 midterms, the math in Congress changes again, so watch for new proposals.

## FAQ

**What is the Electoral Count Reform Act?** A 2022 law clarifying how Congress counts electoral votes [1].

**Did both parties support it?** Yes, it passed with bipartisan votes.

**What is the SAVE America Act?** A Republican bill requiring proof of citizenship and ID [2].

**Why is it stalled?** It lacks 60 Senate votes [2].

**What do both sides agree on?** Security funding and clearer counting rules.
