All right, guys, can a president order the Justice Department to prosecute someone? Legally, the attorney general and the FBI director work for the president, who heads the executive branch. The Supreme Court's 2024 immunity decision even described the president's discussions with the Justice Department as core official conduct [1]. But since the Watergate era, presidents of both parties have followed a tradition of keeping the White House out of specific criminal cases, and courts, grand juries and judges still stand between any request and a conviction. In July 2026, President Trump asked the FBI director to ensure matters were "fully investigated" and to work with the Justice Department "to prosecute those responsible for any crimes" [2].

Here's the tradition, Kash Patel and the FBI, grand juries and what the law says.

**Quick answer**

- Can a president order prosecutions: he can set priorities and direct investigations; charges still need evidence, a grand jury and a judge.
- DOJ independence from president: a tradition, not a statute.
- President direct Justice Department: legally allowed in general; specific cases are politically sensitive [1].
- Unitary executive DOJ: the view that the president controls all executive agencies.

## What law says

This isn't a "trust me" thing. This is "verify me."

1. **The president heads the executive branch.**
2. **Prosecutors have ethical duties** to charge only with probable cause.
3. **Grand juries** must find probable cause.
4. **Judges** can dismiss or block, as one did with a 2026 subpoena.
5. **Referrals don't bind anyone**: [why a referral isn't a charge](/blog/criminal-referral/).

## FAQ

**Can a president order a prosecution?** He can direct priorities; courts and grand juries decide outcomes.

**Is DOJ independence a law?** It's mostly tradition and policy.

**What did the 2024 ruling say?** President–DOJ discussions are core official acts [1].

**What did Trump ask in 2026?** Investigation and prosecution of "any crimes" [2].

**Who checks the DOJ?** Grand juries, judges and Congress.
