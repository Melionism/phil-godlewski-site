All right, guys, there are two ways to get privacy wrong. One is ignoring it. The other is panicking about everything at once. Real vs exaggerated privacy risks is about putting your time where the danger actually is. And the data is clear about where that is: ordinary scams. Phishing and spoofing were the most common complaint to the FBI's Internet Crime Complaint Center in 2025, with 191,561 reports, and total reported losses hit $20.9 billion [1]. The FTC reported $15.9 billion in fraud losses in 2025, and text messages were the most common way scammers reached people [2].

Here are the frequent risks, the rare scenarios, the exaggerated claims and how to prioritize.

**Quick answer**

- Biggest privacy risks: phishing, scam texts, reused passwords, account takeover [1][2].
- Which privacy risks matter: the common ones you can fix cheaply.
- Common online privacy risks: data breaches, oversharing, location tracking.
- Exaggerated: "quantum will expose everything," "your phone hears every word."

## Frequent risks

| Risk | Why it's common | Fix |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Phishing emails and texts | Cheap to send at scale [1][2] | Don't click; go to the site yourself |
| Reused passwords | One leak unlocks many accounts | Password manager |
| Account takeover | Leaked passwords plus no second factor | Two-factor login [3] |
| Oversharing | Birthdays, pets, schools = security answers | Lock down profiles |
| Data brokers | Profiles sold openly | Opt out |

Start with [building a personal threat model](/blog/threat-modeling-ordinary-people/).

## Rare scenarios

These are real but unlikely for most people:

1. **Targeted state hacking.** Mostly hits journalists, activists and officials.
2. **Sophisticated spyware.** Expensive; usually aimed at specific people.
3. **Physical surveillance.** Usually tied to a specific person in your life.

If you're in one of those groups, your plan needs more. If not, don't start there.

## Exaggerated claims

| Claim | Reality |
| --- | --- |
| "Quantum computers will expose all secrets soon" | NIST published post-quantum standards in 2024; migration is underway [4] |
| "Your phone records every word for ads" | Not proven; location and browsing explain most targeting |
| "Hackers have your webcam video" (scam email) | Usually a mass scam using old leaked passwords |
| "Only an expensive product can protect you" | Most protections are free settings |

## How to prioritize

This isn't a "trust me" thing. This is "verify me." Score each risk two ways: how likely, and how bad. Fix the likely ones first, even if they feel boring.

1. **This week:** email password, two-factor login, phone lock.
2. **This month:** password manager, app permissions, data broker opt-outs.
3. **This year:** credit freeze, old account cleanup, family plan.

If something still keeps you up at night, it's worth understanding why: [why the fear of secrets coming out is so strong](/blog/fear-exposure-psychology/).
