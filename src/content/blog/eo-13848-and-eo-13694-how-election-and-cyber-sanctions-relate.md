All right, guys, EO 13848 wasn't the first order used against election meddling. That was EO 13694. President Obama signed EO 13694 on April 1, 2015, to sanction "significant malicious cyber-enabled activities." On December 29, 2016, he amended it with EO 13757 to cover anyone who tampers with or alters information "with the purpose or effect of interfering with or undermining election processes or institutions," and Treasury listed Russia's GRU and FSB, four GRU officials and several companies the same day [1]. In 2018 Trump signed EO 13848, which covers election interference more broadly, covering propaganda and disinformation as well as hacking. Section 2(b) of 13848 says 13694 stays in force and can be used alongside it [2]. That's why Treasury press releases often cite both.

Here's EO 13694, the overlap, the differences and example designations.

**Quick answer**

- EO 13694 vs EO 13848: a 2015 cyber order versus a 2018 election order [1][2].
- Overlap: election-related hacking can fall under both [1][2].
- Difference: 13848 also covers covert influence and requires post-election assessments [2].
- Example: the Center for Geopolitical Expertise was designated under both in 2024 [3].

## EO 13694

- **Signed:** April 1, 2015 [1].
- **Target:** malicious cyber activity from abroad that threatens U.S. security or the economy.
- **Amended:** December 29, 2016, to add election-related tampering [1].
- **First election use:** GRU and FSB, December 2016 [1].

Each section of the 2018 order: [each section of the order explained](/blog/eo-13848-full-text/).

## Overlap

| Activity | EO 13694 | EO 13848 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hacking election systems | Yes [1] | Yes [2] |
| Leaking stolen campaign data | Yes, if tampering or misappropriation [1] | Yes [2] |
| Covert propaganda, fake personas | Not as such | Yes [2] |
| Post-election assessments | No | Yes [2] |

## Differences

1. **Trigger**: 13694 is about cyber activity; 13848 covers any kind of foreign interference in elections.
2. **Process**: 13848 has the DNI and AG-DHS reviews.
3. **Wider sanctions**: 13848's Section 3 allows recommendations against a country's largest firms [2].

## FAQ

**What is EO 13694?** Obama's 2015 cyber sanctions order [1].

**Was it used for elections?** Yes, after the December 2016 amendment [1].

**Did 13848 replace it?** No; both remain [2].

**Why cite both?** Some conduct fits both.

**What does only 13848 have?** The post-election assessments [2].
