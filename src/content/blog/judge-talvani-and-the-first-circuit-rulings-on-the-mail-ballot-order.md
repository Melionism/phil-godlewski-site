All right, guys, a lot of people search for "Judge Talvani," but her name is spelled Indira Talwani. She's the federal judge in Massachusetts who handled the challenges to Trump's March 2026 election order, EO 14399. On June 25, 2026, she issued an injunction blocking key parts of the order in 23 states while the case continues [1]. She found that the President lacked authority to direct the Department of Homeland Security to compile voter eligibility lists, and that the Postal Service lacked statutory power to issue binding mail voting rules [1]. The Justice Department argued the suit was premature, since agencies hadn't finalized anything yet. A First Circuit panel rejected that 2-1, writing that "the Plaintiff States have no practical choice but to respond to the (order) now," given the deadlines and the need to prepare for the September and November elections [1]. The Supreme Court later stayed her injunction on ripeness grounds in August, without ruling on legality, and she then lifted a related injunction in the League of Women Voters case [2][3].

Here's Talwani's ruling, the appeal, the grounds, and later status.

**Quick answer**

- Talvani First Circuit mail ballot ruling: Judge Indira Talwani blocked key parts of EO 14399 on June 25, 2026 [1].
- Grounds: no presidential authority for DHS voter lists; no USPS power for binding mail rules [1].
- First Circuit: refused to lift it, 2-1 [1].
- Supreme Court: stayed it in August on ripeness, not legality [2].

## The appeal

The Justice Department asked the First Circuit to lift the injunction, arguing ripeness. The panel majority said no [1].

## Grounds

1. **Separation of powers**: elections are for states and Congress.
2. **Statutory limits**: the Postal Service has no authority to set voting rules [1].
3. **Timing**: states had to act before the elections [1].
