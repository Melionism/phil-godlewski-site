All right, guys, here's how Fed interest rates affect the economy and you. The Fed's rate committee, the FOMC, sets a target for the federal funds rate: what banks charge each other overnight [1]. That rate ripples out to credit cards, car loans, mortgages and savings accounts. In 2007-08 the Fed cut it from 5.25% to near zero to fight the crisis [2]. Higher rates cool inflation; lower rates support jobs.

Here's how rate decisions travel, inflation and purchasing power, loans and savings, and jobs and growth.

**Quick answer**

- Federal funds rate explained: the Fed's main policy rate [1].
- How do interest rates affect you: loan costs and savings yields.
- Fed rate decisions impact: borrowing, spending, jobs, inflation.
- Example: 5.25% to near zero in 2007-08 [2].

## How do rate decisions travel?

| Step | What changes |
| --- | --- |
| FOMC sets the target | Overnight bank rate [1] |
| Banks adjust | Prime rate, card APRs |
| Markets adjust | Bond yields, mortgage rates |
| You feel it | Loan payments, savings interest |

## Loans and savings

1. **Credit cards:** variable rates follow the Fed quickly.
2. **Mortgages:** follow long-term bond yields more than the Fed rate.
3. **Savings:** high-yield accounts rise and fall with the Fed rate.

## Jobs and growth

This isn't a "trust me" thing. This is "verify me." When rates rise, borrowing gets expensive and hiring slows. When rates fall, the reverse. That's why rate cuts often come in a downturn [2]. If you're worried about a slowdown, read [safe haven assets](/blog/safe-haven-assets/).

## FAQ

**What is the federal funds rate, explained?** The overnight rate banks charge each other [1].

**How do interest rates affect you?** Loan costs and savings yields.

**What is the impact of Fed rate decisions?** On borrowing, spending, jobs and inflation.

**Do mortgage rates follow the Fed?** Loosely; they follow long-term yields.

**Why does the Fed cut rates in a crisis?** To support jobs and lending [2].
