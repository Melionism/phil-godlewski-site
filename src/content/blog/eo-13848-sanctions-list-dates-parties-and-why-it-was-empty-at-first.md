All right, guys, people talk about EO 13848 like it's sitting unused, waiting for a big day. It isn't. It's been used many times, on foreign targets, and the record is public. For the first year it was empty: a Treasury report covering September 2018 to March 2019 said, "No entities or individuals have been designated pursuant to E.O. 13848" [1]. The first designations came on September 30, 2019, against assets tied to Russian financier Yevgeniy Prigozhin, who funded the Internet Research Agency [2]. After that came Ukrainian lawmaker Andrii Derkach and Internet Research Agency staff in 2020, Iran's Revolutionary Guard and its media fronts in October 2020, Derkach's network in January 2021, 29 Russia-linked people and entities in April 2021, an Iranian cyber firm in November 2021, and Iranian and Russian targets over the 2024 election [3][4][5][6][7][8][9]. Every one is a foreign person or entity.

Here's the December 2024 action, getting on and off the list, OFAC's role, asset freezes, and why it was empty in 2019.

**Quick answer**

- EO 13848 sanctions list: designations from 2019 to 2024, all foreign [2][9].
- First use: September 30, 2019, Prigozhin-linked assets [2].
- Biggest single action: 29 persons and entities, April 15, 2021 [6].
- Latest major action: December 31, 2024, an IRGC unit and a GRU-linked Moscow center [9].
- Where to check: OFAC's Sanctions List Search, program tag ELECTION-EO13848 [6].

## Why it was empty in 2019

This isn't a "trust me" thing. This is "verify me."

1. **The process takes time**: assessment, report, then designations [10].
2. **2018 findings**: DOJ and DHS found no evidence a foreign government had a material impact on the integrity or security of the 2018 midterms [1].
3. **Influence cases** came later, starting September 2019 [2].
