All right, guys, I want to give you the best case on both sides of the SAVE America Act, because you should be able to argue either one. Supporters' core point is simple: only citizens may vote in federal elections, and right now most states let people register by checking a box swearing they're citizens. The bill would require documents instead [1]. They point to public opinion: in a March 2026 CBS poll, 80% of Americans supported photo ID to vote, including 65% of Democrats, and 66% supported proof of citizenship to register [2]. Opponents' core point: the problem is tiny and the cost is big. A 2023 University of Maryland survey estimated about 21.3 million adult citizens, roughly 9%, lack easy access to a passport, birth certificate or naturalization papers [3]. Kansas tried a similar rule from 2013 to 2016; more than 30,000 people were blocked or suspended, while only 39 noncitizens were confirmed registered from 1999 to 2013 [4].

Here's the case for, penalties for election workers, the case against, and what the evidence shows.

**Quick answer**

- SAVE America Act pros and cons: election security and public support vs. access and cost.
- For: documents instead of a checkbox; 80% back photo ID [1][2].
- Against: ~21.3 million citizens lack easy access to documents [3].
- Evidence: noncitizen registration is rare; Kansas's version blocked 30,000+ [4].

## The case for

1. **Citizenship is required**, so check it at the front door.
2. **Attestation alone** relies on the honor system.
3. **Photo ID is popular** across parties [2].
4. **Clean rolls**: checks against DHS's SAVE database [1].
5. **National standard** instead of 50 different rules.

What the bill actually requires: [save america act requirements](/blog/save-america-act-requirements/).

## Penalties for election workers

The bill would make it a crime for an official to register an applicant without the required proof, even if the person is a citizen, and lets individuals sue officials [1][5]. The text and the criticism: [the criminal liability in the bill's text](/blog/save-act-penalties-election-workers/).

## The case against

1. **Documents**: millions lack easy access [3].
2. **Name changes**: married women whose names differ from birth records [3].
3. **Mail and online registration** effectively end; you'd register in person [6].
4. **Cost**: no new funding; Orange County alone estimated $6 million [6].
5. **Officials' liability** could make workers reject borderline applicants.
