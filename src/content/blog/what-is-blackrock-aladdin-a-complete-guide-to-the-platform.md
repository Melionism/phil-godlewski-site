All right, guys, let's do the big one. What is BlackRock Aladdin? It's BlackRock's own software platform for investment and risk management. The company's annual report calls it "an end-to-end, SaaS solution for investment and risk management," used by BlackRock and by "a growing number of institutional and retail investors around the world" [1]. The name stands for Asset, Liability, Debt and Derivative Investment Network, and it goes back to 1988 [2].

It's also big money. In 2025, BlackRock's technology services and subscription revenue hit $2.0 billion, up 24%, and Aladdin was the majority of it [3]. Here's how the platform works, what's inside it, who uses it, who competes with it, and where the criticism comes in.

**Quick answer**

- Aladdin BlackRock: an investment and risk management platform [1].
- Started: 1988, as an in-house system [2].
- Revenue: about $2.0 billion from technology services in 2025 [3].
- BlackRock's own AUM: $15.5 trillion at September 30, 2026 [4].

## How does the platform work?

Aladdin system explained in one line: it pulls a firm's holdings, market data and rules into one place, then shows the risk, runs the checks and handles the trades. BlackRock describes it as "a tech platform that unifies the investment management process through a common data language" [5]. Read [how does blackrock aladdin work](/blog/blackrock-aladdin-work/).

## Why is risk management at the core?

Because that's what it was built for. The name itself lists what a big investor must balance: assets, liabilities, debt and derivatives [2]. Tools include value-at-risk, duration, stress tests and scenario analysis; the Bank of Israel uses it for exactly that [6]. See [aladdin risk management](/blog/aladdin-risk-management/).

## Who uses Aladdin?

Asset managers, insurers, pension funds, banks and central banks. In 2017, BlackRock said more than 85 asset-manager clients ran about $18 trillion on it [10]. See [who uses blackrock aladdin](/blog/who-uses-blackrock-aladdin/).

## Who are the main competitors?

BlackRock's 10-K says Aladdin competes with other risk analytics and investment technology providers, and that clients may move to standalone or in-house systems [3]. The big names include MSCI, SimCorp and Charles River. See [blackrock aladdin competitors](/blog/blackrock-aladdin-competitors/).

## What about limits, criticism and related topics?

This isn't a "trust me" thing. This is "verify me." The criticism is real: one system used by so many big investors raises questions about shared risk and oversight. Europe's DORA rules "may potentially subject Aladdin to additional oversight," BlackRock itself says [3].
