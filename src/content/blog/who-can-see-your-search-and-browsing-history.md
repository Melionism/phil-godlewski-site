All right, guys, who can see your browsing history? More people than you'd guess, but not everyone the scary videos say. Your device keeps it. Your internet provider can see which sites you connect to. The websites themselves see what you do there. And your employer or school can see traffic on their networks and devices. Two facts to keep in mind: in 2017 Congress repealed the FCC's broadband privacy rules for internet providers before they took effect [1]. And private browsing modes have limits: in 2024 Google agreed to delete billions of records to settle a lawsuit claiming it tracked people in Incognito mode [2].

Here's your device, your provider, websites and employers, and how to reduce exposure.

**Quick answer**

- Can my ISP see my history: it can see the sites (domains) you connect to, not page contents on secure sites.
- Who can see my search history: your search engine, your account, anyone with your device.
- Browsing history privacy: depends on device, network and account settings.
- Reduce exposure: secure sites, logged-out searching, device locks, and knowing who runs the network.

## Your device

Anyone who can unlock your phone or computer can open your history. Synced accounts mean your history may also be on other devices. Trackers explained here: [the machinery behind targeted ads](/blog/ad-tracking-explained/).

## Your provider

| What your internet provider can see | What it can't see (on HTTPS sites) |
| --- | --- |
| Which sites you connect to | The specific pages and what you type |
| When and how much | Your passwords and messages |
| Your IP address | Content of encrypted apps |

In 2017 Congress used the Congressional Review Act to cancel FCC rules that would have required providers to get permission before using sensitive browsing data [1].

## Websites and employers

1. **Websites** see everything you do on them, and their trackers see more.
2. **Search engines** keep searches tied to your account unless you turn that off.
3. **Employers and schools** can monitor their networks and the devices they issue.
4. **Public Wi-Fi operators** see what your provider would see.

## How to reduce exposure

This isn't a "trust me" thing. This is "verify me."

1. **Turn off search history** in your account settings, or delete it regularly.
2. **Use a separate browser profile** for sensitive searches.
3. **Lock your devices** with a strong PIN.
4. **Know private browsing's limits:** [the limits of private browsing](/blog/incognito-mode-hide/) [2].
5. **Tighten browser settings:** [how to stop your browser from tracking you](/blog/browser-privacy-settings/).
6. **Don't do personal browsing** on work devices.

## FAQ

**Can my ISP see my history?** It can see sites you visit, not page contents on secure sites.

**Who can see my search history?** Your search engine, your account and anyone with your device.

**Can my employer see my browsing?** On their network and devices, often yes.

**Does incognito hide history from my ISP?** No [2].

**Are there federal rules for ISPs on browsing data?** The FCC's 2016 rules were repealed in 2017 [1].

## What to do next

1. Turn off or delete your search history.
2. Lock every device you own.
3. Keep personal browsing off work devices.
