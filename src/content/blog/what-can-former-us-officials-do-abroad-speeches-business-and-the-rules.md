All right, guys, here's a question that comes up every time an ex-official shows up overseas. What are the former US officials foreign travel rules? In short: they can travel, give paid speeches, do business and meet foreign leaders. Three federal laws set the limits: 18 U.S.C. § 207, the Foreign Agents Registration Act and the Logan Act [1][2][3].

So when a former cabinet member meets a foreign leader, the first question isn't "is it secret?" It's "which rule applies?"

**Quick answer**

- Can former officials meet foreign leaders? Yes, as private citizens [1][3].
- One-year bar: senior ex-officials can't represent or advise a foreign government to influence US decisions [1].
- FARA: agents of foreign principals must register and disclose, since 1938 [2].
- Logan Act: two indictments in its history, no convictions [3].

## What about paid speeches and private business?

Those are open to former officials, with timing rules. Under § 207(c), senior ex-officials can't lobby their old agency for 1 year [1]. Under § 207(d), very senior people, like those paid at Executive Schedule Level I, wait 2 years [1].

Real examples are easy to find. Mike Pompeo signed with Fox News as a contributor on April 8, 2021 [4]. John Ratcliffe took a senior advisory role at American Global Strategies in July 2022, and the firm said he could keep "speaking and lecture engagements" [5]. ## Can former officials make party and political trips?

Yes. Ex-officials travel for think tanks, foundations and political events all the time. Pompeo visited Taiwan March 2–5, 2022, at the invitation of the Prospect Foundation, while a Hudson Institute fellow [6]. That trip was public and announced in advance.

## What are the legal restrictions?

Here's the core one. Section 207(f) says that for one year after leaving, a covered ex-official can't knowingly represent a foreign entity, or "aid or advise" it, with intent to influence a US official decision [1]. A "foreign entity" means a foreign government or a foreign political party [1].

FARA adds disclosure. Agents of foreign principals in political work must make "periodic public disclosure of their relationship with the foreign principal" [2]. Ex-officials lobbying foreign governments without registering break that rule.

The Logan Act, from 1799, bars citizens from dealing with foreign governments to defeat US policy "without authority of the United States" [3]. It's a criminal law, but no one has been indicted under it since 1852 [3].

## What do such trips not prove?

I get it, I get it, a meeting abroad looks like a mission. But a trip by itself shows a trip. To show more, you need a document: a FARA filing, an official schedule, a statement from a government [2].

This isn't a "trust me" thing. This is "verify me." Three checks:

1. **Look for a FARA filing** under the person's name [2].
2. **Check the dates** against the § 207 one- and two-year windows [1].
3. **Check who invited them** and whether it was announced.

## FAQ

**What are the former US officials foreign travel rules?** 18 U.S.C. § 207, FARA and the Logan Act set the limits [1][2][3].

**Can former officials meet foreign leaders?** Yes, as private citizens [1][3].

**Can ex-officials lobby foreign governments?** Not for 1 year under § 207(f), and only with FARA registration after [1][2].

**What counts as former officials' foreign activity?** Speeches, business, think tank work, media and political trips [4][5][6].

**Has anyone been convicted under the Logan Act?** No [3].
