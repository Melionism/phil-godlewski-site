All right, guys, let's answer the question straight: what data do tech companies collect? A lot more than most people think, and from places most people never look. Your searches, your location, your purchases, your contacts, how long you look at a post, what device you're on, what you click. And it doesn't stop with the apps you use. Data brokers you've never heard of build profiles too. The FTC studied nine of them and found one held 3,000 data segments on nearly every US consumer [1]. In 2025 the FTC finalized an order against General Motors after alleging it sold drivers' location and driving data without consent [2].

Why do they collect it? Mostly to sell ads, to improve products, and sometimes to sell or share the data with others. Here are data brokers, advertising and tracking, apps and devices, shopping and purchases, and who else collects data.

**Quick answer**

- What data does Google collect (and others): searches, location, device info, activity in apps and websites.
- How tech companies use your data: ads, product design, sometimes selling or sharing.
- Big tech data collection: only part of the picture; data brokers and car makers collect too [1][2].
- What you can do: settings, permissions, opt-outs and your legal rights.

## Data brokers

These are companies that buy, combine and sell information about people. You never signed up with them. The FTC said they collect "largely without consumers' knowledge" [1]. One added three billion new records every month; another had 700 billion data elements [1]. They even create categories like "Diabetes Interest" from what you buy and browse [1]. Read [the firms you've never heard of](/blog/data-brokers/).

## Advertising and tracking

Every ad-supported website and app has trackers: cookies, pixels and device IDs that follow you. In April 2025 Google said Chrome would keep its current third-party cookie approach and wouldn't roll out a new prompt to turn them off [3]. So tracking cookies remain part of the web for now. Read [ad tracking explained](/blog/ad-tracking-explained/).

## Apps and devices

| Source | What it can collect |
| --- | --- |
| Phone apps | Location, contacts, photos, microphone (if allowed) |
| Smart speakers | Voice recordings |
| Smart TVs | What you watch |
| Cars | Location, driving behavior [2][4] |
| Fitness trackers | Health and sleep data |

Mozilla reviewed 25 car brands and every one failed its privacy check; 19 said they can sell personal data [4]. Read [dangerous app permissions](/blog/dangerous-app-permissions/).

## Shopping and purchases

What you buy says a lot: pregnancy, illness, money trouble, religion. Order histories and loyalty programs feed those profiles [1]. Read [what purchase history reveals](/blog/purchase-history-reveals/).

## Who else collects data?

Banks, insurers, employers, schools, health apps, and governments. In a 2023 Pew survey, 71% of Americans said they were concerned about how the government uses their data [5]. For the big picture, see [what data exists about you and who gets it](/blog/digital-privacy/). For the government side, see [mass surveillance snowden](/blog/mass-surveillance-snowden/).

This isn't a "trust me" thing. This is "verify me." Download your own data from Google, Apple or Meta. Most people are surprised by what's there.

## FAQ

**What data do tech companies collect?** Searches, location, device info, activity, contacts and more.

**How do tech companies use your data?** Mostly ads and products; sometimes sharing or selling.

**What data does Google collect?** Search, location, YouTube, app and device activity, depending on settings.

**Who collects data I never agreed to share?** Data brokers [1].

**Do cars collect data?** Yes, a lot [2][4].
