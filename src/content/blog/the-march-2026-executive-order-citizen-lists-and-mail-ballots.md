All right, guys, after Congress didn't pass the SAVE America Act, Trump tried again by order. On March 31, 2026, he signed Executive Order 14399, "Ensuring Citizenship Verification and Integrity in Federal Elections," which he called legally "foolproof" [1]. It directed the Department of Homeland Security and the Social Security Administration to compile lists of citizens for the states, and directed the Postal Service to deliver mail ballots only to voters on a state's participation list [1]. Twenty-three states and D.C. sued, as did the League of Women Voters [2]. On June 25, Judge Indira Talwani in Massachusetts blocked key parts in the plaintiff states; she found the President lacked authority to direct DHS to compile voter eligibility lists and the Postal Service lacked power to issue binding mail voting rules [3]. The First Circuit refused, 2-1, to lift her order [3]. On August 24, the Supreme Court stayed that injunction, finding the states sued too early, while stressing it wasn't ruling on whether the order is lawful [4]. The Postal Service's rule under the order, issued August 21, stayed blocked through Election Day [2][5].

Here's the order, Talwani and the First Circuit, the Supreme Court, the USPS plan, and the new lawsuit.

**Quick answer**

- March 2026 executive order mail ballots: EO 14399, signed March 31, 2026 [1].
- What it did: citizen lists from DHS and SSA; mail ballots only to listed voters [1].
- Courts: Talwani blocked key parts; First Circuit agreed; Supreme Court said too early [3][4].
- USPS rule: blocked through November 3, 2026 [5].

## Talwani and the First Circuit

Judge Talwani ruled June 25; the First Circuit majority wrote that "the Plaintiff States have no practical choice but to respond to the (order) now" [3]. More: [talvani first circuit mail ballot ruling](/blog/talvani-first-circuit-mail-ballot-ruling/).

## The Supreme Court

The Court said the states' injury, measured when they sued, was too speculative, and called the order an internal directive [4]. Three justices dissented [4]. More: [supreme court mail ballot order ripeness](/blog/supreme-court-mail-ballot-order-ripeness/).

## The USPS plan

The Postal Service issued a final rule on August 21 requiring redesigned ballot envelopes and lists of recipients from states [2]. More: [usps mail ballot rule lawsuit](/blog/usps-mail-ballot-rule-lawsuit/).

## New lawsuit

This isn't a "trust me" thing. This is "verify me."

After the rule came out, states and voting groups renewed their challenges; Talwani issued a temporary restraining order on August 27 and a preliminary injunction on September 4; the Supreme Court refused to lift it on September 14 [5].
