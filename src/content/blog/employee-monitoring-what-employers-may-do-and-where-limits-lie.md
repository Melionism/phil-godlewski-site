All right, guys, can employers monitor employees? In the US, mostly yes, on their own systems. Work email, work computers, company networks and company phones are generally fair game, especially when the employer tells you in advance. Some states require that notice. New York, for example, since May 2022 requires private employers to give written notice when they monitor employees' phone, email or internet use, and to post that notice [1]. What employers usually can't do: access your personal accounts without permission, or record in places where you expect privacy.

Here's what's common, personal phones, the legal limits and what to ask at work.

**Quick answer**

- Can employers monitor employees: on work systems, generally yes.
- Workplace surveillance: email, web traffic, keystrokes, screenshots, location on work devices.
- Employee monitoring software: tools that log activity, take screenshots or track productivity.
- Limits: notice laws in some states [1], federal wiretap rules, and personal account protections.

## Legal limits

1. **Notice laws:** New York requires written notice and posting [1]; a few other states have similar rules.
2. **Wiretap laws:** intercepting calls can need consent.
3. **Bathrooms and changing rooms:** off-limits for cameras.
4. **Labor law:** employers shouldn't spy on organizing activity.
5. **Personal accounts:** demanding your passwords is restricted in many states.

## What to ask at work

This isn't a "trust me" thing. This is "verify me."

1. **What's monitored**, and how?
2. **Is there a written policy?**
3. **Does monitoring include my personal phone** if I use work apps?
4. **Who sees the data** and how long is it kept?

And check what apps on your phone can access: [how apps access your phone data](/blog/dangerous-app-permissions/).

## A closer look: the simplest rule

Treat every work device and work account as visible to your employer. Keep personal life, personal banking, medical questions and job searches on your own devices and your own network.
