All right, guys, AI in finance isn't coming. It's already here. Treasury's December 2024 report on artificial intelligence in financial services lists the main uses: fraud detection, credit underwriting, customer service and trading [1]. The same report names the risks: bias, "black box" models nobody can explain, data problems and too much reliance on a few AI providers [1]. That last one matters: respondents warned that "interruption at a single AI provider could create widespread disruptions across the financial system" [1].

Here's where AI is used today, fraud, credit and trading, risks and errors, and what regulators watch.

**Quick answer**

- AI in finance: already used for fraud checks, credit, service and trading [1].
- Artificial intelligence in banking: mostly behind the scenes.
- AI in financial services: growing fast, with rules still catching up.
- AI risks in finance: bias, errors, data leaks, provider concentration [1].

## Fraud, credit and trading

When your card gets blocked after a strange purchase, that's usually a model at work. When a lender approves you in minutes, a model often scored you. On trading desks, models help time and route orders [1].

## Risks and errors

I need you to know the downside:

1. **Bias.** Models can "inadvertently reinforce existing prejudices" [1].
2. **Black boxes.** Hard to explain why you got denied [1].
3. **Bad data.** Including "data poisoning" by attackers [1].
4. **Concentration.** Many firms rely on the same few AI models [1].
5. **AI-powered scams.** Better fake emails and voices [1].

## What regulators watch

This isn't a "trust me" thing. This is "verify me." Treasury collected comments from the industry before writing its report [1]. Existing rules on fair lending, privacy and fraud still apply when a bank uses AI. See [who actually holds the pen](/blog/who-controls-us-financial-system/).
