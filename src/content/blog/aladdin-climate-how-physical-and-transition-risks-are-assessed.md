All right, guys, let's look at the climate piece, because it's a big part of the BlackRock debate. Aladdin Climate is BlackRock's software for measuring climate risk at the level of each security. BlackRock unveiled it on December 1, 2020 [1]. Its COO said: "There is no single issue that clients ask us more about than the impact of climate risk on their portfolio" [1].

It measures two things: physical risk and transition risk [1]. Here's what each means and where climate models hit their limits.

**Quick answer**

- BlackRock Aladdin Climate: launched December 1, 2020 [1].
- Physical risk: damage from climate itself, like floods or heat [1].
- Transition risk: exposure to a shift to a low-carbon economy [1].
- Data partners named at launch: Sustainalytics and Refinitiv [1].

## What does it measure?

Aladdin climate risk adjusts security valuations and risk metrics for climate factors, and lets investors test how changes in policy, technology and energy supply could hit specific holdings [1]. It launched as an add-on for existing Aladdin clients, with coverage rolling out through 2021 [1]. See [everything sold under this brand](/blog/blackrock-aladdin-products/).

## What are physical risks?

The direct effects of climate change: storms, floods, heat, drought. A factory on a flood plain or a farm in a drought zone carries more of it.

## What are transition risks?

The costs of moving away from carbon: new rules, carbon prices, new technology. A coal company carries more of it. Physical and transition risk analytics put a number on both.

## What are the limits of climate models?

I need you to look at this with open eyes. Climate models stretch decades ahead and depend on policy guesses no one can know. The output is a scenario, not a fact. BlackRock itself framed the tool as helping clients "stress test investments to estimate how they might perform in different climate scenarios" [1].

This isn't a "trust me" thing. This is "verify me." Ask which scenario, which data and whose assumptions. See [why BlackRock bought eFront](/blog/blackrock-efront-aladdin/) and [the Aladdin tool for financial advisors](/blog/aladdin-wealth/).
