Patel Patriot, the pen name of Jon Herold, created the devolution theory. It started on July 2, 2021, when he published "Devolution — Part 1: How did we get here?" on Substack [1]. The Patel Patriot devolution origin reaches further back: the term comes from federal continuity-of-government planning. FEMA defined it in 2017 [5], and Newsweek brought it into public view in March 2020 [4].

**Quick answer**

- Who created devolution theory: Patel Patriot (Jon Herold) [1][3].
- When devolution theory started: July 2, 2021, with Part 1 on Substack [1].
- Where the term comes from: federal continuity planning [4][5].
- Size of the series: 25 numbered parts and 5 addenda [3].

## Who is Patel Patriot?

Patel Patriot is the author of the devolution series. His series site introduces it as "Jon Herold's (aka Patel Patriot) Devolution Series," and his 2022 overview carries the same name [2][3].

He presents himself as a researcher. In Part 1 he wrote: "I'm just a guy trying to figure out what the heck is going on" [1]. He built the theory from public material: Pentagon announcements, executive orders, presidential memoranda and court rulings [2].

He calls his work a theory in progress. The 2022 introduction says: "This is a theory based on countless hours of research, but it's still just a theory… for now" [2]. The same page describes the series as "constantly evolving" [2].

His footnotes show the kind of material he works with [2]:

- the court case Campbell v. Clinton on presidential war powers;
- Army Pamphlet 500-30 on continuity of operations;
- DoD Directive 3020.26 on defense continuity;
- Federal Register entries for executive orders and national security memoranda.

## What does the devolution theory say?

The core idea is that Trump used a real continuity-of-government mechanism before leaving office. In the author's own framing, the main points are these [1][2]:

- Trump acted as a "wartime president," treating COVID-19 as an attack from China.
- Between November 3, 2020 and January 20, 2021, a continuity-of-government plan was put into motion.
- Pentagon personnel changes in late 2020 were part of the preparation.
- The 2020 election result was not legitimate, and Trump's return was part of the plan.

## When did devolution theory start?

The starting point was January 20, 2021. Patel Patriot says Trump's farewell speech that day left him confused: "None of this made sense" [1]. He went back to a March 2020 Newsweek article that used the word "devolution." He then lined it up with military standby orders and election-law changes [1].

His 2021 Substack archive lists one earlier post, "My belief in Trump's comeback," before Part 1 [1]. Part 1 followed on July 2, 2021. By August 4, 2021 seven parts were out, and Praying Medic published a summary of all seven [6].

> **In short:** the theory took shape in the six months after January 20, 2021, and went public on July 2, 2021 [1].

## What documents does the devolution theory rely on?

The series builds on public government documents and on reporting about classified ones. The table lists the main ones and how the author uses each.

| Document | Date | What it is | How the series reads it |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Federal Continuity Directive 1 | Jan 17, 2017 | Defines devolution as moving an agency's authority to backup staff and facilities in a catastrophic emergency [5] | Proof that devolution is a real, standing plan [2] |
| Newsweek, William Arkin | Mar 18, 2020 | Reports plans codenamed Octagon, Freejack and Zodiac, and a "line of devolution" outside Washington [4] | The starting point of Part 1 [1] |
| Executive Order 13961 | Dec 7, 2020 | Creates a Federal Mission Resilience Executive Committee and signs a Federal Mission Resilience Strategy [7] | The order that set devolution in motion [2] |
| National Security Presidential Memoranda | Jan 2021 | A rise in the number of memoranda issued [2] | Steps that carried out the plan [2] |
| Presidential Emergency Action Documents | Classified | Emergency orders that are not published [2] | Where the "fine details" of the plan sit [2] |

EO 13961 speaks of "continuity of operations, continuity of government, and enduring constitutional government" [7]. The word "devolution" itself does not appear in the order. The link between the two comes from the author's reading of it [2][7]. On January 19, 2025, Executive Order 14146 revoked sections 1, 3, 4, 5 and 7 of EO 13961 [8].

Activation follows a set rule. FEMA's directive says a devolution plan is activated "upon the threat of, or in response to a catastrophic emergency" [5].

## How did the devolution theory spread?

The series grew through Substack and live audio. Its index links each part to an audio reading and to video commentary [3]. Patel Patriot's appearances drew a wide audience in the months after Part 1, at a time when Q's posts had stopped in December 2020 and readers were looking for a new explanation.

Channels the series used, according to its own index and friendly outlets:

- Substack posts, one part at a time [1].
- Audio readings of every part [3].
- "Devolution Power Hour" on Rumble, Wednesdays and Saturdays at 10:30 PM EST [3].
- Summaries on other blogs, including Praying Medic in August 2021 and Radio Patriot in October 2021 [6][9].
- Video and audio published under Badlands Media [3].

Dates and expectations inside the series shifted as events moved. Part 1 called Trump's return "imminent" in July 2021 [1]. You can follow how the promises changed from 2020 to 2024<!-- link: /blog/devolution-theory-timeline --> on our timeline page.

## Where is the Patel Patriot series today?

The series lives in two places. The Substack holds the full text of every part and addendum [1]. The index at devolution.us gathers 25 numbered parts, 5 addenda and a set of additional articles, each linked to its audio version [3].

The index lists copyright from 2021 to 2025 and credits Badlands Media [3]. The Introduction, first posted on April 6, 2022, remains the recommended entry point for new readers [2][3]. Anyone tracing the Patel Patriot devolution origin today can start there and work back to Part 1.

## How do you read devolution sources yourself?

Five habits help you separate the documents from the commentary:

1. **Start with the original.** Read Part 1 and the 2022 introduction before any summary [1][2].
2. **Open the document by its number.** EO 13961 takes two minutes to find on govinfo.gov [7].
3. **Mark what is in the text and what is added.** The order uses "continuity of government"; "devolution" comes from the reader [2][7].
4. **Check the date of every claim.** The author calls the series "constantly evolving," so an older part may not match a newer one [2].
5. **Ask questions live.** The series itself grew through live power hours where listeners asked questions in real time [3].
