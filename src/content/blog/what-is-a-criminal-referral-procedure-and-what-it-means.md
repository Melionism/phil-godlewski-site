All right, guys, "criminal referral" sounds like someone's about to be arrested. It's not. A criminal referral is a formal message to prosecutors saying, "we think this may be a crime; please look at it." Congress can send one. An agency can send one. An official like the Director of National Intelligence can send one, as Tulsi Gabbard said she did in July 2025 with documents about the 2016 Russia assessment [1]. In December 2022, the House January 6 committee sent referrals about Trump. Prosecutors don't have to act on any of them. A referral has no legal force by itself: only the Justice Department, through investigation and a grand jury, can bring federal charges.

Here's how it works, the possible charges, former presidents and statutes of limitations.

**Quick answer**

- What is a criminal referral: a formal request to prosecutors to consider charges.
- Criminal referral meaning: a tip with paperwork, not a charge.
- DOJ criminal referral process: review, investigation, grand jury, then a charging decision.
- Congress criminal referral: has no legal force; DOJ decides [1].

## How it works

| Step | Who | What happens |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Referral | Congress, agency, official | Sends facts and concerns |
| 2. Review | DOJ | Decides whether to open a case |
| 3. Investigation | FBI and prosecutors | Gather evidence |
| 4. Grand jury | Citizens | Decide whether to indict |
| 5. Charges | DOJ | Filed in court |

## FAQ

**What is a criminal referral?** A request to prosecutors to consider charges.

**Does a referral mean someone will be charged?** No.

**Who can make a referral?** Congress, agencies, officials.

**Who decides on charges?** The Justice Department and a grand jury.
