All right, guys, your home and your car are full of computers now, and every one of them collects something. Smart device privacy covers speakers, watches, TVs and cars, and the record isn't great. In 2023 the FTC and DOJ charged Amazon with keeping children's Alexa voice recordings indefinitely and failing to fully delete them when parents asked; Amazon agreed to a $25 million penalty under a proposed order [1]. Mozilla reviewed 25 car brands and every one failed its privacy check; 19 said they can sell personal data, and some listed categories like "sex life" in what they may collect [2]. And in January 2026 the FTC finalized an order barring GM from sharing drivers' location and driving data with consumer reporting agencies for five years [3].

Here are speakers, watches and TVs, cars and how to reduce collection.

**Quick answer**

- Smart home privacy: speakers and cameras can store recordings and footage.
- Smart TV privacy: many TVs track what you watch to target ads.
- Car data privacy: cars collect location and driving behavior; many brands share or sell it [2][3].
- Reduce collection: turn off what you don't use, review settings, delete recordings.

## Speakers

Smart speakers listen for a wake word, then record and send your request to the company's servers. Recordings can be stored. The Amazon case showed recordings and location data kept for years [1]. They're part of [what apps ask for](/blog/dangerous-app-permissions/).

| Device | Main data | Setting to check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Smart speaker | Voice recordings | Auto-delete recordings, mute button |
| Video doorbell / camera | Video, faces | Who can access footage, encryption |
| Smart TV | What you watch (ACR) | Turn off "viewing data" or ACR |
| Smartwatch | Heart rate, sleep, location | App sharing, cloud sync |
| Car | Location, speed, braking [3] | Connected services, data sharing |

## Watches and TVs

Watches gather health data, often outside medical privacy laws. Many smart TVs use automatic content recognition (ACR) to identify what's on screen and use it for ads. Look for settings called "viewing information," "smart interactivity" or "ACR."

## Cars

Modern cars send data back to the maker: location, speed, hard braking, sometimes phone contacts synced over Bluetooth. Mozilla found 84% of brands said they can share data and only two said all drivers can have their data deleted [2]. The FTC alleged GM sold driving data without consent; its final order requires consent and gives drivers a way to get and delete their data [3].

## How to reduce collection

This isn't a "trust me" thing. This is "verify me."

1. **Speakers:** turn on auto-delete; use the mute button for private conversations.
2. **TVs:** turn off ACR / viewing data in settings.
3. **Cameras:** use strong passwords and two-factor login; check who has access.
4. **Cars:** review the connected services app; opt out of data sharing; don't sync contacts to rental cars.
5. **Everything:** update firmware.

Location is the common thread: see [what your movements reveal about your life](/blog/location-data-privacy/) and [whether your phone is really listening](/blog/are-voice-assistants-recording/).

## A closer look: rental and used cars

When you pair your phone with a rental car, your contacts and call logs can stay in its system for the next driver. Before you return it, delete your phone from the car's Bluetooth list. When selling your own car, do a factory reset of the infotainment system and remove it from the maker's app.

## FAQ

**Is smart home privacy a real concern?** Yes; recordings and footage can be stored [1].

**Does my smart TV track me?** Many do through ACR; you can usually turn it off.

**What does my car know about me?** Location and driving behavior, often more [2][3].

**Can I delete car data?** Some makers allow it; GM must now offer it [3].

**What's the first step?** Check settings on each device.

2. Turn on auto-delete for your speaker.
