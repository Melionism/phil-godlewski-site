All right, guys, if you want to understand EO 13848, read it yourself. It's short, about six pages in the Federal Register. The official text was published on September 14, 2018, at 83 FR 46843 [1]. You can also read it in the American Presidency Project archive, in GovInfo's Daily Compilation of Presidential Documents, and as Appendix A to Treasury's regulations at 31 CFR Part 579 [2]. It has a preamble and 14 sections. The preamble declares a national emergency under IEEPA and the National Emergencies Act. Sections 1 through 3 create the assessment-and-sanctions machine. Sections 4 through 7 set the prohibitions. Section 8 defines the terms. The rest is housekeeping: no prior notice, official business, implementation, reports to Congress and general provisions [1].

Here's what each section does, the "no evidence" line, the AG and DHS report, Treasury's rule, EO 13694, what an executive order is, how to read one, and the definitions, First Amendment clause and reform ideas.

## What each section does: assessments and sanctions

| Section | Plain English |
| --- | --- |
| Preamble | Declares a national emergency over foreign election interference |
| 1(a) | DNI assesses foreign interference within 45 days after each federal election |
| 1(b) | AG and DHS report within 45 days whether it materially affected election or campaign infrastructure |
| 1(f) | Agencies set up a process that keeps intelligence separate from policy and avoids political bias |
| 2 | Treasury blocks property of foreign persons who interfered, supported it, or are owned by those who did |
| 3 | State and Treasury may recommend broader sanctions, including on a country's largest companies |
| 4–5 | No donations to, and no funds, goods or services to or from, blocked persons |
| 6 | Blocked persons can't enter the U.S. |
| 7 | No evasion or conspiracy |
| 8 | Definitions |
| 9 | No prior notice of a listing |
| 10–14 | Official business, implementation, reports, IEEPA limits, general provisions |

## The 'no evidence' line

The preamble says there has been "no evidence of a foreign power altering the outcome or vote tabulation in any United States election" [1]. Renewal notices from 2019 through 2025 repeated it; the August 2026 notice doesn't [3][4]. More: [no evidence altered results eo 13848](/blog/no-evidence-altered-results-eo-13848/).

## AG and DHS report

Section 1(b) is the second review. The AG and DHS check whether foreign activity hit election infrastructure or campaign infrastructure, list any unresolved factual disputes, and can recommend remedial steps other than sanctions [1]. Details: [eo 13848 attorney general dhs report](/blog/eo-13848-attorney-general-dhs-report/).

## Treasury's rule

Section 11 lets Treasury write rules. It did that in April 2019 with the Foreign Interference in U.S. Elections Sanctions Regulations [2]. Read: [31 cfr part 579](/blog/31-cfr-part-579/).

## EO 13694

Section 2(b) says the older cyber sanctions order, EO 13694 as amended, stays in effect and can be used alongside this one [1]. Comparison: [eo 13694 vs eo 13848](/blog/eo-13694-vs-eo-13848/).

## Definitions, First Amendment clause and reform ideas

**Key definitions (Sec. 8)** [1]:

- **Foreign interference**: "covert, fraudulent, deceptive, or unlawful actions" by a foreign government or its agents to influence or alter an election or undermine public confidence in it.
- **Election infrastructure**: technology used to run elections, including voter registration databases, voting machines and tabulation equipment.
- **United States person**: citizens, permanent residents, U.S. entities and anyone in the U.S.

**Speech.** The order never mentions the First Amendment. The closest language is Section 1(f), which says the process must respect "the principles of free speech and open debate" [1]. IEEPA itself bars regulating personal communications and informational materials [6].

**Reform ideas.** Lawmakers have proposed automatic, mandatory sanctions instead of discretion, as in the DETER Act. Others want more of the reports made public.
