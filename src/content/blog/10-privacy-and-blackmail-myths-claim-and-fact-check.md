All right, guys, privacy is full of myths, and some of them cost people money. Here are ten privacy and blackmail myths I see all the time, with a verdict on each. The numbers behind this are big. The FBI's Internet Crime Complaint Center logged 89,129 extortion complaints in 2025, and phishing was the most common complaint type of all, with 191,561 reports [1]. Myths make people easier targets. Facts make them harder ones.

Here are myths about technology, myths about secrets, myths about blackmail and how to check the next claim.

**Quick answer**

- Privacy myths: incognito hides you, VPNs make you anonymous, "nothing to hide."
- Blackmail myths: paying ends it, the hacker really has your camera footage.
- Online privacy myths debunked: most threats are ordinary scams, not super-hackers.

## Myths about technology

| # | Claim | Verdict | Why |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | "Incognito mode makes me invisible" | False | It mainly stops history saving on your device |
| 2 | "A VPN makes me anonymous" | Misleading | It hides traffic from your provider, not from sites you log into |
| 3 | "My phone listens to everything I say for ads" | Unproven | Location, browsing and purchase data explain most "creepy" ads |
| 4 | "Quantum computers will expose everything soon" | Exaggerated | NIST already published post-quantum standards in 2024 [2] |

These ideas get tangled with the basic terms; see [the words behind the debate](/blog/privacy-vs-secrecy-vs-anonymity/).

## Myths about secrets

| # | Claim | Verdict | Why |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 5 | "If I delete it, it's gone" | Often false | Backups, screenshots and the other person's copy remain |
| 6 | "Only celebrities get targeted" | False | Most scams hit ordinary people at scale [1] |
| 7 | "My data is already everywhere, so why bother" | False | You can still cut new collection and remove some old data |

## Myths about blackmail

| # | Claim | Verdict | Why |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 8 | "If I pay, it will stop" | Usually false | Payment often leads to more demands |
| 9 | "The email with my old password means they hacked my camera" | Usually false | Most use passwords from old breaches in mass mailings |

I get it, I get it. When someone threatens you, panic takes over. That's exactly what they count on. The first moves are the same almost every time: don't pay, save the evidence, stop replying, report it.

## How to check the next claim

This isn't a "trust me" thing. This is "verify me."

1. **Who benefits** if you believe it? Often someone selling a product.
2. **Is there a source?** A real study, regulator or court record.
3. **Is it about you?** A threat to governments isn't always a threat to your Gmail.
4. **What's the cheap fix?** Most real risks have free fixes: passwords, two-factor login, settings.

And when someone tells you privacy doesn't matter, here's [a line you've probably heard](/blog/i-have-nothing-hide-argument/).

## FAQ

**What are the most common privacy myths?** That incognito and VPNs make you invisible, and that it's too late to bother.

**Does paying a blackmailer make it stop?** Usually not.

**Are "I hacked your camera" emails real?** Usually mass scams using old leaked passwords.

**How common is extortion?** 89,129 complaints to the FBI's IC3 in 2025 [1].

**Will quantum computers expose everything?** Exaggerated; new standards exist [2].
