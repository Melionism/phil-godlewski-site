All right, guys, while Congress argued about the SAVE America Act, states kept acting on their own, which is how the Constitution sets it up. In 2025, Indiana, New Hampshire, Ohio and Wyoming passed proof-of-citizenship laws for voter registration [1]. Arizona has required documentary proof for state and local elections since a 2004 law; people who register without it can vote only in federal races, which created a "federal-only" voter list [2]. Kansas required it for everyone from 2013 until a federal judge struck it down in 2018, a ruling the 10th Circuit upheld in 2020 [1][3]. New Hampshire's 2024 law, which removed the sworn affidavit option, was ruled unconstitutional on May 28, 2026, and the state is appealing to the First Circuit [4][5]. Separately, 36 states have voter ID laws for casting a ballot [6]. The details differ a lot from state to state, especially on what happens to a citizen who can't find documents.

Here's the map, voter ID rules, Arizona, Kansas and New Hampshire.

**Quick answer**

- Proof of citizenship laws by state: Arizona (state races), plus new 2025 laws in Indiana, New Hampshire, Ohio and Wyoming [1][2].
- Kansas: struck down in 2018 [3].
- New Hampshire: affidavit option restored by court in May 2026; appeal pending [4][5].
- Voter ID: 36 states [6].

## Voter ID rules

Thirty-six states have ID laws; in some, voters without ID can sign an affidavit, and in others they cast a provisional ballot and show ID later [6]. More: [voter id laws by state](/blog/voter-id-laws-by-state/).

## Arizona

The 2024 driver's license database error showed the system's risk: about 218,000 longtime voters had been wrongly coded as having proof on file; the state Supreme Court let them vote full ballots [2]. More: [arizona two tier voter registration](/blog/arizona-two-tier-voter-registration/).

## Kansas

More than 30,000 people were blocked or suspended before the courts ended the law [1]. More: [kansas proof of citizenship law](/blog/kansas-proof-citizenship-law/).

## New Hampshire

This isn't a "trust me" thing. This is "verify me."

Judge Samantha Elliott found the affidavit was "the only method of proof available to a significant number of New Hampshire voters" [4]. The state says the law is a "common-sense approach" [4]. More: [new hampshire proof of citizenship law](/blog/new-hampshire-proof-citizenship-law/). And how registering would change in person and online: [how registering would change in person and online](/blog/documents-prove-citizenship-vote/).
