All right, guys, 2020 was the year a lot of people first heard of Q, and the timing wasn't an accident. Lockdowns put people at home, online, anxious and looking for answers. The Institute for Strategic Dialogue tracked it: in March 2020, individual users posting QAnon content on Facebook rose 161%, on Twitter 139% and on Instagram 77% [1]. Membership in QAnon Facebook groups rose 120% that month [1]. About 8% of QAnon conversation on Twitter that year mentioned COVID-19 [1]. Q's first COVID post appeared on March 23, 2020 [2]. A Wall Street Journal study, cited by researcher Lorna Bracewell, found membership in 10 of the largest public QAnon Facebook groups "grew by nearly 600% from March to July" [3]. Many newer followers came through lifestyle, beauty and parenting influencers on Instagram [3]. Then summer brought #SaveTheChildren, which hit more than 800,000 Twitter uses in the first week of August, and the charity Save the Children said it was not associated with it [4]. Platforms responded: Twitter suspended 7,000 accounts in July 2020 [2].

Here's lockdown, platforms, the Save the Children overlap, and data.

**Quick answer**

- QAnon pandemic growth: March 2020 jumps of 161% (Facebook) and 139% (Twitter) in users [1].
- Facebook groups: up nearly 600% March–July, per WSJ [3].
- New audience: lifestyle and parenting influencers [3].
- #SaveTheChildren: 800,000+ uses in one week [4].
