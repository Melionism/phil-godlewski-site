All right, guys, Mike Lindell has been one of the loudest voices on 2020, and a lot of us watched his events. So let's look at his promises and what happened to them. In early 2021 he released a film called "Absolute Proof." In August 2021 he held a "Cyber Symposium" in Sioux Falls, South Dakota, and offered $5 million in a "Prove Mike Wrong Challenge" to anyone who could show his data wasn't valid November 2020 election data [1]. Software developer Robert Zeidman, a Trump voter, reviewed the files and concluded they did not contain 2020 election data. An arbitration panel agreed and ordered Lindell to pay $5 million [1]. In July 2025, the Eighth Circuit reversed that award, but on contract grounds: it said the panel added a data-format requirement the contract didn't contain [2]. The court didn't find the data proved fraud.

Here's Absolute Proof, the symposium, other promises and the outcomes.

**Quick answer**

- Mike Lindell election evidence promises: "Absolute Proof" (2021) and the Cyber Symposium data [1].
- Mike Lindell cyber symposium: August 2021, Sioux Falls, with a $5 million challenge [1].
- Lindell absolute proof: the promised data was reviewed and found not to be 2020 election data, per arbitrators [1].
- Election big reveal promises: repeated since 2020; none produced outcome-changing proof in court.

## Absolute Proof

The film claimed foreign hacking flipped votes using packet captures. The data was never accepted by a court or independent expert as proof. Its approach is a lot like the "hidden evidence" logic behind [the source of the 25 percent figure](/blog/only-25-percent-election-evidence-revealed/).
