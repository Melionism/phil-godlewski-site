All right, guys, the short answer is: not by himself. The Constitution gives states the power to set the "Times, Places and Manner" of congressional elections, and gives Congress the power to change those rules by law [1]. A president can sign or veto those laws and can direct federal agencies, but an executive order can't rewrite state election codes. Trump tested that twice. His March 25, 2025 order, EO 14248, tried to require proof of citizenship on the federal registration form and to bar counting mail ballots that arrive after Election Day; Judge Denise Casper blocked key parts on June 13, 2025, finding they could leave citizens "disenfranchised" [2]. His March 31, 2026 order, EO 14399, directed federal agencies to build citizen lists and the Postal Service to limit mail ballots; Judge Indira Talwani found the President lacked authority to direct the lists and the Postal Service lacked power to issue binding mail voting rules [3]. The Supreme Court then paused one injunction, saying the challenge came too early, while stressing it wasn't ruling on whether the order is lawful [4].

Here's the 2025 order, the 2026 order, what the Constitution says, and why Congress matters.

**Quick answer**

- Can president change election rules: not on his own; states and Congress set them [1].
- 2025 order: key parts blocked [2].
- 2026 order: key parts blocked; the Supreme Court said one challenge was premature [3][4].
- Real path: a law passed by Congress, like the SAVE America Act [5].

## What the Constitution says

1. **Article I, Section 4**: states set rules; Congress may change them [1].
2. **Article II**: Congress sets the day electors are chosen [1].
3. **No clause** gives the President election rulemaking power [1].

## Why Congress matters

This isn't a "trust me" thing. This is "verify me."

A law passed by Congress binds states in federal elections. That's why the SAVE America Act, not an order, was the main vehicle; it passed the House 218-213 in February 2026 and stalled in the Senate [5][6]. The February 2026 House vote and how the bill got there: [the February 2026 House vote and how the bill got there](/blog/save-america-act-requirements/).

**Myths**

- "An executive order is a law." It directs the executive branch.
- "The Supreme Court approved the 2026 order." It said the challenge was premature [4].
- "An emergency lets the President set election rules." No law says so.
