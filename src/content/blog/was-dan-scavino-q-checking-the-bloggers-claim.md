All right, guys, one idea you'll hear in our corner of the internet, including in streams like mine, is that Dan Scavino is Q, or at least the link between Q and Trump. Let's check it the same way we'd check anything else. Scavino has worked with Trump since the early 2000s, starting by running one of Trump's golf courses, then serving as social media director for the campaign and the first term [1]. As of July 2026 he's assistant to the president, deputy chief of staff and director of the Office of Presidential Personnel [2]. The evidence offered for "Scavino is Q" is mostly his posts: clock images, "tick tock," a violin image captioned "Nothing can stop what's coming," and two eye emojis after the White House "Loading…" clip in September 2026 [2][3]. Q believers read those as nods. But the outside evidence on authorship points elsewhere: two 2022 linguistic studies matched Q's writing to Paul Furber early and Ron Watkins from 2018, and both men deny it [4]. No study has matched Q to Scavino, and no document shows he wrote a drop. Posts that echo Q language show he knows the audience; they don't show he's the author.

Here's the claim, the evidence offered, what's known, and the conclusion.

**Quick answer**

- Was Dan Scavino Q: no evidence shows he wrote the drops.
- Evidence offered: posts with clocks, "tick tock," eye emojis [2][3].
- Linguistic studies: point to Furber and Watkins, not Scavino [4].
- What it shows: awareness of the audience, not authorship.
