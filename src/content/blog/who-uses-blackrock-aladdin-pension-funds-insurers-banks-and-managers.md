All right, guys, who's actually on this thing? Who uses BlackRock Aladdin? BlackRock itself, plus "a growing number of institutional and retail investors around the world," per its 10-K [1]. In 2017, BlackRock said more than 85 asset-manager clients ran about $18 trillion on the platform [2]. Central banks are on it too: the Bank of Israel has used Aladdin since 2019 [3].

Here are the main client types, how firms implement it, why some leave and why clients buy instead of build.

**Quick answer**

- Aladdin clients: asset managers, insurers, pension funds, banks, central banks [1][2][3].
- Scale in 2017: 85+ asset-manager clients, about $18 trillion [2].
- Example: Bank of Israel, $208 billion in FX reserves, since 2019 [3].
- Aladdin users also include wealth firms via Aladdin Wealth [4].

It's a big project: moving data, mapping rules, training staff. The Bank of Israel's setup needed "some system customisation" for its hybrid benchmark [3]. Read [aladdin implementation](/blog/aladdin-implementation/).

## Why do some firms leave?

BlackRock's own 10-K names the risk: clients may move to standalone or in-house systems over "price, perceived market share, platform offerings, or flexibility" [1]. Read [why firms leave aladdin](/blog/why-firms-leave-aladdin/).

## Why do clients buy instead of build?

Building a risk system from scratch takes years and big teams. Buying one gets a firm running faster. I need you to see the catch, too: some of Aladdin's clients compete with BlackRock in managing money. That's the [aladdin conflict of interest](/blog/aladdin-conflict-interest/) question.

This isn't a "trust me" thing. This is "verify me." Client names show up in press releases and awards; numbers show up in BlackRock's filings.
