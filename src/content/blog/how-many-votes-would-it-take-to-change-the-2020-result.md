All right, guys, let's do the math, because the math is where every fraud claim either holds up or falls apart. Nationally, Joe Biden got about 81.3 million votes and Donald Trump about 74.2 million, a gap of roughly 7 million [1]. But presidents aren't picked by the national vote. They're picked by the Electoral College, and Biden won 306 to 232 [2]. To flip the result, you'd need to move 37 electoral votes, which means overturning Biden's wins in several states at once. In the three closest states, Arizona, Georgia and Wisconsin, his combined margin was about 43,000 votes [1]. Flipping all three would produce a 269–269 tie, not a Trump win. To win outright, you'd need at least one more state, like Pennsylvania, where the margin was over 80,000 [1].

Here are the state margins, the math, what that scale requires and what it means.

**Quick answer**

- How many votes to change 2020 election: about 43,000 across AZ, GA and WI for a tie; more for a win [1].
- 2020 election margin by state: Arizona ~10,500; Georgia ~11,800; Wisconsin ~20,700; Pennsylvania ~80,600 [1].
- Votes needed to flip 2020: real, documented illegal votes in that range, all going one way, in several states.
- What it means: individual fraud cases exist, but nothing on record comes close.

## The math

1. Biden had 306; 270 wins, so he had 36 to spare [2].
2. Flip AZ + GA + WI (37 electoral votes): 269–269 tie.
3. A tie goes to the House, voting by state delegation.
4. Flip AZ + GA + WI + PA: Trump wins.

## What scale requires

This isn't a "trust me" thing. This is "verify me." Moving 43,000 votes isn't about one bad clerk. It would need thousands of fraudulent ballots per state, all for one candidate, getting past signature checks, poll books, canvasses, recounts and in Georgia a full hand count of every presidential ballot [3]. Each extra state multiplies the number of people who'd have to be in on it and stay quiet.

## What it means

I get it, I get it. Tens of thousands sounds small next to 158 million votes. But that's documented fraud, not suspicion. Real cases, like those the DOJ charges each year, are counted in the dozens or hundreds, spread over years and states. That's the gap any claim has to close.
