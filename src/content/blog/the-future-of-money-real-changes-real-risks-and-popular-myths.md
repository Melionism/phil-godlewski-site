## How does today's system work?

Four parts do most of the work. The Federal Reserve is the central bank of the United States [5]. Banks hold your deposits and make loans. Markets trade stocks and bonds. Payment systems move money between all of them. Congress writes the rules, and the Fed and Treasury carry them out. Read [how the financial system works](/blog/financial-system-works/).

| Part | What it does | Who sets the rules |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Central bank (the Fed) | Sets rates, runs key payment systems | Congress [5] |
| Banks | Hold deposits, lend money | Congress and regulators |
| Markets | Trade stocks, bonds, funds | Congress and regulators |
| Payments | Move money in seconds to days | The Fed and private networks [1] |

## What is the "great reset" idea?

In June 2020 the World Economic Forum launched an initiative it called "The Great Reset," about rebuilding economies after COVID. The phrase took on a life of its own online. Now it gets used for everything from a new world currency to wiped bank accounts. I get it, I get it. When powerful people use a phrase like that, people pay attention. But a conference initiative isn't a law. It can't change your bank account. Read [great financial reset](/blog/great-financial-reset/).

## What is claimed about 2027?

You'll see posts that say a new financial system switches on in 2027. Here's what's real about that year. The GENIUS Act, the stablecoin law signed July 18, 2025 [2], takes effect on the earlier of 18 months after signing or 120 days after regulators issue final rules [6]. Eighteen months from July 18, 2025 is January 18, 2027. That's a real date, for one specific law about stablecoins. It isn't a reset of the whole system. Read [2027 financial reset](/blog/2027-financial-reset/).
