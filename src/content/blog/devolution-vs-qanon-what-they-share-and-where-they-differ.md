All right, guys, people ask me this all the time: is devolution QAnon? Short answer: devolution grew in the same community, but it's a different thing. Q posted short anonymous drops from 2017 to 2020 [1]. Devolution is a signed, footnoted Substack series that started on July 2, 2021 [2][3].

Some people call devolution "Q without Q." Here's the difference between devolution and QAnon, laid out side by side.

**Quick answer**

- Shared: Trump at the center, the 2020 election, a hidden plan, the military [3][4].
- Different: an anonymous poster vs a named author, drops vs long research parts [1][3].
- Timing: Q went quiet on December 8, 2020; devolution started 206 days later [1][2].
- Word check: Q's archive has no post with the word "devolution" [5].

## What do the two theories share?

Both put Trump at the center of a plan that runs out of public view. Q told readers to "TRUST THE PLAN" on March 6, 2018 [6]. Devolution says that a continuity-of-government plan went into motion between November 3, 2020 and January 20, 2021 [3].

Both give the military a central part. Part 9 of devolution says the Combatant Commanders "play an integral role" [4]. Both speak of good actors on the inside, the white hats, and Q used that phrase in post 3312 on April 11, 2019 [7].

Both also build on real government terms. Devolution itself is official: FEMA's 2017 directive defines it [8]. See [the real meaning of government devolution](/blog/devolution-theory/) on our main page.

The biggest one is the author. Patel Patriot puts his name on the series, and his 2022 introduction says it is "still just a theory… for now" [3]. That's a researcher talking, and you can hold him to his words.

## How did followers move between them?

Do you remember December 8, 2020? That's the date of Q post 4953, the last one before a long silence [1]. Q did not post again until June 24, 2022, with post 4954: "Shall we play a game once more?" [10].

That's a gap of more than 18 months. Devolution Part 1 appeared on July 2, 2021, right in the middle of it [2]. By August 4, 2021, seven parts were out and Praying Medic, a well-known name in the Q community, had summarized them [12].

## How do you read a Q post vs a devolution part?

Different tools for different texts:

1. **A Q post:** note its number and date, then read it next to the posts around it on qalerts.app [1].
2. **A devolution part:** read the footnotes first, then open each document it names [3].
3. **Both:** write down every date they give and come back to it later.

## Why does the distinction matter?

Because you check them differently. A Q post is a riddle you decode. A devolution part names a document you can open, like EO 13961 on govinfo.gov [14]. That makes devolution easier to verify, line by line.
