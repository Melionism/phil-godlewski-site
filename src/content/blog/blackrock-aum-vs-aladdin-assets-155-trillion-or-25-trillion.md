All right, guys, this one trips up almost everyone. BlackRock AUM vs Aladdin assets: two numbers, two meanings. BlackRock managed $15.5 trillion at September 30, 2026 [1]. Aladdin, BlackRock says, "is used to manage $25 trillion in assets globally" [2]. The first is money BlackRock runs for clients. The second is money that many firms, BlackRock included, run through its software.

So how much money does Aladdin manage? None by itself. People and firms manage money; Aladdin is the system they use. Here's what each number counts, why they get confused and how to cite them.

**Quick answer**

- $15.5 trillion: BlackRock's assets under management, September 30, 2026 [1].
- $25 trillion: assets managed on the Aladdin platform, per BlackRock [2].
- Older Aladdin figures: about $18 trillion in 2017 and $21.6 trillion in 2020 [3][4].
- "Aladdin 15 trillion" and "Aladdin 25 trillion" often mix the two counts.

## What are the two different numbers?

| Number | What it counts | Who controls the money | Source |

| $15.5 trillion | BlackRock's AUM | BlackRock, for its clients | [1] |
| $25 trillion | Assets on the Aladdin platform | Each client firm | [2] |

See [blackrock aum](/blog/blackrock-assets-under-management/).

## What does each one count?

AUM is money BlackRock invests under its own funds and mandates [1]. Platform assets add up everything clients process through Aladdin: their own funds, run by their own people, on BlackRock's software [2].

## Why do they get confused?

1. **Both are trillions** and both have "BlackRock" in the headline.
2. **Old numbers stay online** without dates [3][4].
3. **"Manage" is used loosely.** "Aladdin manages $25 trillion" sounds like control.

I need you to see the trap in the last one. A spreadsheet doesn't own the money in it.

## How do you cite them?

This isn't a "trust me" thing. This is "verify me." Use this form:

1. "BlackRock managed $15.5 trillion as of September 30, 2026 [1]."
2. "BlackRock says $25 trillion in assets are managed on Aladdin [2]."
3. Never add them together; they overlap.
