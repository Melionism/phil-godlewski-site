All right, guys, under current federal law you swear you're a citizen when you register. Several states, and the SAVE America Act, would require documents instead. So which papers count? The main ones are a U.S. passport, a U.S. birth certificate, and a naturalization certificate or certificate of citizenship [1][2]. Under the House-passed SAVE America Act, a passport alone would do; a birth record would need to be paired with a government photo ID or military ID [3]. A REAL ID counts only if it "indicates the applicant is a citizen" [2]. Here's the catch: most states' REAL IDs don't show citizenship at all [4]. A few states issue enhanced driver's licenses that do; in Michigan, about 2 million of 8.3 million license and ID holders have one [4]. And people whose current names don't match their birth certificates, often married women, may need extra paperwork [1].

Here are the accepted documents, REAL ID, name and address changes, who lacks them, and how registration would change.

**Quick answer**

- Documents to prove citizenship to vote: passport, birth certificate, naturalization or citizenship certificate [1][2].
- SAVE America Act: passport, or birth record plus photo ID [3].
- REAL ID: only if it shows citizenship; most don't [2][4].
- Enhanced licenses: issued in a few states and show citizenship [4].

## Accepted documents

| Document | Proves citizenship? |
| --- | --- |
| U.S. passport or passport card | Yes [1] |
| U.S. birth certificate | Yes, with photo ID under the bill [3] |
| Naturalization certificate | Yes [1] |
| Certificate of citizenship | Yes [1] |
| Standard driver's license | No |
| Enhanced driver's license | Yes, in states that issue it [4] |

## REAL ID

REAL ID shows you proved legal presence, which includes noncitizens with legal status. That's why it isn't proof of citizenship unless marked [2][4]. Name mismatches add another layer: [name change proof of citizenship voter](/blog/name-change-proof-citizenship-voter/).

## Name and address changes

If your name differs from your birth certificate, you'd likely need a marriage certificate, divorce decree or court order linking the names. The bill requires states to create a process for other evidence with an attestation [3]. What surveys find on missing documents: [what surveys find on missing documents](/blog/americans-without-citizenship-documents/).

## Who lacks them

A 2023 University of Maryland survey estimated 21.3 million adult citizens lack easy access to these documents [1]. A 2024 MIT survey of voters found 97% had a driver's license and 85% said it was unexpired and matched their registration [4].

## How registration would change

This isn't a "trust me" thing. This is "verify me."

- **In person**: bring documents to the election office.
- **Mail and online**: effectively ended for new registrants under the bill, per election officials [5].
- **States already doing it**: see [proof of citizenship laws by state](/blog/proof-citizenship-laws-by-state/).

## FAQ

**What documents prove citizenship to vote?** Passport, birth certificate, naturalization or citizenship certificate [1].

**Does REAL ID count?** Only if it shows citizenship [2].

**Do any licenses show citizenship?** Enhanced licenses in a few states [4].

**What about married names?** You may need a marriage certificate or court order linking the names.

**Is this required now?** Not federally; some states have their own rules.
