All right, guys, if Biden renewing EO 13848 means something special, then we have to explain why he renewed a bunch of other Trump emergencies too. He did. In May 2021, for example, Biden continued the national emergency from Trump's EO 13873, which targets foreign threats to the U.S. information and communications technology supply chain [1]. Presidents of both parties do this all the time. Many emergencies outlast the president who declared them; the oldest still active dates to November 14, 1979, when Jimmy Carter froze Iranian assets during the hostage crisis [2]. Biden also showed he could end Trump emergencies he disagreed with: he terminated the southern border emergency on his first day and the International Criminal Court sanctions emergency in April 2021 [3][4]. So renewal is a choice, made one emergency at a time.

Here are examples, why it's routine, the exceptions, and what it means.

**Quick answer**

- Other Trump orders Biden extended: e.g., EO 13873 on the ICT supply chain [1].
- Routine: emergencies often run for decades; the oldest is from 1979 [2].
- Exceptions: Biden ended the border wall and ICC emergencies [3][4].
- Meaning: renewal reflects policy, not a hidden finding.

## Why routine

1. **Sanctions programs need the emergency** to stay legal.
2. **Ending one** can release blocked assets and signal a policy change.
3. **Agencies recommend** continuing them.

## Exceptions

Presidents end emergencies when they change policy: Biden on the border wall and the ICC [3][4]. In 2019, Congress voted to end the border emergency, and Trump vetoed it [5].

## What it means

This isn't a "trust me" thing. This is "verify me."

Renewing an emergency says "we still want this tool." It doesn't say what happened in a particular case. A fair point to start with: [a fair point to start with](/blog/biden-required-renew-eo-13848/).
