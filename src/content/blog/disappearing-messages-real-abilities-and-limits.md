All right, guys, do disappearing messages really disappear? From your phone and the other person's phone, after the timer runs out, yes, usually. From the world, not necessarily. The other person can screenshot, take a photo of the screen with another phone, or copy the text before it vanishes. Backups may hold copies, depending on settings: Apple, for example, says Messages in iCloud is only end-to-end encrypted if iCloud Backup is off or Advanced Data Protection is on [1]. Disappearing messages are a good habit. They're not a guarantee.

Here's how they work, where they fail, when they're useful and what not to rely on.

**Quick answer**

- Do disappearing messages really disappear: from the devices, after the timer; copies can survive.
- Signal disappearing messages: a timer deletes messages on all devices in the chat.
- Disappearing messages limits: screenshots, photos of the screen, backups, notifications.
- Use them: to reduce what's stored, not to send something you'd regret.

## How do they work?

You set a timer, from seconds to weeks. After it runs out, the app deletes the message on every device in the chat. In end-to-end encrypted apps, the company never had a readable copy anyway. That's how [encrypted chats keep others out](/blog/end-end-encryption-explained/).

| App | Disappearing messages | Default |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Signal | Yes, per chat or default timer | Off unless you set it |
| WhatsApp | Yes, per chat or default timer | Off unless you set it |
| iMessage | Audio and some features | Varies |
| Telegram | In secret chats and some regular chats | Off |

## Where do they fail?

1. **Screenshots.** Some apps notify you, many don't.
2. **A second camera.** No app can stop a photo of the screen.
3. **Copy and paste** before the timer ends.
4. **Backups** made before deletion [1].
5. **Notifications** that show message text on lock screens or watches.
6. **Linked devices** that were offline when the timer ran.

## When are they useful?

1. **Everyday chats** you don't need to keep.
2. **Reducing damage** if your phone is lost or seized.
3. **Group chats** that grow huge over time.
4. **Sharing a code or address** you don't want lingering.

## What not to rely on

This isn't a "trust me" thing. This is "verify me." I need you to remember one rule: if you wouldn't be OK with the other person keeping it, don't send it, timer or not. Also check your backups: [the copy you forgot about](/blog/cloud-backups-chat-privacy/).
