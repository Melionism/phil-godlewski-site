All right, guys, "Five Eyes" comes up in every surveillance conversation, so let's separate what's documented from what's speculation. What is Five Eyes? It's an intelligence-sharing alliance between the United States, the United Kingdom, Canada, Australia and New Zealand. Its roots are public: the NSA has released the original BRUSA agreement, later called UKUSA, signed March 5, 1946 [1]. The NSA's own page describes the UK and US as the main partners and Australia, Canada and New Zealand as "Second Parties" [1].

Here are the members, how it works, what's documented and what's speculation.

**Quick answer**

- What is Five Eyes: an intelligence alliance of five English-speaking countries [1].
- Five Eyes countries: US, UK, Canada, Australia, New Zealand [1].
- Five Eyes intelligence: mainly signals intelligence (communications).
- Documented start: the 1946 BRUSA/UKUSA agreement [1].

## The members

| Country | Main signals agency |
| --- | --- |
| United States | National Security Agency (NSA) |
| United Kingdom | Government Communications Headquarters (GCHQ) |
| Canada | Communications Security Establishment (CSE) |
| Australia | Australian Signals Directorate (ASD) |
| New Zealand | Government Communications Security Bureau (GCSB) |

It's one layer of [how officials actually ask](/blog/government-data-requests/) for and get information.

## How does it work?

The agreement grew out of World War II code-breaking cooperation. Partners divide up collection around the world and share what they gather. The NSA's released documents include appendices on how arrangements with the "Second Parties" work [1].

## What's documented?

1. **The 1946 agreement and its appendices** [1].
2. **Shared signals intelligence** over decades.
3. **Snowden-era documents** describing joint programs.
4. **Public statements** by member governments on cooperation, including on encryption policy.

## What's speculation?

## A closer look: why it matters for privacy

If your data sits with a company in one Five Eyes country, it may be reachable by that country's legal process. That's a reason to prefer end-to-end encryption, where the company can't read your content no matter who asks.
